# Positions: Russell Brunson

His stances on common founder questions, from 56 podcast transcripts (2017 to 2026). Dates are the episode date (the talk may be older; noted where known). Where he changed his mind, both views appear with dates. "No recorded position" means the corpus has nothing; do not guess.

---

### Pricing
- Being the most expensive has value; being the cheapest is a trap unless you are Walmart. "there's a huge strategic advantage of being the most expensive person in town, right?" (S002, 2019)
- Price toward the dream customer. "you want to attract premium people you gotta charge premium prices" (S056, 2024-12)
- But he also keeps a middle tier cheap to fill it with buyers who then ascend. "I like to build up pressure in an offer." (S051, 2025-06). To a fitness coach pricing $597, he said worry less about raising it and more about getting a thousand buyers; $297 might be better (S051).
- These two do not fully agree: premium pricing for the core offer, low pricing on the rung meant to feed the next one.

### Discounting
- Prefers bundling to discounting. "But a lot of you guys don't want to discount prices, which I understand as well." (S028, 2017). Raise value instead (S002).
- Uses price raises as events: announce a doubling, grandfather current members, deadline (S056, 2024-12).

### Raising money
- Strongly against. The show tagline: "How are entrepreneurs like us, who didn't cheat and take on venture capital, who are spending money from our own pockets" (S002, 2019)
- After Rand Fishkin warned him privately in 2015, he told partners "let's not take on money." (S038, 2025 talk). He concedes funded rivals gain some advantages. "We are a bootstrap company through and through" (S038)

### Debt
- Lived its downside: a three-year lease nearly sank him in the call center collapse (S037), and unpaid payroll taxes left him owing the IRS (S033). He later took a loan to buy a software company (S018, 2021). No general rule stated; inferred from S018, S033, S037 that he avoids fixed overhead more than debt itself.

### First hire and hiring
- Costliest mistake: hiring friends he liked instead of skilled people. "I'll hire someone based on like, I like the person, whatever, versus like, how do you find people who are already good at the thing you're trying to get done?" (S050, 2024-12)
- Continuity before employees: "until you have continuity, you don't have a business." (S037, 2017 talk)
- Hire A players with upside, not cash. "A players aren't necessarily looking for money today." (S044, 2022)
- 2026: AI let him grow without hiring copywriters. "I needed to do it in a way where I couldn't hire more people." (S041)

### Firing
- Admits he was too scared to fire a bad hire early on (S037). No later stated rule.

### Partners
- Saying yes to Todd Dickerson as a partner was, he says, his best decision outside marriage (S002, S034).

### Equity
- When unknown, take equity or revenue share for your first deals instead of a fee. "I would charge equity in it." (S052, 2024)

### Focus versus diversification
- 12 companies in 12 months was his biggest mistake. "Like it wasn't compounding. We had to recreate customers every single time." (S027, 2017)
- Yet he admits he keeps doing it: "funnel addiction" (S012) and a value ladder that "keeps becoming a value maze" (S026, 2021). He channels the urge into front ends that feed one core business (S047).

### Side projects
- Some projects (old books, a library) he does for love and monetizes later if he can (S047). To an engineer with a small app, sell or keep the side business as a hobby and focus on the one with spending power (S051).

### Niche
- Niche first. "the more niche specific, the actual presentation, the webinar, the landing page is the higher conversion rates will have across the whole board." (S047, 2024)
- On fitness: "It's like the health fitness market is great." (S051) He goes on that it is wide, hard to go deep, and so a volume business; small, richer niches go deeper.

### Coaching delivery and one-on-one
- One-on-one does not scale. "the one-on-one coaching over time will drown you." (S049, 2024)
- Moved from one-on-one to group to facilitated coaching (S049, S008). Uses certified past clients as coaches (S008).

### Sales calls
- He hates the phone and sells one-to-many; either works (S051). "I'm not a one-to-one sales trainer." (S052)
- Webinars pre-frame calls so reps take orders (S007, S049). Push to a call only when the VSL cannot sell a simple offer (S056).
- Never outbound chase high ticket. "So me outbound calling people I would never do for infinity dollars, right?" (S050)

### Content and social
- 2020 to 2022: social and podcasts build relationships and voice; publish daily (S021, S004).
- 2025, shifted: ads pay now, social later. "Social eventually turns into more money. Ads instantly." (S040). Says he may be bad at social (S040).
- Email sells. "email is still the best selling platform that we have on this planet" (S030, 2022)

### Paid ads
- Changed views. After the Google slap: "I didn't buy ads for 10 years myself." (S007, 2021). ClickFunnels bought no ads for about two years and grew through joint ventures (S051).
- Now: "a million dollars a month ad spend is the goal." (S053, 2023). He spends close to $2 million a month (S051, 2025). Start at the value of one sale and scale (S053).

### Partnerships and joint ventures
- Partnerships first, before ads or social, split 50-50 (S019, S051). For a fitness coach: partner outside fitness, because "it's gonna be hard to get fitness people to promote yourself because most of them are competing with you, right?" (S051)

### Brand
- Connection outlasts features. "they come because of a feeling, and they come because of a connection." (S036, 2019)
- Logos on sales pages: "logos just there to feed the ego of the entrepreneur" (S056)

### AI
- 2026, enthusiastic: "When you understand how to use funnel hacking and AI together, it changes everything." (S041). Says AI let him write a VSL that sells profitably at scale and a $5,500 challenge offer without a sales team (S041).
- Earlier (2024) only practical uses, such as AI translation of trainings (S052). No earlier skeptical view recorded.

### Remote work
- No recorded position.

### Work hours
- No recorded position on hours as such. He describes five-and-a-half-hour Q&A days and 10 months a year on one event (S052, S041).

### Burnout
- Ended Funnel Hacking Live after FHL 10 because he was tired, it cost over $6 million and took 10 months a year (S041, 2026). Grief after Dave Woodward's death made 2023 a blur (S038). "I didn't want to have the stress and anxiety of trying to sell 5,000 tickets to an event." (S042, 2026)

### Selling the company
- Turned down an offer around the 2020 election worth over $110 million each to him and Todd (S038, S040), because he did not feel done.

### Culture and management
- Weak at internal culture by his own account. "we've been so good at building external community culture, but not as good at building an internal team, staff culture." (S043, 2022)
- "Because I'm not good at the whole management and people part of the business." (S038). He gave the CEO role to Dave Woodward and later had to step back in.

### Metrics
- One key metric on the wall, watched daily (S040). Daily list growth (S024, S030). Second costliest mistake: not watching ad numbers daily. "you should never be spending money unless it's profitable all the time" (S050)

### Competition
- Wrestling made him competitive; he names a target to drive toward but says it is not hate (S035). Competitors already gathered your buyers, so target their audiences (S052). Says most SaaS firms cannot market: "Most SaaS companies built SaaS." (S055)

### Timing and recessions
- Keep selling in downturns; people still have money and the biggest companies were built then (S014, S021). Loves selling business opportunities in a recession (S052).

### Events
- Live events sell high ticket: in 2018 FHL tickets made about $3.5 million and coaching sold at the event about $13 million (S035). Ended FHL in 2025, then ran a smaller AI encore (S041, S042).

### Income claims and compliance
- Told guests on a webinar not to share income, out of respect for FTC rules (S017, 2022). No broader stated policy.

### Continuity and recurring revenue
- Core of his business: about 80% of ClickFunnels revenue is continuity (S052). For a weight loss coach selling $3,000 coaching off ads, build a $150 to $200 front end with continuity so the coaching becomes profit (S049). Prefers monthly to annual: "I don't like annuals as much myself." (S049)

### Health and fitness offers
- Weight loss coaching he has seen work at $2,500 to $3,500; rarely at $1,000 on a webinar; $10,000 offers usually add another angle (S049, 2024).
- Use holidays: "There's always a holiday that you will need to lose weight" (S056)
- Front-end health messaging should move people out of pain (S056); show the cost of inaction when people expect health care to be free (S052).
