# Frameworks: Russell Brunson

22 frameworks, most-used first, built from 56 podcast transcripts (2017 to 2026: solo episodes, Funnel Hacking Live keynotes, live Q&As, and the Andrew Warner interview series). "Sources" counts the corpus sources that use the framework or a clear synonym. Book tags show where he places a framework in his books, as he teaches it out loud; no book text is used. Quotes come from auto transcripts, so they keep transcript quirks. Scoring lines are guidance drawn from his own numbers, not rules he states as law.

---

## Selling

### 1. Hook, Story, Offer *(DotCom Secrets, Expert Secrets, Traffic Secrets)*
- **What it is:** His master diagnostic. Every ad, page, email and pitch has a hook (what makes someone stop), a story (what makes them care and raises perceived value) and an offer (what they get). If a funnel fails, one of the three is broken.
- **Parts:** Hook buys a few seconds and adds no value. Story changes their state and raises value. Offer bundles the product into something worth more than the price. Apply it page by page: ad, landing page, sales page, upsell (S014, S005).
- **When to use:** Any funnel or pitch that is not converting. Run it before changing traffic, price or product.
- **How to score:** Find the step where people drop. Clicks but no opt-ins means the hook or landing page; opt-ins but no sales means the story or offer. He wants about 30% on a registration page (S056).
- **Quote:** "It's either your hook or your story or your offer." (S002)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S002, S004, S005, S013, S014, S048, S056)

### 2. Value ladder *(DotCom Secrets)*
- **What it is:** A sequence of offers at rising price and value: free content, a book or low-ticket offer, a mid offer, coaching, a mastermind. The real secret is that each rung is built to sell the next.
- **Parts:** Give real value on each rung so people want more. Build a mechanism into each product that moves them up (bonus calls, an event pitch, speakers from the next tier). Solve one problem in a way that opens the next. Front rungs break even; profit comes deeper (S006). Map the whole ladder, then build only the first funnel (S046). Avoid the value maze, where every rung has four next steps (S026).
- **When to use:** Planning a product line, deciding what to build next, or when front-end ad costs rise.
- **How to score:** Ask whether every offer has one clear next step and a mechanism that sells it. Branches with no clear path score low.
- **Quote:** "It's structuring your product so that the product does the selling to move somebody to the next year." (S006)
- **Sources:** 10 (S006, S007, S008, S010, S026, S046, S049, S050, S051, S052)

### 3. Offer versus product, and the stack *(DotCom Secrets, Expert Secrets)*
- **What it is:** A product is a commodity that competes on price. An offer bundles it with other things to make it unique. Every offer is presented as a stack with a value beside each part.
- **Parts:** Never cut price; raise value until the price looks cheap. Bulk up with information (books, interviews, recorded workshops) even for physical products (S003). Use an "If You Give a Mouse a Cookie" stack where each part solves the problem the last part created (S048). Apply the sick-to-your-stomach test: if it feels too generous to give, it is probably irresistible (S054). For fast action, introduce a constraint in the stack, then release it (S051).
- **When to use:** Pricing, weak sales, a commodity product, or any launch.
- **How to score:** If buyers compare you on price, the offer is weak. If the stack does not make you a little nervous, it is probably not strong enough.
- **Quote:** "There is no circumstance when I ever sell that I don't use a stack slide." (S002)
- **Sources:** 7 (S002, S003, S028, S036, S048, S051, S054)

### 4. Epiphany Bridge, big domino, three false beliefs *(Expert Secrets)*
- **What it is:** People buy on emotion. Decisions need a state change, and story changes state. Tell the story of your own epiphany so they have it too.
- **Parts:** Chain of false belief: experience, then the story they tell themselves, then the belief. Find the one big domino belief that knocks down the rest. Break the three core false beliefs: the vehicle (this opportunity), internal (I can't do it) and external (something outside stops me), with one story each after the origin story (S004, S034).
- **When to use:** Writing a webinar, VSL, sales call or launch email; when buyers say "not for me".
- **How to score:** Name the big domino in one sentence. If you cannot, the pitch is not ready. Complex or expensive products need more stories.
- **Quote:** "your story trumps their story, and then your story becomes theirs." (S004)
- **Sources:** 5 (S004, S011, S014, S034, S051)

### 5. The perfect webinar and one-to-many selling *(Expert Secrets)*
- **What it is:** A scripted presentation: origin story, three secrets that break the three false beliefs, then the stack and close. He calls it his main way to sell anything above a low price.
- **Parts:** Sell the strategy (the what), never the tactics (the how) (S051). Open loops at drop-off points (S052). Do it live, weekly, and do not automate it while you are still improving it (S019). For coaches it pre-frames phone sales so calls become order taking (S007, S049).
- **When to use:** Offers from about $300 to $10,000; replacing one-to-one selling; launching a new expert.
- **How to score:** His rough guidance: on a $1,000 offer he aims for 10 to 15% of live attendees (S047).
- **Quote:** "your job is not to teach the how your job is to sell the how." (S051)
- **Sources:** 11 (S007, S015, S016, S017, S019, S047, S048, S049, S051, S052, S053)

### 6. Match the selling format to the price
- **What it is:** The more a thing costs, the more time a buyer needs with you before buying.
- **Parts:** Who, What, Why, How for short ads; an Epiphany Bridge VSL of 12 to 20 minutes for free-plus-shipping to about $97; a 90-minute webinar near $1,000; a three-day event or a VSL plus two phone calls for $10,000 to $25,000 (S048, S047, S055).
- **When to use:** Choosing a funnel for a new offer, or when an offer is not selling in its current format.
- **How to score:** If price is high and contact time is short, expect low conversion.
- **Quote:** "the more time someone has with you, the higher, the more expensive you can spend." (S047)
- **Sources:** 4 (S047, S048, S049, S055)

## Money and traffic

### 7. Whoever spends most to acquire a customer wins, and the break-even funnel *(DotCom Secrets)*
- **What it is:** His central law, from Dan Kennedy. Build a front end that pays back its ad cost at the point of sale, so customers arrive free and the back end is profit.
- **Parts:** Price the front end to liquidate ad costs (S056). Gamble the value of one sale to test a campaign, then scale (S053). Aim to spend as much as you can profitably (S052, S053).
- **When to use:** Any decision about paid ads, pricing a front end, or growth plans.
- **How to score:** Cash back on the front end versus ad spend. Under break even with no strong back end is a red flag.
- **Quote:** "whoever can spend the most money to acquire customer wins." (S039)
- **Sources:** 9 (S006, S021, S025, S039, S040, S049, S052, S053, S056)

### 8. Follow-up funnel and emotion, logic, fear *(DotCom Secrets)*
- **What it is:** The visible funnel breaks even; the money is in the emails and texts after. He reports $16.49 of follow-up revenue per $1 of front end over 30 days (S021).
- **Parts:** Track at which email you break even on the lead. Order every sequence the same way: emotion for about half of buyers, logic for the next 30%, fear (real urgency and scarcity) for the last 20%.
- **When to use:** After any opt-in or first purchase; launch planning.
- **How to score:** Do you know the break-even email? Is there real urgency at the end?
- **Quote:** "If you look at how I do my webinars, I lead with emotion, I follow logic, and I end with fear." (S021)
- **Sources:** 3 (S021, S030, S056)

### 9. The Dream 100 *(Traffic Secrets)*
- **What it is:** From Chet Holmes. List the people who already gathered your dream customers (podcasts, influencers, email lists, channels) and market to them, not the crowd.
- **Parts:** Work your way in (earn promotion, guest spots, revenue share) and buy your way in (ads to their audiences) at the same time (S023). Dig the well before you are thirsty. For B2B, target decision makers by mail and phone (S052).
- **When to use:** Getting first customers with no ad budget; any new launch.
- **How to score:** Count the partners who already reach your buyer and whether you have contacted them.
- **Quote:** "Because if I get one of those 100 people to say yes, it can blow up your business." (S022)
- **Sources:** 6 (S022, S023, S047, S051, S052, S007)

### 10. Earn, control, own traffic, and $1 per name per month *(Traffic Secrets)*
- **What it is:** Turn earned and bought traffic into owned traffic: your email list.
- **Parts:** Every interview and ad should send people to a lead page (S024). Rule of thumb from a friend: at least $1 per name per month. Email at least three times a week (S030). Track list growth daily.
- **When to use:** Choosing between social, ads and email; sizing a list goal.
- **How to score:** Followers do not count. Names on your list times $1 a month is the rough baseline.
- **Quote:** "When you own traffic, you own your own destiny, right?" (S024)
- **Sources:** 4 (S023, S024, S030, S040)

## Coaching and experts

### 11. Five steps to a coaching offer, and selling frameworks *(Expert Secrets)*
- **What it is:** How he says an expert turns a result into a paid program.
- **Parts:** Get the result yourself. Write a framework hypothesis. Test it on yourself many times. Give it a proprietary name. Prove it on others for free, then let their stories sell (S032). Teach it as story, strategy, tactics, case study (S016). Your dream customer is you five years ago (S032, S052).
- **When to use:** A coach or trainer building or repricing a program.
- **How to score:** No case studies on other people means not ready to sell high ticket.
- **Quote:** "You have to go and prove it now on somebody else." (S032)
- **Sources:** 5 (S015, S016, S032, S052, S034)

### 12. Lead with high ticket; start in the middle of the ladder
- **What it is:** For experts, start with a webinar or high-ticket offer, not a book. It is easier to make money and to break even on ads, then add cheaper front ends.
- **Parts:** Reverse engineer the freedom number: monthly income, price, sales needed, close rate, leads (S051). A $5,000 program and four sales a month can mean freedom.
- **When to use:** A new coach choosing what to sell first.
- **How to score:** If the plan needs thousands of low-ticket sales to reach the income goal, it scores low.
- **Quote:** "I'm actually a big proponent of, especially in the expert business, to lead with high tickets." (S008)
- **Sources:** 4 (S007, S008, S051, S027)

### 13. Free case study and application funnel
- **What it is:** A high-ticket coaching funnel with no live webinar: ad, a free case study or client success video, an apply button, an application that makes them pitch you, a homework page, then a setter and closer.
- **Parts:** The takeaway sell. Inbound calls beat outbound. His guidance: cost per application under $100; he reports about $1,500 per application (S031).
- **When to use:** Selling coaching or masterminds over $3,000.
- **How to score:** Cost per application against revenue per application.
- **Quote:** "A takeaway sell is like, look, I don't want to take everyone." (S031)
- **Sources:** 4 (S031, S032, S050, S052)

### 14. Facilitated coaching
- **What it is:** His newest delivery model (2024). After one-on-one and group comes recorded framework teaching, live facilitators, workbooks, work time and breakout practice. He calls it the least work to fulfill with the best student results he has had.
- **Parts:** Certify successful past clients as coaches (S008). Graduated access: more access to him only at the top (S008).
- **When to use:** A coach whose one-on-one load caps growth.
- **How to score:** If revenue depends on your hours, it scores low.
- **Quote:** "the one-on-one coaching over time will drown you." (S049)
- **Sources:** 2 (S008, S049)

## Focus and positioning

### 15. New opportunity versus improvement versus repair; the opportunity switch
- **What it is:** From Dan Kennedy. A new opportunity replaces a category; an improvement is better or faster; repair fixes. Switch a customer into your opportunity once, then stack every later product in it.
- **Parts:** Sell the opportunity on the front end, improvement in coaching, repair one-on-one (S045). One switch per business (S027).
- **When to use:** Positioning, naming, or deciding whether a new product fits.
- **How to score:** Does the new product ask buyers to switch again? If so, it scores low.
- **Quote:** "A new opportunity will out convert any improvement offer all day long." (S045)
- **Sources:** 3 (S027, S045, S036)

### 16. One market, one message, one offer; specialist over generalist
- **What it is:** Go deep in one niche before adding another. Niche presentations convert better and ads cost less.
- **Parts:** One webinar sells one thing (S056). Small rich markets go deeper than big wide ones (S051). Climb to a bigger market only in a way that feeds the same business (S052).
- **When to use:** A founder with several offers or audiences.
- **How to score:** Count the markets and messages. More than one on the front end scores low.
- **Quote:** "I would just focus on one market, one message, one offer." (S050)
- **Sources:** 6 (S027, S047, S050, S051, S052, S056)

### 17. One funnel to rule them all
- **What it is:** Each business has one core funnel; every other funnel exists to feed it.
- **Parts:** Build the back end once, then only make front ends (S010, S026). Small hinges swing big doors: a thank-you page link or a re-recorded offer can triple results (S055).
- **When to use:** Too many funnels, a value maze, or a new idea that may not fit.
- **How to score:** Does the new thing feed the core funnel? If not, it is a distraction.
- **Quote:** "there's only one funnel in each of my businesses that actually matters." (S055)
- **Sources:** 4 (S010, S026, S046, S055)

### 18. Funnel hacking, now with AI
- **What it is:** Buy and study what already works (sequence, offers, packaging), model it, then make your own. In 2026 he pairs this with AI to collect and reverse engineer competitors' sales letters.
- **Parts:** Buy slowly and watch the process (S020). Do not guess where someone has figured it out (S055).
- **When to use:** Any new funnel, copy or offer.
- **Quote:** "When you understand how to use funnel hacking and AI together, it changes everything." (S041)
- **Sources:** 4 (S020, S041, S051, S055)

### 19. Away from pain on the front end, toward pleasure on the back end
- **What it is:** From NLP. Front-end messaging gets people out of pain; the event or coaching sells the aspirational future. Entrepreneurs wrongly assume buyers move toward pleasure.
- **When to use:** Writing headlines and hooks; health, trauma or weight loss offers.
- **Quote:** "Most people buy something, especially on the front end, because they're trying to get out of pain." (S056)
- **Sources:** 3 (S023, S052, S056)

### 20. Geometric growth
- **What it is:** When something wins, roll it across every other business instead of moving on.
- **Quote:** "When you have a win, you got to compound that win upon the other win" (S025)
- **Sources:** 2 (S025, S055)

### 21. Volume of hooks
- **What it is:** Publish many hooks, let the market vote, turn the winners into ads.
- **Parts:** An hour of ad creative for every hour of social (S040). Test hooks like a comedian tests jokes (S005).
- **Quote:** "I have no idea what hooks are going to work, right?" (S005)
- **Sources:** 4 (S005, S040, S047, S051)

### 22. Sell the vision, not the product
- **What it is:** Sell the end result (the hole in the wall, time with your kids), not the tool.
- **Quote:** "People don't want to buy a hammer." (S050)
- **Sources:** 2 (S030, S050)
