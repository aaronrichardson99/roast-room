# Principles: Greg Nuckols

Built from 50 of his own Stronger By Science articles (2013 to 2026). 22 decision rules. "Sources" is the number of distinct sources that state or apply the rule. Confidence: high = 3 or more sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

1. **Judge an effect by its real size, not its label or its p-value.** "Don’t be misled when people are claiming a “significant” finding. Figure out how large the difference actually is, and ask yourself whether that difference actually matters." (S046) "these descriptors are fairly arbitrary in the first place" (S003) Sources: 4 (S003, S008, S025, S046). Confidence: high.

2. **Direct evidence on the outcome beats mechanisms and proxies.** "Trying to counter a large body of direct longitudinal evidence exclusively with wishy-washy presumed mechanisms and logical extrapolations is the equivalent of bringing a knife to a gun fight." (S005) "To directly assess the impact of creatine on muscle growth, you need studies that directly assess muscle growth." (S008) Sources: 6 (S002, S005, S007, S008, S012, S020). Confidence: high.

3. **A hypothesis is not evidence.** "You’re taking a process used for generating questions, and pretending like it generates answers." (S002) Sources: 3 (S002, S012, S020). Confidence: high.

4. **Scale your confidence to the amount of data.** "my confidence in any recommendation scales with the quantity of data supporting it" (S002) "I have much more confidence about the bottom end of that range" (S004) Sources: 4 (S002, S004, S013, S040). Confidence: high.

5. **Single studies are less trustworthy than people think; wait for replication.** "the findings of individual studies aren’t nearly as trustworthy as most people would like to believe" (S025) "Be leery of any single result that hasn’t been replicated, because p-values won’t save you." (S035) Sources: 4 (S025, S035, S027, S014). Confidence: high.

6. **One study does not overturn a strong case.** "I also think the case in favor of hypertrophy increasing strength potential is strong enough that it would take substantially stronger empirical evidence than was provided by this study in order to disprove it." (S014) Sources: 2 (S014, S038). Confidence: medium.

7. **When strong evidence goes against you, change your mind, and say so.** "this is generally what you should actually do" (S005) "As it turns out, many people (myself included) may have gotten too excited, too soon." (S020) Sources: 5 (S005, S007, S020, S021, S004). Confidence: high.

8. **Commit to a method, not an outcome.** "My commitment is to a particular method of inquiry, not to a particular outcome." (S002) Sources: 2 (S002, S045). Confidence: medium.

9. **State your bias up front, even when it cuts against your business.** "From the outset, I want to make my pre-existing bias clear." (S026) "as a business owner, the low-volume position is clearly the more marketable position" (S002) Sources: 4 (S002, S014, S026, S030). Confidence: high.

10. **Average results do not tell you how one client will respond.** "We should also remember that “no difference on average” isn’t necessarily the same as “no difference for individuals.”" (S011) Sources: 6 (S002, S008, S009, S011, S019, S027). Confidence: high.

11. **When the average is a wash, let individual trial and error decide.** "For now, good old fashion trial-and-error is the only way to find out which approach will give you better results." (S011) Sources: 4 (S009, S011, S036, S046). Confidence: high.

12. **If the program is working, do not change it.** "If you’re currently making solid, consistent gains training however you currently train, don’t change a thing. A bird in the hand is worth two in the bush." (S002) Sources: 4 (S002, S026, S044, S009). Confidence: high.

13. **Coaches should act on good odds when the downside is small.** "If you’re 70% sure something will help, and the costs or risks associated with that decision are minimal, it typically makes sense to pull the trigger." (S026) Sources: 1 (S026). Confidence: low.

14. **Effort is the most common missing piece.** "I really think that the median lifter just doesn’t put that much effort into their training." (S010) "More effort tends to yield better results." (S039) Sources: 5 (S010, S038, S039, S002, S044). Confidence: high.

15. **Hard work makes muscle grow; keep the takeaway broad.** "Hard work makes muscles grow. More work and harder work generally make them grow more." (S038) Sources: 3 (S038, S044, S002). Confidence: high.

16. **Rebut the idea and the popular version, not the person.** "I want to reiterate that I’m not trying to call Chris out, or anything of that nature." (S023) "I’m quick to call out ideas that are suspect or outright wrong (while trying to exercise the principle of charity ), but I try to avoid passing personal judgements unless I have all the facts and am sure beyond a reasonable doubt that the people in question are behaving unethically." (S035) Sources: 5 (S023, S035, S029, S021, S001). Confidence: high.

17. **Most bad statistics are ignorance, not malice.** "I think that some p-hacking is conducted maliciously, but I think most instances are simply due to people not knowing any better ." (S025) Sources: 3 (S025, S004, S002). Confidence: high.

18. **Do not let a gene test, a label, or a slow start set the ceiling.** "you don’t truly know how good your genes are until you train for a decade with the expectation that you have good genes" (S024) "I think most classifications of “high responders,” “low responders,” and “nonresponders” are essentially bunk." (S009) Sources: 4 (S009, S024, S033, S036). Confidence: high.

19. **Do not shame clients; it corrupts the data and the relationship.** "We don’t expect people to eat like robots, and we don’t shame people for the choices they make." (S015) "Don’t be a dick to people who are having a hard time." (S036) Sources: 3 (S015, S036, S049). Confidence: high.

20. **Flag opinion that has no data behind it.** "I won’t pretend like I have solid references to back this up" (S013) "This is a decidedly non-evidence-based recommendation, but it’s one I believe in strongly." (S039) Sources: 4 (S013, S039, S041, S017). Confidence: high.

21. **Bro-science is R&D: useful, provisional, and it yields to good research.** "When bro-science contradicts something that’s been well supported by good research, real science should trump it." (S046) Sources: 3 (S046, S045, S050). Confidence: high.

22. **Do fewer things well, and only sell what you would buy.** "We either need to do some hiring, or we need to do fewer things overall so that we can do the remaining things well. Maybe/probably both." (S006) "I’d never charge someone for something that I wouldn’t buy myself." (S043) Sources: 3 (S006, S043, S049). Confidence: high.
