# Positions: Alan Aragon

Built from the notes on 38 sources (2009 to 2026). 34 topics, written for a fitness coaching audience. Each stance has a verbatim quote, source id, and date (see `sources.md`). Where he changed his mind, both views sit side by side with dates; the newer one is marked. Topics with no evidence say so.

## Protein: how much
- **Stance:** The macro he cares most about. Set it on target bodyweight. General public 1.2 to 1.6 g/kg (he prefers 1.6 as a floor); muscle gain 1.6 to 2.2; lean lifters dieting up to about 3 g/kg. Over time his numbers stayed in this band.
- 2019-09: "I ballpark protein targets right around 1 g per pound of LBM." (S028)
- 2024-08: "So 0.7 to 1.0 gram per pound of ideal body weight, or target body weight is the sweet spot for total daily protein intake." (S034)
- 2026: "For the general population, a daily protein intake of 1.2–1.6 g/kg BW is appropriate for supporting total body weight and fat loss" (S027)
- 2021-02: "Protein is an underrated macronutrient." (S032); 2024-09: "It is both underrated and overrated." (S038)

## Protein: per meal and distribution
- **Stance:** Total first. For elite muscle gain, about four feedings of 0.4 to 0.55 g/kg; for everyone else distribution barely matters; when cutting it matters even less. "Absorption" is not a limit.
- 2010-01: "The concern that the body can only use X amount of protein in a single dose is nonsense." (S018)
- 2018-02: "to maximize anabolism one should consume protein at a target intake of 0.4 g/kg/meal across a minimum of four meals in order to reach a minimum of 1.6 g/kg/day." (S025)
- 2020-06: "For the goal of retaining muscle during a cut meal frequency, basically doesn’t matter, bro." (S031)

## Protein timing and the anabolic window
- **Stance:** The window is days wide when you have eaten before training; pre versus post makes no difference when daily protein is met. His 2013 review and meta-analysis made this case.
- 2009-11: "If you have to chew it, it ain't anabolic." (S019) (sarcasm)
- 2025-07: "So the anabolic window is actually not hours but days." (S030)

## Very high protein and fat gain
- **Stance:** In trained, free-living lifters, adding protein on top rarely adds fat and sometimes causes recomp; in sedentary ward studies it does add fat. Safe for healthy kidneys.
- 2025-07: "just stack the protein on top, nobody gains fat and some people lose fat." (S030)
- 2025-08: "there is virtually zero threat to those organ systems that you would think might be threatened by a high protein intake." (S029)

## Plant versus animal protein
- **Stance:** Animal protein is more anabolic per gram; at 1.6 g/kg vegans and omnivores gained alike in untrained subjects. He stays omnivore; mix sources; soy isolate under about 60 g a day.
- 2023-06: "I'm always going to personally default to omnivore ism for that." (S035)
- 2021-02: "So there’s a certain virtue to variety of sources" (S032)

## Carbohydrates
- **Stance:** Carb needs scale with activity, leanness and goal. Eat the most carbs that still allow fat loss. For maximal muscle gain at least about 3 g/kg (or 1 to 1.5 g/lb). No strict minimum for fat loss.
- 2013-03: "The leaner & more trained someone becomes the more a higher carb intake can actually enhance body composition – fat loss included." (S003)
- 2012-09: "the underlying aim is to consume the highest amount of carbs that still allow a satisfactory rate of fat loss." (S039)

## Low carb and keto
- **Stance:** Works mainly by cutting junk and raising protein; no metabolic advantage when protein and calories match; most people cannot sustain it; costs endurance and some muscle size; a Mediterranean-style keto is legit.
- 2013-03: "he simply did not bring a comparatively compelling case for a low-carb/ketogenic diet's application to competitive athletes." (S003)
- 2019-09: "there's no fat loss magic to low-carb/keto diets." (S028) (quote continues to protein)
- 2025-08: "There's something about the ketogenic diet that the majority of people who engage it just can't stick to." (S029)

## Dietary fat
- **Stance:** Floor at about 0.3 g/lb or 20 percent of calories; favor nuts, olive oil, avocado, seeds, fish; saturated fat in moderation; seed oils over-vilified.
- 2021-02: "that’s the kind of the lower cut. That you would be very wary of going below." (S032)
- 2025-07: "The big picture is that people over vilify seed oils." (S030)
- 2010-03 (older, softer): skeptical of omega-6 as a single villain. "There's magic in peanut butter, you didn't know this?" (S014)

## Sugar, fructose and fruit
- **Stance:** Dose and context. Fruit improves outcomes. Added sugar's problem is micronutrient dilution and hyperpalatable combos; keep it near 10 percent of calories.
- 2010-01: "Demonizing fructose without mentioning the dose-dependent nature of its effects is intellectually dishonest." (S017)
- 2025-08: added sugars about 10 percent max, within a 10 to 20 percent discretionary margin. (S029, paraphrase)

## Artificial sweeteners
- **Stance:** Net positive for weight control; saccharin the only bad actor; aspartame cancer scare is a dose fantasy.
- 2025-08: "Generally speaking, they're a nothing burger and there's a lot of scaremongering around artificial sweeteners." (S029)
- 2023-08: "the doses required to reach the levels of concern are just so astronomical." (S033)

## Fat loss rate and deficit size
- **Stance:** Deficit 10 to 20 percent for most; 20 to 40 percent only as a crash he does not love; 0.5 to 1 percent bodyweight a week; fatter people faster, lean people slower.
- 2024-08: "the safest caloric deficit to target is right around 10% to 20% below maintenance." (S034)
- 2025-08: "You're basically crash dieting. I don't love doing that though, honestly." (S029)

## Plateaus and metabolic adaptation
- **Stance:** A plateau is 4 to 8 weeks flat with good adherence; mostly adherence or a new maintenance point. Adaptation is real but modest and mostly NEAT; "metabolic damage" is overblown.
- 2025-08: "I have to emphasize the major component that slows down is your non exercise activity." (S029)
- 2026: "challenging the concept of a persistent "starvation mode" that prevents long-term weight loss/weight loss maintenance." (S027)

## Diet breaks, refeeds, cheat meals, reverse dieting
- **Stance:** Diet breaks yes (a week every 4 to 8 weeks); refeeds help adherence in long low-carb diets, not hormones; cheat meals are a crutch for a bad diet; reverse diet back to maintenance within two weeks.
- 2019-09: "Diet breaks ideally should be done on an autoregulated basis." (S028)
- 2019-09: "In my opinion, that means 2 weeks max to get back into full pre-dieting maintenance intake." (S028)

## Intermittent fasting
- **Stance (changed):** Older view doubted long protein gaps for muscle; newer view: IF works as calorie control, equal to daily restriction; TRE plus lifting keeps muscle; very narrow windows and ADF are riskier for lean people; autophagy is no reason to fast.
- 2012-01 (older): "regularly going 16-20 hours without protein would not likely be conducive to goals such as muscular strength &/or hypertrophy" (S007)
- 2012-09: "IF presents an effective option for those who prefer the convenience and luxury of larger meals" (S039)
- 2024-09 (newer): "It's really just another way to control total energy coming in through the week." (S038)
- 2025-08: "fasting is great until it isn't because you've achieved what you needed to achieve." (S029)

## Meal frequency and breakfast
- **Stance:** Preference decides; grazers and gorgers both fine. Breakfast is not essential for adults.
- 2012-09: "I've seen the greatest client success come from letting individual preference dictate meal frequency." (S039)
- 2024-09: "Not credible at all. It's false." (S038) (on breakfast as essential)

## Carbs at night and food combining
- **Stance:** Broscience; works only by cutting calories.
- 2010-06: "There are no night-time insulin fairies ready & waiting to store carbs in the fat tissue" (S010)

## Cardio
- **Stance:** Least amount needed for fat loss; type by preference; fasted versus fed makes no difference; resistance training matters more.
- 2019-09: "the objective should be to do the least amount of cardio necessary to get the job done." (S028)
- 2024-04: "do the type that you personally prefer and the type that you personally can stick to" (S037)

## Training volume
- **Stance (stable with refinement):** About 8 to 16 sets per muscle per week (2010); 10 to 20 for advanced, 6 to 12 for most, 3 to 6 to maintain (2024). Defers to Schoenfeld on hypertrophy.
- 2010-01: "4-8 worksets per training bout equates to 8-16 worksets per muscle group per week." (S018)
- 2024-04: "Three to six is kind of the catch all minimum effective dose." (S037)

## Frequency
- **Stance:** Each muscle about twice a week; split more than about 8 to 10 weekly sets across two sessions. He lifts 4 to 5 days a week. (S018, S029, S037)

## Intensity and failure
- **Stance:** People leave 2 to 3 reps in reserve anyway; take isolation and machine work to failure; leave 1 to 2 on heavy barbell lifts.
- 2025-08: "But for single joint isolation exercises and even machine exercises and lighter loads, where it's safe. Take it to failure, man." (S029)
- 2025-07: "I like to train to failure all the freaking time." (S030)

## Exercise selection and variety
- **Stance:** Rotate one main exercise per month for interest; push, pull, squat as a beginner base; cluster and drop sets to save time. Admits his own training is fun, not optimal. (S036, S037, S030)
- 2025-07: "it is probably not the most efficient way to make muscle gains, but I just find it fun and I enjoy it." (S030)

## Periodization, deloads and maintenance phases
- **Stance:** Build maintenance phases and rest weeks in; plateaus should get longer.
- 2009-11: "people should consciously build plateau phases into their programs." (S019)

## Bulking, cutting and recomposition
- **Stance:** Recomp for overfat or deconditioned beginners; bulk/cut for lean advanced lifters; surplus 10 to 20 percent; accept up to 1:1 fat to muscle when very advanced; avoid "fulking".
- 2010-04: "What I'm trying to convey is that more isn't always better when it comes to racking up pounds." (S013)
- 2024-09: "A one to one ratio at most of lean mass gain and fat gain during a bulking cycle." (S038)

## Supplements
- **Stance:** Creatine is king; multivitamin, fish oil, D3 reasonable; caffeine modest; BCAAs pointless with enough protein; collagen he takes despite evidence-based minimalism; frames his own stack as bets. No universal prescriptions.
- 2009-11: "The majority of health nuts will spend $100's a month on useless supplements, but won't spend a dime on actually educating themselves on the facts about the body." (S019)
- 2024-09: "Whey is like BCAA plus." (S038)
- 2025-08: "It's the only non pharmacological supplement that really, really has a very strong and deep and broad evidence base for enhancing the effects of resistance training." (S029)

## Diet adherence and flexible dieting
- **Stance:** The center of his work. No taboo foods; 80 to 90 percent whole foods; a 10 to 20 percent margin; meals you love.
- 2011-12: "Moderation & not tagging any foods as taboo or off-limits is the best way to sustain good dietary habits in the long-term." (S007)
- 2024-08: "Nobody talks about how crucial it is to actually not hate your diet." (S034)

## Tracking
- **Stance:** Individualize precision. Track 1 to 2 weeks to learn portions; then many people do fine loose. He no longer counts.
- 2019-09: "IMO, life is way too long to be counting macro grams, unless you've got a lot at stake, such as a competition." (S028)

## Beginners versus advanced
- **Stance:** Beginners recomp and grow on almost anything; advanced lifters need precise surplus, protein spread and volume; teens should not expect elite size.
- 2020-06: "You have to pull a lot more strings with the pros and the elites, whereas shoot with the newbies. You throw anything at the wall and most of it will stick" (S031)

## Women, menopause and the female athlete
- **Stance:** Almost no sex-specific diet rules; start women at 1.6 g/kg; menopause scaremongering is unwarranted; lower expectations about half; watch for lost periods and RED-S.
- 2025-08: "What has been effective is just lowering the expectation of progress." (S029)

## Contest prep and physique sport
- **Stance:** Not healthy; keep carbs as high as possible; natural prep runs six months but breeds neurosis; diet breaks in prep.
- 2021-02: "The physical unhealth of contest prep is something that’s very understated." (S032)

## GLP-1 drugs
- **Stance:** Effective; a life jacket for people who need it; should be a last resort and weaned off with skills built; worried about kids and first-line use.
- 2025-08: "So I almost want to see them fail, but the evidence is in favor of them." (S036)
- 2024-04: "last resort, let's learn the skills we need to learn in order to get a win win out of this." (S037)

## Alcohol
- **Stance:** Red wine literature mostly positive in his reading, but alcohol disinhibits and adds calories; about 10 percent have a use disorder; he quit. He does not preach abstinence for everyone.
- 2024-09: "Since I quit, I lost about 25 pounds of fat and I gained about 5, maybe 7 pounds of muscle, and it's night and day." (S038)

## Evidence versus anecdote, social media and fitness myths
- **Stance:** Research over hearsay, cross-checked with the field; be skeptical; extremes sell.
- 2010-06: "Both anecdotal and research evidence have to be viewed critically." (S010)
- 2025-08: "Just be more skeptical. Don't believe stuff so quickly." (S036)

## Client communication and coaching
- **Stance:** Frequent check-ins; results motivate; write down reasons and barriers; set realistic expectations; teach until the client gets it.
- 2025-08: "Midweek check-ins, even daily reports from clients, individuals, even in coaching groups, that seems to work a lot better than just sending them off on their own untethered for a week." (S036)
- 2013-01: "I'm the one who needs to step up my teaching game in order to reach the seemingly unteachable." (S004)

## Running a coaching business
- **Stance:** Results and care beat credentials; do great work rather than hype launches; work as an employee first; keep it simple (he avoided owning a gym or having employees); price low for reach; online coaching with daily contact. Admits he is weak at marketing.
- 2010-11: "For anyone thinking of creating a successful business, spend some time first as an employee" (S008)
- 2012-10: "My marketing plan has always been to consistently produce great work. Anything beyond that seems too complicated." (S005)
- 2021-02: "I’m not good enough at marketing to do that." (S032)

## Pricing and money
- **Stance:** Few direct numbers; kept AARR cheap on purpose; turned down a pro team job to keep his model and family time. (S009, S023, S031)
