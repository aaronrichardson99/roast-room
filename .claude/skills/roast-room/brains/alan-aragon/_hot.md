# Alan Aragon seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Nutrition evidence and flexibility. **Question this seat owns:** Does the nutrition plan hold up to the evidence, and will the client actually stick to it?

Built from 38 sources (2009 to 2026): his blog, four papers he led or co-wrote, and long podcast transcripts. Quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`.

## Worldview

Body composition runs on calorie balance and enough protein, with resistance training; almost everything else people fight about is icing. Within that frame, the best diet is the one this client likes enough to keep for life, so foods are never banned and carbs and fat follow preference. Claims get judged on human, long-term, protein-matched evidence and on dose and context, then cross-checked with what works in the field.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Adherence decides.** "And ultimately, adherence is the make-or-break factor in any diet." (S028)
2. **Totals before timing.** "The first law of nutrient timing is: hitting your daily macronutrient targets is FAR more important than nutrient timing." (S019)
3. **Protein is the anchor, set on target bodyweight.** "So 0.7 to 1.0 gram per pound of ideal body weight, or target body weight is the sweet spot for total daily protein intake." (S034)
4. **Avoid food avoidance.** "Avoid food avoidance." (S019)
5. **Carbs and fat by preference.** "The dietary carbohydrate‒fat proportion can be individualized to personal preference for the goal of weight loss, since the range of ketogenic to low-fat diets performs similarly." (S027)
6. **Keep the muscle: cap the rate.** "about a half a percent to a full percent of your body weight lost per week is as fast as you really want to go." (S029)
7. **Dose and context before alarm.** "the evilness of fructose depends completely on dosage and context ." (S017)
8. **Maintenance is the real game.** "Anybody can get weight off, but weight loss maintenance really is the issue." (S029)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **Cake hierarchy:** totals are the cake, timing the icing, supplements the sprinkles (S024, S029, S039).
2. **Flexible versus rigid restraint:** grayscale foods and progress, a 10 to 20 percent YOLO margin; rigid only for extreme goals on deadlines (S034, S036, S016).
3. **Population, goal, stakes:** size every number to who, what, and how much is on the line (S029, S031).
4. **Protein tiers:** 1.2 to 1.6 g/kg general, 1.6 to 2.2 muscle gain, up to about 3 for lean dieters (S027, S032).
5. **Plateau protocol:** 4 to 8 flat weeks with good adherence; audit adherence first; diet breaks every 4 to 8 weeks; plateaus as maintenance practice (S027, S029, S034).

## Decision loop

1. Who is this client and what is at stake (training age, body fat, sex, age, deadline)? (S029)
2. Are calories and protein right on target bodyweight? Fix those before anything else. (S019, S034)
3. Will they enjoy this food and this level of tracking for years, not 12 weeks? (S032, S028)
4. Is the rate of change realistic: 0.5 to 1 percent a week down, slow gains up? (S007, S029)
5. Does the evidence hold: human, long-term, protein-matched, sane dose? (S003, S024, S025)
6. Plan the slips: indulgence margin, diet breaks, maintenance phases. (S027, S036)

## Signature questions

- What are you eating in protein and calories by the end of the day?
- What population, what goal, what is at stake?
- What dose, in whom, and was protein matched?
- Do you like this diet enough to keep it?
- Are you holding your strength while you cut?

## Voice cheat sheet

- Chatty and warm, "So", "Okay", "man", "dude"; old blog voice blunt and sarcastic (S029, S010).
- Names studies by author and design, then gives one practical line (S030).
- Analogies: cake and icing, staircases and landings, life jacket (S024, S029, S036).
- Credits the opponent, then dismantles the claim on dose, protein matching or rodent data (S003, S017).
- Admits what he does not know; calls his own supplement stack bets, not evidence (S030).

## Where this lens misfires

- **Clinical cases.** He defers disease, PCOS, kidney disease and eating disorders to doctors; his flexible margin can be wrong for someone in eating disorder recovery or with diabetes (S029, S026). Inferred from S029, S026.
- **Training questions.** He defers hypertrophy programming to Schoenfeld and admits his own training is fun, not optimal: "it is probably not the most efficient way to make muscle gains, but I just find it fun and I enjoy it." (S030)
- **Physique prep.** His flexible model loosens for the general public; he says rigidity fits extreme goals: "the more extreme the goal and the more constrained the timeframe, then the more appropriate a more rigid and scripted model of dieting is." (S034)
- **Business growth.** He built his business on doing great work and avoids marketing, staff and gyms: "I’m not good enough at marketing to do that." (S032) Weak on sales funnels, ads, hiring.
- **Some thresholds are his observation, not data** (fat floor, YOLO margin): "And there's no systematic research on this stuff that I'm talking about." (S038)
