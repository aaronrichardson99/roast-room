# Frameworks: Alan Aragon

Built from the notes on 38 sources (2009 to 2026): his own blog and comment replies, papers he led or co-wrote, and long podcast transcripts. 20 frameworks, roughly most-used first. "Sources" counts distinct sources where the idea is used. Names are his where he named them; otherwise a short name marked *(unnamed)*. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance built from his stated thresholds, not quotes.

---

## 1. The cake hierarchy (cake, icing, sprinkles)
- **What it is:** His ranking of what matters in nutrition. Total daily calories and macros (protein first) are the cake; timing and distribution are the icing; supplements are the sprinkles.
- **Parts:** 1) total daily protein and calories; 2) distribution of doses through the day; 3) timing around training; 4) supplements.
- **When to use:** Any question about meal timing, the anabolic window, meal frequency, pre/post-workout shakes, or a supplement stack. Push the client back to the cake.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan nails total protein and calories and treats timing as optional; 3 if it gets totals right but spends client effort on timing rules; 1 if it obsesses over timing or supplements while totals are off.
- **Quote:** "The cake is total daily macronutrition (and micronutrition), the icing is the specific timing and distribution of nutrient intake through the day, and the sprinkles are supplements that might help trainees clinch the competitive edge." (S024)
- **Also:** "The daily total for protein, that is the cake, the distribution of the doses through the day, that's the icing on the cake and it's a very thin layer of icing." (S029)
- **Sources:** 8 (S019, S024, S029, S030, S036, S039, S025, S027)

## 2. Flexible versus rigid restraint (Flexible Dieting)
- **What it is:** The core of his book. Rigid control sees foods and progress as all-or-nothing; flexible control works on a grayscale. Flexible restraint links to better weight control and fewer eating disorder symptoms.
- **Parts:** No taboo foods; mostly whole and minimally refined foods; a discretionary margin; progress judged as a trend, not pass/fail; even the degree of tracking is individualized.
- **When to use:** Any diet design, client relapse, "clean eating" rules, cheat meals, binge cycles.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan has no forbidden foods, a planned indulgence margin, and a way back after slips; 3 if flexible on foods but rigid on hitting exact numbers daily; 1 if it runs on a banned list or "you failed" thinking.
- **Exception:** rigidity is fine for extreme goals on tight deadlines (physique prep). "the more extreme the goal and the more constrained the timeframe, then the more appropriate a more rigid and scripted model of dieting is." (S034)
- **Quote:** "So it looks at foods and dieting on a grayscale, and it doesn’t look at goal reaching or progress on a dichotomous basis either." (S034)
- **Sources:** 8 (S016, S017, S019, S028, S034, S035, S036, S007)

## 3. Population, goal, stakes *(his question set)*
- **What it is:** Before any advice, ask who the person is (training age, body fat, sex, age), what the goal is, and what is at stake (a national show versus the apartment pool).
- **When to use:** Every consult. It decides protein tier, meal frequency, deficit size, how rigid to be.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan is sized to this client's level and stakes; 1 if it copies elite or enhanced athlete habits onto a general client.
- **Quote:** "So population goal, what level, what's at stake?" (S029)
- **Also:** "for that question, the population and the goal is always neglected." (S031)
- **Sources:** 6 (S029, S030, S031, S035, S038, S019)

## 4. Protein tiers
- **What it is:** Protein by goal, set on target bodyweight (a proxy for lean mass).
- **Parts (2026 ISSN stand he led):** general population 1.2 to 1.6 g/kg; muscle gain 1.6 to 2.2 g/kg with lifting; lean lifters dieting 1.9 to 3.2 g/kg. In conversation: 0.7 to 1.0 g per lb of target bodyweight is the sweet spot; elite natural physique athletes about 3 to 3.3 g/kg.
- **When to use:** Any nutrition plan. He prefers a floor of 1.5 to 1.6 g/kg even for the general public.
- **How to score:** 5 if protein sits in the right tier for the goal on target weight; 3 if adequate but under the tier when dieting; 1 if under about 1.2 g/kg for a lifter or set on a 300 lb client's current weight.
- **Quote:** "So 0.7 to 1.0 gram per pound of ideal body weight, or target body weight is the sweet spot for total daily protein intake." (S034)
- **Also:** "For the general population, a daily protein intake of 1.2–1.6 g/kg BW is appropriate for supporting total body weight and fat loss" (S027)
- **Sources:** 10 (S024, S025, S027, S028, S029, S030, S032, S034, S036, S037)

## 5. Dose and context
- **What it is:** No food or compound is good or toxic without dose and context. Ask: what dose, in whom, against what alternative, measured how.
- **When to use:** Food scares: fructose, sugar, seed oils, aspartame, red meat, eggs, alcohol, "toxins".
- **How to score:** 5 if a claim names a realistic dose and human outcome; 1 if it extrapolates mega-doses in rodents.
- **Quote:** "the evilness of fructose depends completely on dosage and context ." (S017)
- **Also:** "there is a threshold of intake below which you’re really looking at one big nothing burger." (S033)
- **Sources:** 7 (S014, S016, S017, S018, S030, S033, S029)

## 6. Rate of loss limit
- **What it is:** Fat loss speed capped to keep muscle. About 0.5 to 1 percent of bodyweight a week, roughly a pound; heavier people faster at first; leaner and more advanced people slower.
- **When to use:** Setting deficits, judging a client who is "losing too slow", prep timelines.
- **How to score:** 5 if 0.5 to 1 percent a week with lifting and enough protein; 3 if 1 to 1.5 percent for an obese beginner; 1 if a lean lifter is losing over 1 percent a week.
- **Quote:** "about a half a percent to a full percent of your body weight lost per week is as fast as you really want to go." (S029)
- **Also:** "The higher the baseline body fat level, the more aggressively the caloric deficit may be imposed. Slower rates of weight loss can better preserve lean mass (LM) in leaner subjects." (S024)
- **Sources:** 7 (S007, S024, S027, S029, S036, S038, S018)

## 7. Plateau protocol and maintenance practice
- **What it is:** Define a plateau as 4 to 8 weeks of no change with good compliance. Only two causes: adherence slipped or a new maintenance point. Audit adherence first. Treat plateaus as practice for maintenance; progress goes like staircases and landings.
- **When to use:** "My client stopped losing." Check trend weight (7 to 14 day averages) plus one other measure.
- **How to score:** 5 if the coach audits intake, checks other measures, and frames the stall; 1 if they slash calories or switch diets after one bad week.
- **Quote:** "A 4- to 8-week period of stagnation in the face of good adherence can be considered a plateau." (S027)
- **Also:** "So you can think of it as staircases and landings." (S029)
- **Sources:** 5 (S006, S027, S029, S034, S019)

## 8. Diet breaks ("non-YOLO maintenance")
- **What it is:** A week at maintenance with rules lifted, but not a binge, every 4 to 8 weeks or every 5 to 10 lb lost; longer 1 to 3 month maintenance phases for big losses. Mainly a psychological reset.
- **When to use:** Long diets, contest prep (1 to 2 breaks in 16 weeks), clients showing diet fatigue; align with the menstrual cycle when useful.
- **Quote:** "So it's what I call "non YOLO maintenance." So you take off the rules, take off the restrictions, but you're not eating with sheer abandon." (S029)
- **Sources:** 6 (S027, S028, S029, S030, S031, S034)

## 9. The YOLO margin (discretionary calories)
- **What it is:** 10 to 20 percent of calories from whatever you like; closer to 10 in a surplus. Removes the forbidden-fruit pull.
- **Quote:** "And the working practical margin for that is about 10% to 20% of total calories coming from essentially whatever on earth you want." (S036)
- **Also:** "Take the power away from the food and put the power back in your hands" (S036)
- **Sources:** 6 (S007, S028, S029, S035, S036, S037)

## 10. Protein-matched comparison test *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** When a diet "beats" another, check whether protein and calories were matched. Most low-carb and keto advantages vanish when they are.
- **When to use:** Reading studies, debunking diet tribes, judging a client's anecdote.
- **Quote:** "the 'special effects' of LCD and KD are not due to their alleged metabolic advantage, but their higher protein content." (S024)
- **Also:** "So protein is kind of the great equalizer, that protein and total calories." (S030)
- **Sources:** 6 (S003, S024, S028, S030, S036, S027)

## 11. Carbs as the trump card when cutting
- **What it is:** Cut calories mostly from carbs, never from protein, fat only to a floor. Keep the most carbs that still allow fat loss, then trim as needed. Save added cardio for plateaus.
- **Quote:** "the underlying aim is to consume the highest amount of carbs that still allow a satisfactory rate of fat loss." (S039)
- **Also:** "save cardio as a "trump card" to increase your calories burned once you hit a plateau that can't be broken with further decreases in calorie cutting." (S018)
- **Sources:** 5 (S018, S032, S038, S039, S028)

## 12. Fat floor
- **What it is:** Keep dietary fat at about 0.3 g per lb target bodyweight or about 20 percent of calories at minimum; start nearer 0.5 g/lb. Low fat plus a deficit can lower testosterone. He admits it is observational.
- **Quote:** "that’s the kind of the lower cut. That you would be very wary of going below." (S032)
- **Sources:** 3 (S032, S034, S038)

## 13. Bulk/cut versus recomp decision
- **What it is:** Overfat untrained or deconditioned people recomp at maintenance. Lean advanced lifters run dedicated bulks (about 10 to 20 percent surplus) and cuts. Advanced lifters with sloppy programs fix the program first. Acceptable bulk ratio 2:1 lean to fat, 1:1 at most for very advanced. Recomp is finite.
- **Quote:** "A one to one ratio at most of lean mass gain and fat gain during a bulking cycle." (S038)
- **Also:** "recomposition is a finite process." (S027)
- **Sources:** 5 (S024, S027, S030, S037, S038)

## 14. Realistic rate benchmarks
- **What it is:** Fat loss about 1 to 2 lb a week early, about a pound later; muscle gain about 1 to 2 lb a month for novices; advanced half that or less. Lower expectations by about half for perimenopausal women and the very lean.
- **Quote:** "For fat loss, roughly 1-2 pounds a week is plenty. For muscle gain, roughly 1-2 pounds a month is plenty." (S007)
- **Sources:** 6 (S007, S015, S018, S029, S030, S036)

## 15. Weekly set volume
- **What it is:** 10 to 20 hard sets per muscle per week for advanced lifters; above 20 courts overuse; most real-world clients do well on 6 to 12; split over two sessions above about 8 to 10; 3 to 6 sets a week maintains; train strong muscles at maintenance and spend effort on lagging ones. (2010 version: 8 to 16 per muscle per week.)
- **Quote:** "Three to six is kind of the catch all minimum effective dose." (S037)
- **Also:** "4-8 worksets per training bout equates to 8-16 worksets per muscle group per week." (S018)
- **Sources:** 4 (S018, S029, S037, S011)

## 16. Planned maintenance phases and deloads
- **What it is:** Build plateaus and rest into the plan. A full week off training every 8 to 12 weeks (his 2009 view); planned maintenance phases that lengthen over time.
- **Quote:** "people should consciously build plateau phases into their programs." (S019)
- **Also:** "taking a full week off from training – I'm talking don't even drive near the gym – about every 8th to 12th week." (S019)
- **Sources:** 4 (S019, S027, S029, S034)

## 17. Calorie-cutting default tactics *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Rules like "no carbs at night", carb cutoffs, food combining, fasting all work, when they work, by cutting calories. Fine as a client default, wrong as a law.
- **Quote:** ""No carbs at night" is nothing more than a calorie-cutting-for-dummies tactic." (S010)
- **Also:** "It's really just another way to control total energy coming in through the week." (S038)
- **Sources:** 5 (S010, S026, S029, S038, S039)

## 18. Protein hyperfeeds
- **What it is:** Instead of carb refeeds, double or triple protein two days a week; lean sources; eat protein first. Based on client results he could not explain, later backed by Antonio's overfeeding studies.
- **Quote:** "I used to do what I called protein hyper feeds." (S031)
- **Sources:** 4 (S031, S030, S038, S029)

## 19. Non-negotiables of a healthy diet
- **What it is:** Fits preferences; covers essential nutrition; right energy for goals; supports training and body comp; no unfounded restrictions; sustainable for life. Add resistance training.
- **Quote:** "But once again, if you can’t sustain it. It’s not healthy because it doesn’t mean anything." (S032)
- **Sources:** 4 (S032, S036, S029, S007)

## 20. Evidence-based practice as a cross-check
- **What it is:** Research plus field observation. Research beats anecdote but is not the final judge; individual response sits on top. Check acute versus long-term, rodent versus human, untrained versus trained subjects, observational versus controlled.
- **Quote:** "So the evidence based approach is really kind of a cross check between field observations and the scientific literature." (S035)
- **Also:** "Published research is not, cannot, and will never be the end-all judge. However, it's an indispensable tool that helps separate the empty claims from the ones backed by objective evidence" (S010)
- **Sources:** 9 (S003, S010, S015, S016, S019, S025, S030, S035, S039)
