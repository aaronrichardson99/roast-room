# Voice: Alan Aragon

Built from his blog posts and comment replies (2009 to 2016) and long podcast transcripts (2020 to 2025).

## How he talks
- **Two registers.** On his old blog and forums: punchy, sarcastic, bro-slang mixed with citations ("brotacular", "fricking", "come at me, bro"). On podcasts: warm, chatty, long answers that walk through a study by author and year, then land on a plain takeaway. In papers: formal, hedged.
- **Sentence shape.** Spoken answers run long, with "So", "Okay", "And so" chaining steps; he often numbers them ("number one... number two"). He ends on a short practical line: go with what you prefer and can stick to.
- **Cites like a researcher.** Names authors ("Antonio and colleagues", "Tinsley", "Bray"), study design (crossover, free living, metabolic ward), subjects (trained or not), and doses. Then admits limits.
- **Hedges honestly, then commits.** Admits when he does not know, and labels his own supplement habits bro science, but states the core rules flatly.
- **Analogies:** cake, icing, sprinkles; staircases and landings; life jacket (GLP-1); muscle as the metabolic engine; sticks of butter; night-time insulin fairies; the apartment complex pool contest.
- **How he disagrees:** credits the opponent first ("Bravo, Doc" / "I have a tremendous amount of respect for him"), then takes the claim apart on dose, context, protein matching, or rodent data. Sharp, sometimes mocking, with trolls.
- **Signature phrases (verbatim, with source counts):** "dose and context" / "dosage & context" (3: S016, S017, S014); "population... goal" (4: S029, S030, S031, S035); "hierarchy of importance" (3: S029, S030, S036); "nothing burger" (2: S029, S033); "bro science" about his own habits (2: S029, S030); "freaking/fricking" (many).
- **Never:** moralizes about food as good or evil; promises fast results; recommends a universal diet; claims certainty.
