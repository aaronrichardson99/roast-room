# Stories: Alan Aragon

Built from the notes on 38 sources (2009 to 2026). 15 stories and case studies he reuses, each with the lesson he draws.

1. **The Schoenfeld critique that became a partnership.** He emailed Brad Schoenfeld to tear apart his fasted cardio paper in AARR; Schoenfeld welcomed it and invited him to co-write the anabolic window review, which became the most viewed JISSN paper. Lesson: honest critique keeps science honest and earns allies. (S002, S006, S034)

2. **The 2013 Volek debate.** On stage at NSCA he pressed low-carb researcher Jeff Volek on a study he cited; Volek conceded protein was not matched. Lesson: check protein before believing a diet comparison. (S003)

3. **East African runners.** The athletes holding most distance records eat carb-heavy diets. Lesson: real-world performance argues against low-carb absolutism. (S003)

4. **The Lustig fructose fight.** His 2010 critique of Robert Lustig's "sugar is poison" lecture drew 400+ comments and three rounds with Lustig himself. Lesson: without dose and context, alarmism misleads. (S016, S017)

5. **Phillip and the gallon of milk ("fulking").** A client fat-bulked to 216 lb on a gallon of whole milk a day; at 176 lb he looked far better with five inches less waist. Lesson: body composition beats scale weight; more pounds is not better. (S013)

6. **Kelechi Opara's contest prep.** He prepped the natural bodybuilder with fruit and milk in the diet up to contest day. Lesson: bro-dogma food bans are not needed to get lean. (S012)

7. **Turning down the LA Kings.** Offered the full-time traveling nutritionist job, he declined to keep remote work and family time. Lesson: protect your own vision from shiny offers. (S009)

8. **Lyle's correction and the birth of AARR.** Lyle McDonald pushed him to write Girth Control, then caught an error in it; that pushed him to start a monthly research review in 2008 to stay current. Lesson: build a system that forces you to keep learning. (S020)

9. **Stone Cold Steve Austin's email.** Austin wrote that he had done his research and Aragon was the guy, asking for an exception to his no-new-clients rule, to get lean for a TV comeback. Lesson: results build a reputation that sells itself. (S029)

10. **Pam Greshock's last 8 pounds.** A perimenopausal, highly trained coach wanted to lose 8 lb from her midsection; he set the target at 1 to 2 lb a month and had her picture 32 sticks of butter. Lesson: set expectations by population and stakes; small losses are big. (S029)

11. **Protein hyperfeeds before the research.** He had clients double or triple protein two days a week and saw recomp he could not explain; Antonio's overfeeding studies later backed it. Lesson: field results can run ahead of the literature. (S031)

12. **100 days of whey and ice cream.** A friend lived on whey, Ben & Jerry's and some whiskey for 100 days and hit his macros, but felt terrible and could barely train. Lesson: macros are not the whole story; food quality matters. (S032)

13. **The fasted versus fed cardio trial.** With Schoenfeld he ran a study where he hand-built every subject's diet; fasted and fed groups lost the same fat and kept their lean mass. Lesson: choose by preference. (S030, S037)

14. **His wife's two clients.** One ignored coaching and lost 20 lb on Ozempic; the other followed the plan, lost about the same weight, but more fat, gained muscle and new habits. Lesson: skills beat drugs for the long run. (S037)

15. **Quitting alcohol.** From about 40 to 46 he drank a bottle to a bottle and a half of wine nightly, until his life "imploded"; he quit cold, fought insomnia, then lost about 25 lb of fat and gained muscle. He resists cravings by playing the night forward in his head. Lesson: when moderation fails, stop; redirect the habit-loving brain into training. (S029, S030, S038)

16. **"Except Brett."** Giving a talk on success he said there was no one he'd trade places with; Schoenfeld shouted from the audience that Brett Contreras was the exception. Lesson: success is loving what you do (told with a laugh). (S035)
