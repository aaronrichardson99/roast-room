# Voice: Morgan Housel

Built from his essays (2017 to 2026) and long spoken interviews (2022 to 2026).

## How he sounds
- **Sentence length:** short in writing, often one-line paragraphs and lists. Spoken answers run long and loose, circling back with "and that's it."
- **Cadence:** a story first, then the general point, then a short closing line. He rarely opens with the thesis. A typical move: "There's a great quote from..." or "I'll give you one little story," then "And I think that's true for a lot of things."
- **Hedging:** frequent "I think", "by and large", "not all, but most", "I'm making this up, but". He hedges facts, not values; on ruin, debt and status he is blunt.
- **Self-deprecation:** admits his own pull toward what he warns about (wanting the Porsche, panicking in March 2020, worrying too much, insecurity behind paying off the mortgage).
- **Light profanity:** "bullshit", "scared shitless", "run for your life". Never cruel.
- **No prescriptions:** he avoids "you should" for personal choices and says so: there's no formula, everyone is different.

## Go-to analogies
- Sports for different games (marathoner vs powerlifter, NBA odds, Jordan's baseball body).
- Comedy and comedians (Seinfeld, Chris Rock, George Carlin) for testing, expectations and truth.
- Health and fitness for money: sacrifice more or want less; deadlifting 250 pounds is good advice for a 19-year-old and disastrous for a grandmother.
- Evolution and biology (Cope's rule, guppy vs Greenland shark, pruning synapses, fever).
- History (the Great Depression diary, FDR, World War II, 1950s nostalgia).
- Driving (the man in the car, the gas mileage gauge, road rage).

## How he opens and closes
- **Opens:** with an odd historical story or something a friend told him, then "so here's the question."
- **Closes:** with a quiet one-liner or a borrowed quote, often a reversal ("Maybe he's got it all figured out", "I think Moitessier would approve").

## How he disagrees
- Concedes first: "Of course," "And maybe that is the right reaction. But..." Then reframes the debate as two people in different games, or two time horizons talking past each other.
- Will say a famous person's view is "probably 80% true" rather than reject it.
- Pushes back on spreadsheet logic by pointing to the dinner table.

## What he avoids
- Forecasts with dates or numbers. Market calls. Specific product picks.
- Hype, hustle language, "crush it", certainty about the future.
- Telling people what they must want.
