# Books: Morgan Housel

Three books. Book text was not fetched; these summaries are in my own words, built from his essays (several began as the chapters) and his interviews about each book.

## The Psychology of Money (2020)
- **Core thesis:** Doing well with money is about behavior, not intelligence. A janitor can beat a finance professional because temperament, patience and knowing what you want matter more than skill.
- **Key frameworks:** luck and risk as two sides of one coin; never enough and the moving goalpost; compounding with time as the exponent; getting wealthy vs staying wealthy; tails drive everything; freedom and control over your time as money's highest dividend; the man-in-the-car paradox; wealth is what you don't see; save money without a reason; reasonable vs rational; room for error; you will change, so avoid extremes; nothing is free (fees, not fines); play your own game; the seduction of pessimism.
- **Taught in sources:** the 2018 essay that grew into the book (S011), a posted excerpt (S017), and long book interviews (S001, S004, S010). He says it was written as short chapters out of desperation (S010) and contains no direct advice (S004).

## Same as Ever (2023)
- **Core thesis:** You cannot predict what will change, but you can be confident about human behavior that never changes: how people respond to greed, fear, risk, uncertainty and opportunity. Plan around those.
- **Key frameworks:** hanging by a thread (small flukes reshape lives); risk is what you don't see; expectations and reality; wild numbers (rare events happen all the time); best story wins; calm plants the seeds of crazy; too much, too soon, too fast; overnight tragedies and long-term miracles; tiny and magnificent (compounding both ways); it's supposed to be hard; keep running (why competitive advantages die); the wound heals but the scar remains; when the magic happens (stress and progress).
- **Taught in sources:** S002, S004, S006, S008, S009, S010, and essays that seeded chapters (S014, S018, S020, S029, S030, S047, S050).

## The Art of Spending Money (2025)
- **Core thesis:** There is advice on building wealth but little on using it. Spending is an art, not a science; money is either a tool for a better life or a yardstick of status, and many people aim for the first while chasing the second.
- **Key frameworks:** tool vs yardstick; money can use you; independence plus purpose; contentment over happiness; the deserted island test (status vs utility); reverse obituary; frugal vs independent; nice stuff vs fancy stuff; show off the inside of your house; savers who cannot switch to spending; try many kinds of spending to find your own.
- **Taught in sources:** his announcement (S062), the essays it grew from (S022, S023, S024, S025, S065), and interviews (S005, S007).
