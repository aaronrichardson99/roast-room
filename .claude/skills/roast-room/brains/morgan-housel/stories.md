# Stories: Morgan Housel

18 stories and cases he reuses, with the lesson he draws. Summaries in my words; source ids in parentheses.

1. **The second ski run.** At 17 he skied out of bounds at Squaw Valley with his best friends Brendan Allan and Bryan Richmond, caught a small avalanche, laughed, and on a whim declined a second run. They went again and died in a large slide. Lesson: the most important decision of his life took no thought; life hangs by a thread; tail consequences are all that matter; his risk tolerance dropped for good (S002, S004, S015).
2. **His father the ER doctor.** His parents were poor students, then his dad became a doctor and kept their old frugal habits. After 20 years of ER shifts he quit the day he had enough, while richer-living colleagues could not. Lesson: unspent money buys independence, which made his parents far happier than a bigger house would have (S001, S004).
3. **The valet and the man in the car.** Parking Ferraris at a luxury hotel in Los Angeles, he never once admired a driver; he imagined himself driving. Many drivers were not rich, just spending a paycheck on a lease. Lesson: no one admires you for your stuff; wealth is what you do not see (S001, S004, S010, S011).
4. **The Quiksilver founder in the Tacoma.** The kindest, wealthiest member at the hotel drove a silver Toyota Tacoma while the rude ones drove Rolls-Royces. Lesson: comfort with yourself shows as kindness; find the nicest person in the room (S007).
5. **Grace Groner and Richard Fuscone; Ronald Read.** A secretary left $7 million; a Merrill executive went bankrupt; a janitor left millions. Lesson: money success is behavior, not credentials. He regrets making Read look like a role model, since he would not want that life (S001, S004, S011, S023).
6. **Jesse Livermore.** Made a fortune shorting the 1929 crash, then levered up and lost it, four times over, and took his life. Lesson: getting rich and staying rich are different skills; success swells the head (S004, S016, S017, S019).
7. **Rick Guerin.** As smart as Buffett and Munger but "in a hurry", he used margin and was forced to sell in 1973 to 1974. Lesson: avoid ruin; patience compounds (S017).
8. **Buffett's age and the Snickers bar.** Most of Buffett's wealth came after 60 because he kept going for 80 years. In 2009 Buffett answered "how will we recover?" by noting Snickers was the best-selling bar in both the 1960s and now. Lesson: endurance; bet on what never changes (S001, S002, S004, S006).
9. **Buffett said he would not sell, then sold the airlines.** In early 2020 even Buffett acted differently once the reason for the fall was frightening. And Housel himself panicked and told his realtor to list the house that day. Lesson: you cannot forecast how you will feel under stress (S001, S008).
10. **Stephen Hawking.** Asked if he was always so cheerful, Hawking said his expectations were reduced to zero at 21. Lesson: happiness is results minus expectations (S004, S019, S030).
11. **The Vanderbilts and Biltmore.** The family built huge houses they barely used and blew the fortune in a few generations; the heirs seemed miserable, and Anderson Cooper, the first without the money, seemed happiest. Lesson: devotion to expense regardless of pleasure; money cannot buy a self (S001, S002, S012, S025).
12. **The anonymous $8 billion family.** Avoided publicity, gave anonymously, and raised polite, grounded kids. Lesson: rich and anonymous beats rich and famous; social debt is real (S002).
13. **Teaching his son to ski, and the ice cream.** Holding his son between his legs slowed his learning; later his shy son bought ice cream alone and beamed. Lesson: help by withholding help; doing it on your own terms feels far better (S002, S022).
14. **Crowhurst and Moitessier.** In the 1968 solo round-the-world race, Crowhurst faked his voyage for acclaim and died at sea; Moitessier, on track to win, quit and sailed to Tahiti because he was happy at sea. Lesson: your life is shaped by whom you want to impress (S002, S065).
15. **The client who just likes Europe.** An advisor's client refuses to hear about benchmarks; he only cares if he can keep traveling with his wife. Lesson: internal benchmarks (S001, S041, S065).
16. **Psychology of Money as a failure.** He gave up on long chapters and, out of desperation, used short blog posts; the publisher thought it would flop. Lesson: serendipity; use your best work again; do not take your own success as all skill (S010).
17. **Chris Rock at the Stress Factory.** Rock tests 40 or 50 shows in a small club before a tour, cutting what fails. Lesson: fail cheaply before the big stage (S050, S056).
18. **The top 10 richest men's divorces, and MySpace Tom.** Thirteen divorces among the ten richest; he would rather be Tom traveling with $50 million than Zuckerberg under pressure. Lesson: idolize skills, not lives; every outlier success has hidden costs (S004, S005, S010, S037).
