# Frameworks: Layne Norton

Built from 47 sources (2007 to 2025): his biolayne.com articles, a co-authored JISSN review, and long-form podcast transcripts. Scoring lines are guidance drawn from his own thresholds. Most-used first.

## 1. Adherence is the best diet *(unnamed)*
- **What:** Popular diets produce the same long-term loss on average; sorted by adherence, loss rises in a straight line. The best diet is the one the person will keep.
- **Use:** Any "which diet" question, any plan a client must live with.
- **Score (guidance):** Ask "can you do this for the rest of your life?" No means rethink. A plan that needs a big lifestyle change scores lower than one close to current habits.
- **Quote:** "the best diet for the individual is the one that they can consistently execute." (S005)
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S005, S011, S014, S027, S007.

## 2. Pick your restriction *(credits Peter Attia's three buckets)*
- **What:** Fat loss always needs some restraint: tracking (calories or macros), dietary (low carb, low fat, plant based), or time (fasting windows). None wins on adherence across a population.
- **Use:** Matching a method to a person's psychology.
- **Score:** Choose the one that feels least restrictive to this person; never assume what worked for you works for them.
- **Quote:** "you have to practice some form of restriction, but you should choose the form of restriction that feels the least restrictive for you." (S001)
- **Sources:** S001, S002, S005, S008, S011.

## 3. Calories in, calories out vs counting (the budget)
- **What:** Energy balance is the law; counting is one tool, like a budget is one tool for saving money. Labels and trackers are imprecise, but consistent tracking plus the scale reveals the true intake.
- **Use:** "Calories don't work for me", tracking objections.
- **Quote:** "people assume calories in, calories out is the same thing as counting calories. That's like saying the law that in order to save money, you have to earn more than you spend is the same thing as keeping a budget." (S005)
- **Quote:** "Just because you cannot track something exactly does NOT mean it's completely worthless to try." (S046)
- **Sources:** S005, S046, S020, S002, S004, S007.

## 4. TDEE components and NEAT
- **What:** TDEE = BMR (50 to 70%, mostly set by lean mass) + TEF (5 to 10%) + exercise + NEAT. NEAT is subconscious and falls in a diet, up to 400 to 500 kcal/day after a 10% loss. Deliberate steps are exercise, not NEAT.
- **Use:** Explaining plateaus; setting step floors.
- **Score:** If loss stalls, check steps first; Team Biolayne holds clients at their current step count and adds 15 to 30 minutes of cardio only when steps drift down.
- **Quote:** "NEAT is not something you can consciously modify." (S002)
- **Sources:** S002, S005, S046, S020, S011, S003, S004.

## 5. Metabolic adaptation, sized honestly
- **What:** Dieting slows BMR beyond what lost mass predicts, on average about 15%, with outliers near 35 to 40%. It builds over weeks or months and appears on every diet type. His 2022 view: BMR adaptation mostly happens early and recovers at maintenance; plateaus are mostly NEAT.
- **Use:** Answering "my metabolism is broken" and fad claims.
- **Quote:** "I used to be somebody who thought that BMR, metabolic adaptation was a big reason why people stopped losing weight or plateaued. And now, I think it's much more to do with NEAT." (S002)
- **Quote:** "All of a sudden, that deficit is no longer a deficit. That's now maintenance, right?" (S005)
- **Sources:** S002, S005, S024, S046, S050, S020, S003.

## 6. Weigh daily, judge the averages
- **What:** Daily weight is mostly fluid; weekly and monthly averages show tissue change. Fluctuations make people quit.
- **Use:** Monitoring any fat loss or gain phase.
- **Score:** Compare week averages; if the average stalls for weeks, re-establish the deficit. Skip daily weighing for people it makes anxious.
- **Quote:** "day-to-day changes in weight are much more dictated by fluctuations in fluid, but week-to-week and month-to-month averages reflect loss or gain of mass." (S005)
- **Quote:** "if you've plateaued, it means you have to reestablish the deficit." (S005)
- **Sources:** S002, S005, S007.

## 7. Rate of loss and the 1% cap
- **What:** Lose about 1% of body weight a week or less on average, slower as you get lean; diet on as many calories as you can. Obese beginners can go faster early because quick results buy adherence.
- **Use:** Setting deficits.
- **Score:** Consistent 3 to 4 lb/week in a lean person is too fast. Leaner means more lean mass lost per pound.
- **Quote:** "One percent of your bodyweight is a good cap to shoot for in terms of how fast you want to lose body fat." (S026)
- **Quote:** "the more adipose tissue you have, the more aggressively you can diet without negative consequences." (S002)
- **Quote:** "If you can lose your goal amount of body fat on 2500 calories a day, by God, do that. Don't do 1500." (S026)
- **Sources:** S026, S002, S050, S006.

## 8. Diet breaks, fat loss sprints, and holiday maintenance
- **What:** One to two weeks at maintenance during a cut may protect metabolic rate; his own cut was 2 to 3 week sprints at a 750 to 1,000 kcal deficit then at least as long at maintenance. Holidays go to maintenance. Use breaks only if the person likes them.
- **Quote:** "When you do one to two week phases at maintenance while you're cutting, it seems like it preserves your metabolic rate much better than if you're just doing a straight deficit." (S027)
- **Quote:** "diets and holidays tend to ruin holidays and diets." (S011)
- **Sources:** S027, S007, S011, S006, S050.

## 9. Reverse dieting (and how his view changed)
- **What:** Slowly adding calories after a diet to limit regain and raise maintenance. 2015 to 2018: framed as restoring metabolic rate ("metabolic repair"). 2023: the gains come mostly from activity and NEAT, not BMR; a tool, never required.
- **Quote (2018):** "in reverse dieting is just basically slow, controlled additions of calories to raise metabolic rate while hopefully minimizing body fat gain." (S024)
- **Quote (2023, newer):** "I have changed my tune on it. Okay, I don't think the changes in reverse and like an energy expenditure are from basic changes in basal metabolic rate." (S020)
- **Quote:** "Nobody thinks about the diet after the diet." (S026)
- **Sources:** S026, S024, S020, S027, S050.

## 10. Flexible dieting / IIFYM
- **What:** Any food can fit if it fits the calorie and macro budget, with protein, fiber and micronutrients covered first. Moderation breaks the restrict-binge cycle; treats are a poor buy on a small budget.
- **Score:** For a small dieter on 1,200 kcal, junk food is a bad use of funds; for a big active lifter it is fine.
- **Quote:** "In most cases, I saw a REDUCTION of calories from junk food intake by at least 30% over the course of a week when I allowed them to consume it in moderation but fit it into their macro targets vs. them having a 'cheat day' once per week." (S048)
- **Sources:** S048, S005, S001, S008, S003, S028.

## 11. Fat balance
- **What:** Fat balance = fat stored minus fat burned. High fat burns more and stores more; high carb burns less and stores less. With calories and protein equal, net fat loss is the same.
- **Quote:** "burning more fat isn't the same thing as losing more body fat because it only accounts for one side of the fat balance equation." (S044)
- **Sources:** S044, S046, S005, S001.

## 12. Accountability with empathy (the Biolayne way)
- **What:** Coaching needs both. Accountability alone makes a drill sergeant clients lie to; empathy alone gives no reason to change. After a slip: acknowledge, find the antecedent, add barriers and mindful moments, mirror back the client's own goals.
- **Quote:** "you have to have two very important things for effective coaching, empathy and accountability. They have to coexist." (S009)
- **Quote:** "Shaming yourself is not going to facilitate behavior change, okay?" (S004)
- **Sources:** S005, S009, S004, S015.

## 13. Mechanism vs outcome
- **What:** If there is an outcome there is a mechanism, but a mechanism does not guarantee an outcome. Go first to human trials measuring the thing you care about.
- **Quote:** "If there's an outcome, there will be a mechanism to support that outcome. But just because there's a mechanism doesn't mean there's an outcome." (S005)
- **Quote:** "getting focused on mechanisms is like looking at a mutual fund and getting focused on the individual stocks in it." (S005)
- **Sources:** S005, S003, S028, S037, S002, S004.

## 14. Evidence convergence and the hierarchy
- **What:** Meta-analyses with sensible inclusion criteria, then tightly controlled RCTs, then cohorts, animals, anecdote. Strong evidence means mechanism, animal data, dose response, RCTs and epidemiology all point one way. Apply the same logic to both sides.
- **Quote:** "you have to apply your logic symmetrically." (S003)
- **Quote:** "If you want to create a narrative, you can always find a study to create a narrative." (S003)
- **Sources:** S003, S047, S048, S028, S001.

## 15. Big rocks first
- **What:** Calories, protein, fiber, training, sleep, stress before seed oils, timing, supplements.
- **Quote:** "don't drop the big rocks to pick up the pebbles." (S005)
- **Sources:** S005, S001, S003, S028, S004.

## 16. Intensity is the medicine, hard sets are the dose
- **What:** Muscle grows from mechanical tension: sets within a few reps of failure, enough hard sets, muscles trained at long lengths. Progress load, then reps, then sets. Compounds within 2 to 3 reps of failure, isolation 1 to 2.
- **Quote:** "intensity is the medicine and the number of hard sets, which number of sets close to failure, is the dosage." (S005)
- **Quote:** "The research suggests you don't have to go to failure, but you have to get pretty darn close within a couple reps" (S005)
- **Sources:** S005, S003, S004, S007.

## 17. Stimulus to fatigue for strength
- **What:** Heavy top single, double or triple, then more sets of fewer, fast back-off reps. Failure's fatigue masks strength.
- **Quote:** "It's amazing what fatigue will mask." (S003)
- **Sources:** S003, S004, S007.

## 18. Protein hierarchy
- **What:** Total daily protein is the big lever (1.6 g/kg covers most; about 1 g/lb maxes it out for nearly everyone; more in a deficit); distribution is the last 3 to 10%; then source. Do not push protein so high that carbs and fat cannot fuel training.
- **Quote:** "The big rocks are about a gram per pound of body weight if you want to really, for all intents and purposes, max out the anabolic response, you're going to be fine." (S003)
- **Sources:** S003, S002, S004, S007, S026.

## 19. Slow gaining for trained lifters
- **What:** 100 to 200 kcal surplus; about 5 lb in 12+ weeks; trained people gain near 50:50 lean to fat; trim and repeat. Beginners: 2 to 4 lb a month. New lifters at high body fat: cut first.
- **Quote:** "if fat loss should be slow to retain lean body mass, then weight gain should be snail's pace." (S007)
- **Quote:** "I usually say, "When in doubt, cut."" (S027)
- **Sources:** S007, S027.

## 20. PHAT (Power Hypertrophy Adaptive Training), older program
- **What:** Each muscle twice a week: two power days (3 to 5 reps), three hypertrophy days (speed sets then 8 to 20 reps); deload every 6 to 12 weeks.
- **Quote:** "a combination of power and hypertrophy training over the long term is going to provide more muscle by increasing your growth potential!" (S039)
- **Sources:** S039.

## 21. Exposure therapy for pain
- **What:** Find a version of the lift that does not worsen pain, then progress one lever (depth, load, speed) at a time; manage stress and sleep.
- **Quote:** "the single biggest lever I've pulled to get me consistently training and pain-free was becoming more relaxed and less stressed out all the time" (S003)
- **Sources:** S004, S003, S018, S001.
