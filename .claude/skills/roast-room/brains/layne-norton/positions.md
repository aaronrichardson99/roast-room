# Positions: Layne Norton

Stances on fitness coaching topics, from 47 sources (2007 to 2025). Dates are source dates. Where he changed his mind, both views are shown and the newer is marked.

## Fat loss and dieting
- **Best diet:** None wins long term; adherence predicts loss. "the best diet for the individual is the one that they can consistently execute." (S005, 2024)
- **Low carb vs low fat:** Equal when calories and protein match; low carb is not better and not worse, so choose it if you like it (S008, 2022). "if you hate low carb, it doesn't matter what the study that came out said, you probably shouldn't do low carb." (S027, 2019) Two of six Carbon settings are low carb and keto (S008).
- **Intermittent fasting:** Works by creating a deficit. "if it is something that you can be consistent with and it helps you control your intake, it is a fabulous way for a lot of people to control their calorie intake and lose weight." (S005, 2024). Changed view: once worried about catabolism; by 2022, 16:8 with training and enough protein shows no lean mass penalty, though not ideal for someone chasing maximum muscle (S008, S003).
- **Rate of loss:** About 1% of body weight a week as a cap; slower when lean; obese clients can start faster for buy-in. "buy-in is huge for sustainability." (S002, 2022); (S026, 2015).
- **Metabolic adaptation:** Real, about 15% of BMR on average with outliers; newer view (2022) is that plateaus are mostly NEAT. (S002, S024)
- **Reverse dieting:** 2015 to 2018: restores metabolic rate (S026, S024). Newer (2023): works mostly through activity, and is optional. "I always tell people, I'm like, listen, who cares, it works. I've never said, like you have to reverse diet or anything like that." (S020)
- **Diet breaks and holidays:** Useful tools if the person likes them; holidays at maintenance (S027, S011).
- **Cheat days:** Moderated treats beat weekly cheat days. "When people were told they could not eat 'bad' foods while dieting except for their 'cheat days' they simply turned the cheat days or cheat meals into massive, all out binge eating sessions." (S048, 2022)
- **Snacking:** Maintainers rarely snack. "One big one, stop snacking." (S001, 2024)
- **Tracking:** A tool, not required; weigh everything for one week to learn portions. "For one week, weigh out every single thing you put in your mouth." (S001, 2024)
- **Self-reported "can't lose on 1,200":** People misjudge portions, not lie. "I don't think it's lying. I think that one, people don't really understand portion sizes." (S005, 2024)
- **Sugar:** Not fattening independent of calories; cut sugary drinks. "I would recommend avoiding sugar sweetened beverages simply because they have virtually no impact on satiety and just add easy to consume calories that are devoid of any other nutrients." (S048, 2022)
- **Diet soda:** A better step than regular soda. "there is no situation where it is not a net positive to take somebody who drinks sugar sweetened beverages and have them drink an artificially sweetened beverage." (S002, 2022). Changed slightly: sweeteners are "not inert" but effects unknown (S002, S003).
- **GLP-1 drugs:** Net positive with nutrition and lifting. "I'm pro on board with these. Just say it should be done in conjunction with nutritional counseling and lifestyle modification" (S005, 2024). "maybe some people just need some more training wheels than other people, okay?" (S003)
- **Obesity and blame:** Changed view from "lazy" to complex causes, still personal responsibility (S005, S001, S028).
- **Menopause:** BMR barely changes; sleep, stress and lower spontaneous activity explain most gain (S004, S003).

## Protein, supplements, food
- **Protein amount:** 1.6 g/kg covers most; about 1 g/lb maxes it out; more in a deficit. "So protein is the biggest lever. I would shoot for 1.6 grams per kilogram." (S002, 2022)
- **Protein distribution:** Older view: eat often (8 meals as a young lifter, S041). Newer: total first; two to four protein meals is the last few percent (S002, S003, S004).
- **BCAAs:** 2016 recommended 10 to 20 g/day (S038). Newer (2022): dropped from his products; whey beats them (S008).
- **Plant protein:** Changed: you can build muscle plant-based with planning and isolated protein (S002, S001).
- **Supplements:** Tier 1 creatine monohydrate, whey, caffeine. "creatine monohydrate, don't waste your money on anything else." (S005). "I would never tell people they need supplements." (S002)
- **Fiber:** The underrated lever. "if you want a longevity hack, I mean, fiber is kind of the longevity hack." (S002)
- **Seed oils and saturated fat:** Seed oils not harmful in substitution trials; keep saturated fat near 7 to 10% of calories (S002, S003, S028).
- **Processed food:** Problem is overeating; a tool for athletes with huge needs (S002).
- **Alcohol:** Fits if counted, but poor satiety per calorie (S003).

## Training
- **Volume:** Dose response with hard sets; about 6 to 10 hard sets per muscle per session (S007, S004). 2018: "We all know the most important metric of progressive overload is volume." (S043)
- **Failure:** Get close, rarely all the way on compounds. "For muscular hypertrophy, you need to get close to failure. But you probably don't need to train to failure to maximize hypertrophy, but you got to get pretty close." (S003). 2016 PHAT also warned against overusing failure (S039).
- **Frequency:** He grew more on 2x/week (S041, S039); newer: frequency mostly distributes volume (S007).
- **Rep ranges and exercise selection:** 1 to 30 reps work near failure; machines equal free weights; pick what the client enjoys and tolerates. "There is no hypertrophy rep range like people used to think about." (S003)
- **Periodization:** Changed view: DUP not superior to linear (S004).
- **Deloads:** Every 6 to 12 weeks in PHAT (S039); later, manage stimulus to fatigue so you can go longer between tapers (S007).
- **Cardio:** 2012 to 2015 favored HIIT (S042, S026). Newer: with work equated, no fat loss difference; he walks 10,000+ steps and does little formal cardio (S002, S003, S044).
- **Soreness:** Not a growth signal (S007, S021).
- **Beginners vs advanced:** Beginners grow on little and can recomp; trained lifters need slow surpluses and more volume (S007, S027).
- **Older adults:** Start now with machines and low pain; same relative gains. "Now is the perfect time to start." (S003)
- **Women:** Same relative muscle gain; very hard to get "too bulky" (S003, S002).
- **Pain and form:** Biopsychosocial; perfect-form beliefs increase pain; stress and sleep matter most (S004, S001, S003).

## Coaching and business
- **Client communication:** Accountability with empathy; mirror their goals; no shaming (S005, S009, S004).
- **What a coach is for:** "The real benefit to having a coach is somebody who's emotions are out of it." (S026, 2015)
- **Fitness industry:** "Anybody can call themselves a fitness coach. There's no barrier at all." (S005, 2024). Bad coaches survive on genetic elites (S028).
- **Online coaching business:** Started at $10 a week in grad school, full-time by graduation, about $1,000 a month at his peak, then built a $10/month app to reach people who cannot afford coaching (S017, S002, S005).
- **Partners:** Regrets investing too much in other people's companies (S023, 2018).
- **Social media:** Good content, consistent, collaborate (S019); debate for onlookers, not opponents (S004); misinformation always harms (S046).
- **Evidence vs anecdote:** RCTs over anecdote, but anecdote is low quality evidence, still evidence (S003).
- **Coach physique:** Not required, but people buy application; he sold less when heavier (S005).
- **Pricing, raising money, hiring:** no recorded position beyond the above.
