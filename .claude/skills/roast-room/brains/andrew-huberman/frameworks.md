# Frameworks: Andrew Huberman

Built from 43 sources (2021 to 2025): Huberman Lab solo episodes and Essentials, 14 Neural Network newsletters he wrote, and four long interviews (Lex Fridman, Tim Ferriss). 20 frameworks, most-used first. "Sources" counts distinct sources that use the idea. Names are his where he named them; otherwise a short name marked *(unnamed)*. The How-to-score lines are guidance for the council built from his stated thresholds, not quotes.

---

## 1. Behavior first, then nutrition, then supplements, then drugs (the tool ladder)
- **What it is:** His order of operations for any health or performance goal. Behavioral tools first because they change the brain through practice; then food; then supplements; then prescription drugs or devices, only if the earlier rungs fail.
- **When to use:** Every time someone asks "what should I take?" He answers by asking about sleep.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan fixes behavior (sleep, light, movement) before buying anything; 3 if it mixes; 1 if it leads with a pill, a stack, or a gadget.
- **Quote:** "So I always say behavioral tools first, then nutritional tools, then supplementation tools" (S008) "behavioral tools used over time engage neural plasticity." (S008) "I always believe that behavioral tools should come first. Behavioral tools should come first." (S006)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S002, S005, S006, S008, S023, S025, S031, S040)

## 2. The five foundations, re-upped every 24 hours
- **What it is:** Sleep, nutrients (with hydration), movement, sunlight, relationships (including with yourself). Each needs a deposit every day. Only after these does he discuss specific protocols.
- **When to use:** Any "I feel off", low energy, low mood, or burnout question. Audit the five before anything else.
- **How to score:** Count the foundations the decision protects. 5 = all five intact; 3 = it costs one for a short season; 1 = it costs sleep or relationships for months.
- **Quote:** "every 24 hours I need to invest something into each one of those things." (S039) "I consider viewing morning sunlight in the top five of all actions that support mental health, physical health and performance." (S032)
- **Sources:** 5 (S032, S039, S040, S011, S041)

## 3. Sleep as the foundation (and QQRT)
- **What it is:** Sleep is the base layer for mood, focus, willpower, and learning. QQRT, from his Matt Walker series: quantity (7 to 9 hours), quality (efficiency 85 percent or more), regularity (bed and wake within 30 minutes, weekends too), timing (match your chronotype).
- **When to use:** Any plan that adds hours, early starts, late nights, travel, or stress.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan keeps a fixed wake time and 7 or more hours most nights; 3 if it bends sleep for a short, bounded push; 1 if it treats sleep as the buffer.
- **Quote:** "sleep is THE foundation of our mental and physical health and performance in all endeavors." (S023) "Aim for consistent bedtimes and wake times with a ± 30 minutes margin of error, whether it's the weekend or a weekday." (S024) "Start with sleep. Figure it out. Figure out how to get your sleep right because it's the fundamental layer of mental health." (S040)
- **Sources:** 12 (S001, S002, S003, S008, S011, S012, S023, S024, S025, S039, S040, S041)

## 4. Morning light and the day's three phases
- **What it is:** View sunlight within 30 to 60 minutes of waking (10 min clear, 20 cloudy, 30 to 60 overcast) and again late afternoon; avoid bright light 10 pm to 4 am. Then split the day: Phase 1 (0 to 8 hours after waking) for hard, focused work and hard habits; Phase 2 (9 to 15 hours) for looser, creative work; Phase 3 (16 to 24) for rest and sleep.
- **When to use:** Scheduling: when to train, when to do deep work, when to have hard conversations, when to stop.
- **How to score:** 5 if the hardest thing sits early and the evening is dim and quiet; 3 if timing is mixed; 1 if hard work and screens run late every night.
- **Quote:** "View sunlight by going outside within 30-60 minutes of waking. Do that again in the late afternoon, prior to sunset." (S023) "Set 1-4 habits for completion in Phase 1" (S028) "We are not the same person across the different hours of the day, at least not neurochemically." (S033)
- **Sources:** 11 (S001, S002, S010, S012, S013, S023, S028, S032, S033, S040, S041)

## 5. Dopamine baseline versus peaks (the wave pool)
- **What it is:** Motivation depends on dopamine now relative to recent history. Big, frequent peaks (stacked stimulants, music, phones, constant rewards) drop the baseline after. Protect the baseline with sleep, light, cold, and movement; keep peaks rare.
- **When to use:** Loss of drive, phone and social media habits, pre-workout stacks, any "I need more to feel the same".
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan removes stacked sources and builds the baseline; 3 if it keeps one stacked habit; 1 if it chases a bigger high to fix a low.
- **Quote:** "Your experience of life and your level of motivation and drive depends on how much dopamine you have relative to your recent experience." (S004) "The peaks and the baseline are not independent of one another." (S005) "The worst thing you can do in the below-baseline phase is to try and spike your dopamine again with pharmacology" (S029)
- **Sources:** 8 (S004, S005, S008, S026, S029, S042, S037, S010)

## 6. Reward the effort itself, at random (the holy grail of motivation)
- **What it is:** Attach the feeling of winning to the effort, not the prize. Celebrate some wins, not all, on an unpredictable schedule (he suggests a coin flip). Blunt the big wins. Rewards given only after the work make the work harder next time.
- **When to use:** Long goals, plateaus, people who crash after achievements, people who hate the work but love the payoff.
- **How to score:** 5 if the person can enjoy the process and rewards are occasional; 3 if rewards are predictable; 1 if all motivation is the outcome.
- **Quote:** "The key is to celebrate your wins, but do not celebrate every win" (S026) "Even better, associate "winning" with the effort process itself. That's the holy grail of dopamine management for success." (S026) "working hard at something for sake of a reward that comes afterward, can make the hard work much more challenging and make us much less likely to lean into hard work in the future." (S004)
- **Sources:** 7 (S004, S005, S009, S026, S027, S042, S008)

## 7. Goal setting: one priority, moderate difficulty, 12 weeks, weekly review
- **What it is:** Pick one priority goal (one to three a year at most), hard but reachable, broken into specific actions with set days, hours, and time windows, run in 12-week blocks, reviewed weekly, and kept private at the start.
- **When to use:** New year plans, fitness goals, business goals, "I want to change everything at once".
- **How to score:** 5 if it is one goal, specific actions, a 12-week block, weekly check; 3 if two of those; 1 if it is several vague goals announced to everyone.
- **Quote:** "What is the one goal I can give the necessary time and energy to pursue right now?" (S027) "I'd suggest a 12-week time period is appropriate for most goals." (S027) "Set goals that are moderately hard to hard, but not so hard, nor so easy that they don't engage your brain and body properly." (S008) "So weekly seems like a good solid rule of thumb" (S008)
- **Sources:** 3 (S008, S009, S027)

## 8. Foreshadow failure
- **What it is:** Picture the win only at the start. To keep going, picture concretely how bad it will be if you fail. Fear is built into the goal circuit (amygdala). Not self-punishment, and not calling yourself a failure.
- **When to use:** Unmotivated days, long middles, people who rely on vision boards.
- **How to score:** 5 if the person can name the specific cost of quitting; 3 if they rely on the dream; 1 if they avoid thinking about failure at all.
- **Quote:** "there's a near doubling, near doubling in the probability of reaching one's goal if you focus routinely on foreshadowing failure." (S008) "Humans will work very hard to avoid feelings of failure." (S027) "It's not intended to be self-flagulation." (S009)
- **Sources:** 3 (S008, S009, S027)

## 9. Limbic friction and task bracketing (habits)
- **What it is:** Limbic friction is the activation energy to act when you are too anxious or too tired. Lower it by doing habits in the right phase of the day and within a window, so the brain predicts them (task bracketing). Linchpin habits you enjoy make others easier.
- **When to use:** Building or breaking habits, routines that collapse under stress.
- **How to score:** 5 if the habit has a set window and sits in the right phase; 3 if loose; 1 if it depends on feeling motivated.
- **Quote:** "Adjusting habits requires overcoming what I call "limbic friction" (energy to overcome anxiety, procrastination and/or fatigue)." (S028) "task bracketing is what underlies whether or not a habit will be context dependent or not." (S010) "those linchpin habits always, always, always are things that we enjoy doing." (S010)
- **Sources:** 2 (S010, S028)

## 10. 21 days, six habits, four of six, no compensation
- **What it is:** Pick six new habits, aim for four to six a day for 21 days, never make up a missed one, then 21 days of testing with nothing new to see what stuck. Breaking a habit: notice the slip, then do any positive act right after.
- **When to use:** Someone starting a big lifestyle change; someone who punishes themselves for misses.
- **How to score:** 5 if there is room to miss and no catch-up binges; 1 if one miss means quitting or doubling up.
- **Quote:** "Do not fret about only doing four out of six of the new habits per day, and never compensate by doing more than six per day" (S028) "this approach to forming habits is based not so much on the specific habits that you're trying to form, but the habit of performing habits." (S010)
- **Sources:** 2 (S010, S028)

## 11. Physiological sigh and real-time stress control
- **What it is:** Two inhales through the nose, one long exhale through the mouth; one to three to calm down fast. Exhale-heavy breathing lowers heart rate; inhale-heavy raises it. Five minutes of cyclic sighing daily lowered stress in his lab's trial. Panoramic vision also calms.
- **When to use:** Acute stress, before a hard talk, between sets, falling asleep.
- **How to score:** 5 if the person has a real-time tool and uses it in the moment; 1 if the only plan is a vacation.
- **Quote:** "cyclic physiological sighs, practiced daily for 5 minutes, reduce overall stress, promote relaxation, improve sleep, lower resting heart rate and enhance mood." (S030) "The best things you can do for stress involve controlling your stress response in real time." (S012) "when your mind isn't where you want it to be, use your body to control your mind." (S040)
- **Sources:** 7 (S007, S012, S030, S031, S040, S041, S001)

## 12. Short, medium, and long-term stress (sleep is the line)
- **What it is:** Short stress mobilizes energy, sharpens focus, and helps immunity. It turns harmful when it lasts and costs sleep. The test of whether stress is a problem: can you still sleep and be at peace?
- **When to use:** Taking on a hard season, a deadline, a demanding relationship.
- **How to score:** 5 if the stress is bounded and sleep holds; 3 if bounded but sleep slips; 1 if it is open-ended and sleep is already broken.
- **Quote:** "When you are no longer able to achieve good sleep, you are now moving from acute stress to chronic stress." (S007) "Stress isn't bad if you can sleep well at night, you can be stressed all day, go, go, go, go, go, go, go." (S038) "stress is fantastic for us, except when it interferes with our sleep" (S018)
- **Sources:** 6 (S006, S007, S012, S018, S035, S038)

## 13. Cortisol rhythm and two kinds of burnout
- **What it is:** Cortisol should peak in the hour after waking and stay low in the 4 hours before sleep and the first 2 hours of sleep. Burnout comes in two patterns: wired in the morning and exhausted later, or tired in the morning and wired at night. Fix the rhythm at the end that is wrong.
- **When to use:** Burnout, "wired and tired", morning anxiety, afternoon crashes.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan restores a bright, active morning and a dim, calm evening; 1 if it adds caffeine and late work.
- **Quote:** "Cortisol is not a stress hormone per se." (S012) "the first pattern of burnout is super stressed in the morning, exhausted in the afternoon, evening and at night. Second pattern of burnout, stressed at night, exhausted in the morning." (S012)
- **Sources:** 1 (S012), with rhythm support in S023, S032

## 14. Willpower as a trainable circuit (micro-sucks)
- **What it is:** The anterior mid-cingulate cortex grows when you do safe things you do not want to do, or resist things you want. Willpower rides on sleep, pain, and distraction, and on what you believe about it.
- **When to use:** Discipline questions, "I have no willpower", building toughness without injury.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan adds small, safe hard things on top of good sleep; 3 if hard things but no recovery; 1 if it expects willpower while sleep-deprived.
- **Quote:** "You want to do something, you resist doing it. That's building up tenacity and willpower." (S011) "if you're not taking care of the foundational modulators of tenacity and willpower, none of the subsequent tools and protocols that we will discuss are going to help you that much over time." (S011)
- **Sources:** 3 (S011, S036, S022)

## 15. NSDR (non-sleep deep rest)
- **What it is:** His coined term for yoga nidra, hypnosis, and similar 10 to 30 minute protocols. Used when short on sleep, for an afternoon dip, after learning, or to fall back asleep.
- **When to use:** Busy people who cannot sleep more; late-night wakings.
- **How to score:** Use as a fix for a bad night, never as the plan.
- **Quote:** "Non-sleep deep rest, or NSDR, is an acronym that I coined" (S041) "I would never want anyone to try and use NSDR as a total replacement for sleep" (S006)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S002, S005, S006, S014, S037, S040, S041)

## 16. 90-minute deep work blocks
- **What it is:** One or two (he says up to three) focused bouts of up to 90 minutes a day, early, phone off, with a warm-up and a cool-down. Focus beats hours logged. Defocus on purpose too.
- **When to use:** Work hours, side projects, study.
- **How to score:** 5 if the day protects at least one undisturbed block; 1 if the plan is more hours with the phone on.
- **Quote:** "one or two of these really deep focused 90 minute work blocks are about what one schedule and even mind can handle." (S041) "It's not even so much about how many hours you log. It's really about focus in the moment." (S038)
- **Sources:** 5 (S006, S037, S038, S041, S009)

## 17. Visual focus as a lever (goal line and space-time bridging)
- **What it is:** Hold your gaze on one point for 30 to 60 seconds to raise alertness before work; narrow gaze lowers perceived effort. Space-time bridging steps attention from inside the body out to the horizon and back, three breaths at each station.
- **When to use:** Starting hard work, long efforts.
- **Quote:** "most of your cognition follows your visual perception." (S008) "The vision and our visual system is perhaps the strongest lever by which we can shift our state of mind and body." (S040)
- **Sources:** 4 (S008, S009, S027, S040)

## 18. Stress-is-enhancing plus growth mindset
- **What it is:** Belief changes physiology. Learning that stress can help improves performance under stress; praising effort, not identity, improves learning. Combined, they work better than either.
- **When to use:** Fear before a challenge; people who label themselves.
- **Quote:** "how we think about stress impacts how we react to stress." (S018) "Identity-label feedback actually undermines effort and results in the long run!" (S035)
- **Sources:** 4 (S018, S035, S011, S042)

## 19. Natural versus synthetic happiness, and prosocial giving
- **What it is:** Natural happiness comes from getting things; synthetic happiness from making the best of choices you made. Both take effort; presence is the shared lever. Giving money away predicts happiness more than the amount received.
- **When to use:** Money, ambition, "I will be happy when".
- **Quote:** "money cannot buy happiness, but it certainly can buffer stress." (S015) "the manner in which they spent that bonus was a more important predictor of their happiness than the size of the bonus itself." (S015)
- **Sources:** 3 (S015, S042, S034)

## 20. Expressive writing and gratitude by story
- **What it is:** Pennebaker protocol: 15 to 30 minutes, the same hard event, on four occasions; build a coherent narrative. Gratitude works best by receiving thanks or recalling a true story of someone being helped.
- **When to use:** Old wounds that still drive behavior; low mood.
- **Quote:** "your narrative about those events is vitally important in terms of how you experience either ongoing distress from or relief from those events." (S017) "the most potent form of gratitude practice is not a gratitude practice where you give gratitude or express gratitude, but rather where you receive gratitude" (S020)
- **Sources:** 4 (S017, S020, S034, S040)
