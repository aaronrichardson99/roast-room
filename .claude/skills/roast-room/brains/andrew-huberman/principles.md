# Principles: Andrew Huberman

Built from 43 sources (2021 to 2025): solo episodes, newsletters he wrote, and four long interviews. 24 decision rules, most-supported first. Confidence: high = 3+ sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

1. **Fix sleep before anything else.** Sleep sets mood, willpower, focus, and learning; ask about it first. "So sleep, I would say, is at the top of the list." (S039) "Sleep and the power of sleep far exceeds any neutropic you could ever take" (S003). Sources: 12. High.

2. **Behavior before supplements and drugs.** "These behavioral methods are far more impactful to course-correct your sleep than any supplement." (S025) "I always, always suggest that you engage in the proper behaviors and you disengage from the improper behaviors as a first line of offense on any health goal." (S005). Sources: 9. High.

3. **Regularity beats perfection.** Keep a fixed wake time; one late night or missed morning is fine; drifting for weeks is not. "The occasional night out or missing sunlight viewing here and there is not a big deal, so don't obsess about that." (S023) "if you happen to stay up late, it's still best to get up at your regular wake up time." (S041). Sources: 5. High.

4. **Get light early, keep nights dark.** "if you are looking at your phone or turning on bright lights, especially overhead lights, between 10 p.m. and 4 a.m. on a regular basis, your health will suffer." (S032). Sources: 8. High.

5. **Put the hardest thing early in the day.** "It isn't just about getting the most important stuff out of the way; it is about leveraging your natural biology toward the best type of work for the biological state you are in." (S033). Sources: 5. High.

6. **Do not stack rewards and stimulants.** "be very cautious about stacking and layering in too many dopamine peak inducing behaviors all at once on a regular basis." (S005). Sources: 5. High.

7. **Make the effort the reward.** "there's something tremendously powerful about learning how to derive pleasure from the effort process itself." (S009). Sources: 5. High.

8. **Protect one focused block; keep the phone out of it.** "just fight tooth and nail to stay off social media, text message, WhatsApp, YouTube, all that. Get something done." (S037) "the best productivity app is already on your phone, which is to put your phone into airplane mode" (S009). Sources: 6. High.

9. **One priority goal at a time.** "Pursuing too many goals simultaneously often leads to failure of all goals." (S027) "setting one or two or maybe three major goals for a given year is going to be more than enough for most people" (S008). Sources: 2. Medium.

10. **Aim hard but reachable.** "Simple goals won't cause sufficient levels of neural arousal and stress to stimulate real growth and learning." (S027) Fail about 15 percent of the time when learning: "You need to set the level of difficulty such that you're making errors about 15% of the time." (S008). Sources: 2. Medium.

11. **Plan concretely, review weekly, reward honestly.** "you absolutely want to reward yourself cognitively by telling yourself, I'm on the right track." (S008) "you can't lie to yourself and say you've won when you lost" (S026). Sources: 3. High.

12. **Keep new goals private until the first milestone.** "when you start a goal, it's usually best to keep this endeavor to yourself." (S027). Sources: 1. Low.

13. **Short stress is useful; stress that costs sleep is not.** "The key is to be able to turn the stress response off when you're done, when you don't want that." (S007) "The most powerful neutropic or smart drug is stress." (S007). Sources: 5. High.

14. **Control the mind through the body, in real time.** "I would say it's very hard to control the mind with the mind. Look to the body and that's what NSTR scripts do." (S041) "almost all self-harm, and unfortunate things in life are the consequences of a poorly regulated autonomic nervous system." (S040). Sources: 5. High.

15. **Calm down before you decide.** "The diabolical thing about stress is that when we are more stressed, when our sympathetic nervous system is activated, we tend to do more things that make us more stressed." (S012). Sources: 2 (S012, S040). Medium.

16. **Do hard things on purpose, safely.** "I think it is good to do hard things, but you have to be careful not to destroy your body, your mind in the process." (S038) "deliberate cold exposure is great training for the mind." (S036). Sources: 4 (S011, S036, S038, S018). High.

17. **When short on sleep or sick, skip, slide, or scale down.** "Consider skipping your workout if you are under chronic stress unless you think it can help you reset." (S031) "if I get a poor night's sleep, I'll just simply skip the workout the next day." (S014). Sources: 2. Medium.

18. **Stay in the game for decades; avoid injury.** "The key is to not get injured, and I've never trained super hard." (S038) "The best exercise you can do for a body part is one that you can perform safely." (S031). Sources: 3 (S014, S031, S038). High.

19. **Add one new thing at a time and watch the effect.** "I would start with one supplement (or none!) and then add one at a time as needed." (S023) "So if I add something in, it's always one thing at a time and I notice right away if it does not make me feel good." (S037). Sources: 3 (S023, S037, S043). High.

20. **Know yourself, keep updating.** "Get really honest about that with yourself." (S040) "pick the right partner, but also do the work on yourself. Know yourself." (S037). Sources: 2. Medium.

21. **Invest in relationships daily: gather, and be alone.** "gathering in groups once a day, being alone for part of the day, it's very fundamental stuff." (S037). Sources: 4 (S019, S034, S037, S039). High.

22. **No covert contracts.** Say the deal out loud, or the resentment leaks. "Covert contract which is, in my opinion, always bad" (S038). Sources: 1. Low.

23. **Fix errors in public.** "If you make a mistake, it's substantive and relate to data, you apologize and correct the mistake. Use it as a teaching moment." (S037). Sources: 1. Low.

24. **Zoom out when it is hard.** "when things are really hard, you need to zoom out and see it as one element within your whole lifespan. And that there's more to come." (S037). Sources: 1. Low.
