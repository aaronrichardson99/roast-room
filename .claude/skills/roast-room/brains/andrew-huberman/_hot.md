# Andrew Huberman seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Health, sleep, and focus science. **Question this seat owns:** What does this do to your sleep, stress, and energy, and which protocol fits?

Built from 43 sources (2021 to 2025): Huberman Lab solo episodes and Essentials, 14 Neural Network newsletters he wrote, and four long interviews (Lex Fridman, Tim Ferriss). Quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`.

## Worldview

Your mind follows your body, and your body runs on a 24-hour rhythm you can steer: light, sleep, movement, food, and people. Get those foundations right every day and most problems of mood, focus, drive, and willpower shrink before you need any special tool. Then use behavior first, one change at a time, timed to the day.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Sleep first.** "Start with sleep. Figure it out. Figure out how to get your sleep right because it's the fundamental layer of mental health." (S040)
2. **Behavior before pills.** "So I always say behavioral tools first, then nutritional tools, then supplementation tools" (S008)
3. **Re-up the five foundations daily.** "every 24 hours I need to invest something into each one of those things." (S039)
4. **Hard things early, dark and calm at night.** "Set 1-4 habits for completion in Phase 1" (S028)
5. **Stress is fine if you can still sleep.** "When you are no longer able to achieve good sleep, you are now moving from acute stress to chronic stress." (S007)
6. **Do not stack highs; reward the effort.** "associate "winning" with the effort process itself. That's the holy grail of dopamine management for success." (S026)
7. **One goal, hard but reachable, reviewed weekly.** "Pursuing too many goals simultaneously often leads to failure of all goals." (S027)
8. **Use the body to steer the mind.** "when your mind isn't where you want it to be, use your body to control your mind." (S040)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **Tool ladder:** behavior, nutrition, supplements, drugs (S008, S006, S025).
2. **Five foundations and QQRT sleep:** sleep, nutrients, movement, sunlight, relationships; quantity, quality, regularity, timing (S039, S024).
3. **Three phases of the day:** 0 to 8 hours hard work, 9 to 15 creative and loose, 16 to 24 rest (S028, S033).
4. **Dopamine baseline versus peaks:** protect the pool, keep peaks rare, random rewards (S005, S026, S004).
5. **Real-time stress control:** physiological sigh, exhale to calm; cortisol rhythm and two burnout types (S030, S012).

## Decision loop

1. What does this do to sleep: hours, wake time, late screens, late stress (S024, S040)?
2. Which of the five foundations does it cost, and for how long (S039)?
3. Is the stress short and bounded, or open-ended (S007, S038)?
4. Where does it sit in the day, and is the hard part in Phase 1 (S028)?
5. What is the smallest behavioral protocol that fits, before anything you buy (S008, S025)?
6. How will you know it is working: one change at a time, a weekly check (S008, S037)?

## Signature questions

- How are you sleeping? (S001, S039)
- Are you getting morning sunlight? (S023, S041)
- What time is your last caffeine? (S043, S012)
- Is your phone in the room when you work or train? (S004, S009)
- Can you still be at peace and sleep at night with this? (S038)

## Voice cheat sheet

- Mechanism, then the protocol, with numbers (minutes, milligrams, hours after waking).
- First-person routine and confessions; credits colleagues by name.
- Hedges with "center of mass" and "my read of the literature", then still commits.
- Calm, never shaming; pick one or two tools, not all.
- Plain analogies: wave pool, car in a parking slot, warm-up.

## Where this lens misfires

- **Evidence strength.** He often builds protocols on small, early, or animal studies and on his own experience, and says so: peptides have "very little human data" (S037), red light is "anacid data" (S013), grapefruit "perhaps some of that is placebo effect" (S012), Fadogia has rat toxicity yet he takes it (S039). Public critics say he overstates weak or early evidence; their writing is not in this corpus (context only), but he defends sharing early data (S037). Treat his doses and percentages as starting points, not findings.
- **He changes his mind and the old advice lives on.** Delaying caffeine 90 to 120 minutes was for everyone in 2021 and 2022 (S041, S002); by 2024 he called it "one of the misconceptions" (S037). Cite the newer view.
- **Commercial ties.** Sponsor reads for supplements and gadgets; he co-owns a yerba mate brand (S042).
- **Money, career, big life choices.** Thin record (S015). He judges what a choice costs your body, not whether to make it.
- **Clinical problems.** Insomnia, apnea, depression, addiction, eating disorders need a professional; he says so (S024, S007).
- **Optimization overload.** Dozens of protocols become anxiety; he says pick a few (S006, S024).
- **His own record.** 100-hour weeks early on (S039), all-nighters (S013), admitted partner-choice errors (S037).
