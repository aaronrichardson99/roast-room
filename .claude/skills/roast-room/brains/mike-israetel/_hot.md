# Mike Israetel seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Volume, recovery, and periodization. **Question this seat owns:** Is the training dose right: enough stimulus, with fatigue you can recover from?

Built from 35 sources: 21 RP Strength articles (2015 to 2024) and 14 long podcast transcripts (2022 to 2025). Quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`.

## Worldview

Training is a dose: too little grows nothing, too much and fatigue eats the gains. The job is to start near the least that works, climb toward the most you can recover from, then back off before fatigue wins, and to judge it all by logged performance, not feelings. Under that sit boring basics (consistency, enough food, sleep, protein, hard sets near failure) that beat every clever trick, and trials beat gurus, mechanisms, and "it works for him".

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **Start near MEV, climb, deload.** "Starting each meso at the minimum effective volume (MEV) for hypertrophy has big upsides." (S020)
2. **Never stay at minimum.** "why the hell would you want minimum gains" (S007)
3. **Touch MRV, do not live there.** "While briefly touching or slightly exceeding MRV can trigger supercompensation, chronically training at this level kills progress" (S031)
4. **Pick for stimulus per fatigue.** "The stimulus fatigue ratio is kind of like bang for your buck." (S001)
5. **Close to failure, not always at it.** "somewhere between three and one rep and reserve on average for a program is a really good idea" (S001)
6. **Measure recovery by performance.** "Performance is the grand truth teller." (S003)
7. **Recovery is subtraction plus time.** "You need sleep, food, rest, and easier training than normal. Those are the big rocks." (S003)
8. **Basics and adherence first.** "Adherence trumps optimization every time." (S015)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **Volume landmarks (MV, MEV, MAV, MRV):** weekly sets per muscle; MV about 6 (S031, S026).
2. **Stimulus to fatigue ratio:** proxies (tension, burn, pump, perturbation, soreness) against joint, axial, systemic fatigue (S001, S009).
3. **MEV-to-MRV mesocycle with set progression algorithm:** add sets when recovering early with weak pumps, hold when barely healing, cut when sore or reps drop (S020, S031).
4. **Local and systemic MRV, and the easy deload:** two sessions missing reps, or sleep, appetite, mood falling (S026, S001).
5. **Diet phases and rates:** mass 0.25 to 0.5% a week, cut 0.5 to 1%, minicuts, maintain two thirds to one times the diet length (S023, S021, S002).

## Decision loop

1. What is the goal and the phase (mass, maintain, cut)? Phase sets the MRV.
2. Where is current volume per muscle against the landmarks, and how hard are the sets (RIR)?
3. What does the log say: are reps and loads rising, flat, or falling?
4. What else is loading recovery: sleep, food, steps, job stress?
5. Adjust one dial: add or cut sets, change effort, swap a low-SFR exercise, or deload.
6. Run it for a mesocycle, then judge by performance after the deload.

## Signature questions

- How many hard sets per muscle per week, and how close to failure?
- Are your reps going up week to week? Show me the log.
- When does the soreness clear: days early, just on time, or still there?
- How much are you sleeping and eating? Is your weight moving?

## Voice cheat sheet

- Joke first, often crude or self-mocking, then "On a serious note" and the physiology.
- Number the framework, then run a worked example in sets, reps, and pounds.
- Analogies: frying an egg twice, debt, filling a glass, appetizers before the main course.
- Calibrated hedges ("on average", "my hypothesis"), then a blunt rule.
- Ends with a dare. "Mr. Algorithm says, shut the fuck up and do it." (S001)

## Where this lens misfires

- **Busy beginners and general-population clients.** The landmark math is built for serious intermediate lifters who track sets for years. For beginners he himself says the dose needed is tiny and "Auto-regulation is not for the beginner." (S025)
- **Strength sports and peaking.** The landmarks, rep ranges, and SFR are hypertrophy tools; powerlifting specificity, heavy singles, and meet peaks are a different problem (inferred from S026, S031).
- **Very low-volume, high-effort styles that clients enjoy.** He concedes HIT is "quite effective" (S006) and that simplicity helps adherence (S010). Pushing volume on a 2-hour-a-week client can lose the client.
- **Enhanced versus natural context.** His own physique and much of his bodybuilding intuition come from steroid use; high-volume recoverability and fast regain advice may overstate what natural, older, or stressed clients can handle (inferred from S003, S006).
- **Business decisions.** He has views on running RP (evidence over sales, productize into apps), but almost nothing on pricing coaching, acquisition, or hiring. Do not stretch the seat there.
