# Positions: Mike Israetel

Built from 35 sources (21 RP articles 2015 to 2024, 14 podcast transcripts 2022 to 2025). 30 fitness-coaching topics. Dates are article dates or episode dates from `sources.md`. Where his view moved, both versions sit side by side. Topics with no evidence say so.

## Training volume
- **Stance:** Volume is the main dial for hypertrophy. Start near MEV, climb toward MRV, deload. About 5 to 8 hard sets per muscle per session is the average sweet spot; weekly targets per muscle come from the landmarks.
- 2017-01: "MV is surprisingly low ... typically around 6 working sets per muscle group per week when training each muscle at least twice weekly" (S031)
- 2024-02: about 5 to 8 sets per muscle per session as the meta-analytic best guess; past that is junk volume. "It's like frying an egg after it's already fried." (S001)
- 2024-04: open to very high volumes when systemic fatigue allows: "you can do 30, 40, or 50 sets per muscle per week for weeks on end and get better gains than if you did 10, 20, 30 sets" (S010)
- **Read:** the newer view is more volume-tolerant than the 2017 landmarks; he still caps per-session volume.

## Adding sets within a mesocycle (the debate with Eric Helms)
- **Stance:** Add sets week to week inside the meso when recovery allows; treat sets like load, reps and RIR. Written as a reply to Eric Helms and Brian Minor, who argued for adding sets only between mesos.
- 2020-07: "allowing load, reps, and RIR to change within the meso but categorically leaving volume changes off limits during the meso seems arbitrary" (S020)
- He welcomed the debate: "This contention is excellent, as it sparks discussion and moves the community closer toward the truth." (S020)

## Frequency
- **Stance:** Train a muscle again when performance is back; 2 to 4 times a week for most; 2 is plenty for health.
- 2024-02: "Muscles just don't take that long to recover." (S001)
- 2024-01: "so long as you’re recovered to train again (can perform at or above normal levels), training is a better idea than waiting to train" (S026)

## Intensity, failure and RIR
- **Stance:** Go close to failure, average 1 to 3 RIR, reach failure at the end of a meso, test failure now and then so RIR is honest.
- 2024-02: "somewhere between three and one rep and reserve on average for a program is a really good idea" (S001)
- 2025-02: "However, the fatigue of true failure training, probably mostly because of that nervous system component, is exponentially higher." (S006)

## HIT, Mentzer, and one-set training
- **Stance:** Effective and simple, but not optimal for anyone past the first weeks; the failure dogma is wrong.
- 2024-04: "research study after study after meta regression, after meta analysis shows that training to failure is at best an equivalent method to get to muscle size and at worst an inferior method" (S010)
- 2025-02 on low-volume heavy training like Dorian Yates: "It's not the most efficient or the most effective way to train, but it is quite effective" (S006)

## Rep ranges and load
- **Stance:** 5 to 30 reps near failure all grow muscle; mix ranges; compounds heavy, isolations light.
- 2024-02: "anywhere between sets of five and sets of 30 is kind of unlikely to be a ton of really wrong answers" (S001)

## Exercise selection
- **Stance:** Anything that hits the muscle through a deep stretch with good SFR. Few exercises per week, kept for as long as they keep working.
- 2024-02: "there is no one right exercise." (S001)
- 2024-01: "If you’re doing an exercise, there should be a reason for it." (S026)
- Deadlifts: poor SFR for any one muscle (S001), but fine for beginners with good technique (S008, 2024-06).

## Range of motion and partials
- **Stance:** Mostly full ROM with the deep stretch prioritized; lengthened partials can beat full ROM; top-end partials are ego.
- Changed emphasis: earlier he simply said full ROM. 2015-01: lists "Full ROM lifting" among the basics (S034). 2024-02: "I used to say full range of motion." (S001) Now the stretch matters most.
- 2022-10: "Bottom end leg extensions have actually been shown in the scientific literature, at least in one study, to work better than full range leg extensions and way better than top end partials." (S009)

## Periodization and deloads
- **Stance:** Mesocycles of accumulation until MRV, then an easy deload; blocks of mesos; active rest a couple of weeks about twice a year.
- 2024-01: "This makes the deload VERY EASY, which is the whole point, since hard training doesn't bring down fatigue!" (S026)
- 2024-09: "every six months or so, many kinds of athletes should take an active rest phase, which is ... two weeks in length" (S003)

## Recovery modalities (cold, sauna, massage, NSAIDs)
- **Stance:** They mask, they do not recover; cold and NSAIDs can blunt growth. Sauna is fine if you enjoy it.
- 2024-09: "If you take a shitload of NSAIDs and do a bunch of cold plunges, yeah, you'll feel much ... better very soon, but you didn't grow any muscle." (S003)

## Sleep
- **Stance:** The base of everything; 7 to 9 hours for most.
- 2024-02: "If you are chronically under slept, I actually don't need to hear about your program or your ... diet." (S001)

## Cardio and steps
- **Stance:** Cardio is a calorie tool. Steps (about 10,000 a day) beat formal cardio for most dieters. Low intensity first; HIIT at most 3 to 4 sessions a week. High daily activity also eats recovery.
- 2016-11: "Low Intensity Cardio is STILL KING in Bodybuilding" (S022)
- 2024-07: "Cardio is best seen as I need to burn a certain number of calories to get ... me to that moderate high level." (S002)
- 2024-09: "it turns out that daily physical activity contributes substantially to fatigue" (S003)

## Fat loss rate and diet length
- **Stance:** Moderate pace, shorter diets, then maintenance.
- 2016-11: cut at about 0.75% bodyweight a week for 8 to 12 weeks (S022).
- 2020-07: "Avoid the extremes of weight loss pace. Aim for ~0.5-0.7% body weight lost per week." (S021) and "running two six week diets might be best choice to increase chances of completing the goal" (S021)
- 2024-07: "something like two thirds to one times the length of the previous diet is how long your maintenance phase or slow bulk should be after" (S002)

## Calories and counting
- **Stance:** Calories rule fat loss. Counting is optional while it works and the fix when it does not.
- 2024-07: "It is no more mandatory to count calories in order to lose fat than it is to count your money in order to become wealthy." (S002)

## Muscle gain rate (bulking)
- **Stance:** Slow and steady, about 0.25 to 0.5% bodyweight a week; minicut when bodyfat creeps up; no dirty bulks.
- 2016-12: "the best post-cut massing plan is to go slow and steady, about ½ percent of your body weight per week for around 2 months" (S030)
- 2017-10: "minicuts re-sensitize the body to further muscle gains" (S023)

## Protein
- **Stance:** About 1 g per lb for serious lifters; just over 0.5 g per lb covers most people's health needs. No kidney risk without kidney disease. The 30 g per meal ceiling is a myth for mixed meals.
- 2017-11: "getting “too much” protein is not a concern within most ranges for healthy adults" (S029)
- 2024-07: "Gram per pound is an amazing rule." (S002)
- 2025-03: "they can fully utilize for muscle growth, 100 grams of protein ... at a time" (S012)

## Carbs versus fats
- **Stance:** For lifters, carbs over fats, especially when massing. For general fat loss, preference once protein and calories are set.
- 2023-01: high-carb low-fat beats surplus fats and low-carb for muscle gain (S015).
- 2024-07: carbs and fats are interchangeable after protein for most dieters; an even split is most flexible (S002).

## Seed oils and food quality
- **Stance:** Seed oils are fine or good per RCTs; most fats from plants; aim about 75% of intake from whole foods.
- 2019-09: 75/25 healthy to fun food split (S024).
- 2024-07: "Inference doesn't beat the real thing." (S002)

## Supplements
- **Stance:** Creatine works; protein powder is food; almost nothing else matters next to sleep, stress, lifting, and activity. No recovery supplements.
- 2024-09: "There are no recovery supplements that I know of." (S003)
- About 2025: "creatine loading is a gigantic waste of time in ... almost every case" (S035)

## Diet adherence and simplicity
- **Stance:** Simple, repetitive meals, batch cooking, bland food late in a hard cut, routines.
- 2016-11: "If you like your life like a machine (at least when in serious fat loss mode, not all the time for mental health!), you get machine-like results." (S022)
- 2020-01: "If you meet your macros and the meal is tasty enough to eat, you’re good!" (S028)
- 2023-01: "Adherence trumps optimization every time." (S015)

## Beginners versus advanced
- **Stance:** Beginners grow from almost anything, need very little volume, can go months without a deload, and should not autoregulate on feel. Advanced lifters have narrow MEV-MRV windows and deload every 3 to 6 weeks.
- 2016-08: "Auto-regulation is not for the beginner." (S025)
- 2020-07: "The more advanced you are, the more your MEV-MRV window narrows" (S020)
- About 2025: "the minimal ... effective dose is profoundly small, which is why I can say things like if you work out for 20 minutes twice a week, you're going to get great gains" (S035)

## Time off and muscle memory
- **Stance:** Lost muscle comes back fast; do not panic about breaks; come back with low volume.
- 2020-06: "A few months of easy or no training after years of training can actually be a NET POSITIVE" (S018)
- 2025-05: "we're paranoid about it, we think any time off is gonna fuck us up, but it really just reality is the very, very opposite of that" (S013)

## Women and lifting
- **Stance:** Women, especially in their 40s and 50s, should lift hard; "getting bulky" by accident is a myth.
- 2024-07: "my favorite Psyop is the one that women give to themselves, which is if I lift weights, I will become too jacked" (S002)

## Coaching clients and communication
- **Stance:** Trust logged performance over self-report; keep the plan stable while clients crave novelty; a trainer the client connects with is worth a lot.
- 2016-08: "no one is as in touch with your body and its states of change as YOU" (S025)
- 2020-07: "If the app, the template, or your coach makes a recommendation, it’s probably a good idea to take that input seriously." (S021)
- 2025-02: "If you have a trainer where you connect with like that, you got yourself a great trainer, ... even if they're not super evidence-based" (S006)

## Motivation and adherence to training
- **Stance:** Lower the barriers, set process goals, train with people, do lifts you like; passion makes motivation moot.
- 2024-02: "If you have to ask how to get motivated to go to the gym, you don't need to be going to the gym. You don't want it enough." (S001)
- 2025-07: "if you have to drive one hour to the gym ... and back every day, you're gonna need a lot of motivation, like by definition and probably a lot of willpower too" (S005)

## Evidence versus anecdote, and fitness myths
- **Stance:** Humans are similar; controlled trials beat mechanisms and gurus; "it works for him" usually fails the basics test.
- 2015-01: "Humans are incredibly similar in basic anatomy and physiology." (S034)
- 2024-07: calls people who say calories do not matter anything from misinformed to charlatans (S002).

## Social media and online content
- **Stance:** Social media is net good and his main channel; "best split" thumbnails are a game; do not read the comments.
- 2024-03: "reading comments on social media is one of the stupidest things you can do" (S011)
- 2024-06: says social media is among the best things to happen, while harming some kids (S008).

## Running a coaching business (RP)
- **Stance:** Sell what the evidence supports even when it sells worse; turn methods into templates and apps; use your own product.
- 2024-02: "We're not going to do bullshit. ... Just facts. Sometimes that doesn't sell that well." (S001)
- 2020-07: checks RP app data on 10,000 diets and flags survivor bias (S021).

## GLP-1 drugs
- **Stance:** Strongly for; the "cheating" objection is nonsense.
- 2024-07: "Your ability to lose weight can be a test of your willpower. Or you can take the fucking pill or the injection, ... and with no added willpower, lose all the weight you want." (S002)

## Steroids and TRT
- **Stance:** Openly enhanced; harm reduction; warns about fertility and mental cost; says enhanced muscle partly stays.
- 2025-02: "don't just start steroids or TRT without a real long hard think about what the hell you want out of life, especially if you have yet to have children but want children" (S006)
- 2024-09: "anabolic steroids and growth hormone are one hell of a recovery supplement, but they got their downsides" (S003)
