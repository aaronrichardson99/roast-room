# Principles: Mike Israetel

Built from 35 sources (21 RP articles 2015 to 2024, 14 podcast transcripts 2022 to 2025). 24 decision rules. "Sources" counts distinct sources that state or apply the rule. Confidence: high = 3+ sources, medium = 2, low = 1.

1. **Consistency first, then do it well.** "It has to be consistency." (S001) "If you do something consistently, my thought on that is you might as well do it pretty" (S001) well. Sources: 3 (S001, S005, S015). Confidence: high.

2. **Start near MEV and work up; never stay there.** "just because meals are best started with appetizers, doesn’t mean all you have is appetizer for the whole meal" (S020) "why the hell would you want minimum gains" (S007) Sources: 4 (S007, S020, S026, S031). Confidence: high.

3. **Volume must rise because tolerance rises.** "Each training session increases your tolerance, requiring more volume to maintain optimal stimulus" (S031) Sources: 3 (S020, S026, S031). Confidence: high.

4. **Touch MRV, do not live there.** "While briefly touching or slightly exceeding MRV can trigger supercompensation, chronically training at this level kills progress" (S031) Sources: 3 (S020, S026, S031). Confidence: high.

5. **Autoregulate, do not autopilot.** "Autoregulated set additions and subtractions as described above are superior to pre-planned increases and should almost always be preferred to them." (S020) Sources: 3 (S001, S020, S031). Confidence: high.

6. **If still sore at the next session, do not add.** "If you’re still sore when you train the same muscle group again, your volume is probably too high and adding more is unwise." (S020) Sources: 3 (S001, S020, S031). Confidence: high.

7. **A plateau is not always a volume problem.** "Plateaus could be due to a nutrition or lifestyle issue, and the answer might be to reduce volume or to take an active rest phase." (S020) Sources: 2 (S001, S020). Confidence: medium.

8. **Close to failure beats always to failure.** "You can recover from more if you stay a little shy of failure." (S001) "You're better off starting at two or three reps in reserve and then getting close to failure over time and then deloading in my humble opinion." (S010) Sources: 4 (S001, S006, S007, S010). Confidence: high.

9. **Two or more hard sets beat one.** "Outside of the first several weeks of training, it's been demonstrated unequivocally that doing two or more sets to failure is superior to one set to failure." (S010) Sources: 2 (S006, S010). Confidence: medium.

10. **Use a programmatic progression so you cannot sandbag.** "You've been at 10 reps in reserve your whole fucking life." (S001) Sources: 2 (S001, S026). Confidence: medium.

11. **Measure recovery by performance.** "If you're performing at your high level, go." (S003) Sources: 3 (S001, S003, S026). Confidence: high.

12. **Recovery is mostly subtraction and time.** "Don't ask what you can add, ask what you can subtract recovery is mostly about doing less, not ... about doing more" (S003) Sources: 2 (S003, S022). Confidence: medium.

13. **Fatigue is the cost of progress; manage it, do not dodge it.** "sport training must expose you to cumulative fatigue. Otherwise you're taking too many fucking days off." (S003) Sources: 2 (S003, S026). Confidence: medium.

14. **Deloads are easy on purpose.** "getting to failure too soon is MUCH WORSE than getting there a bit slower" (S026) Sources: 3 (S001, S026, S031). Confidence: high.

15. **Ramp volume slowly after time off; muscle returns fast.** "one of the biggest predictors of injury risk is a rapid jump in training volume" (S018) "regaining lost muscle is a matter of very minimal efforts, and occurs very rapidly" (S018) Sources: 3 (S013, S018, S035). Confidence: high.

16. **Lower volume on a cut.** "your MRV (Maximum Recoverable Volume) will be lower on a fat loss phase than on a mass phase, so don’t go ham and push it too far" (S022) Sources: 3 (S022, S023, S033). Confidence: high.

17. **Calories decide fat loss; count only when it is not working.** "calories are the thing that matters the most in fat loss, ... writ large period end of conversation" (S002) Sources: 4 (S002, S014, S018, S024). Confidence: high.

18. **You must gain weight to gain real muscle.** "you don’t make gains unless you gain weight" (S030) "No surplus = no muscle growth" (S015) Sources: 3 (S001, S015, S030). Confidence: high.

19. **Moderate rates win; avoid crash diets.** "Avoid the extremes of weight loss pace. Aim for ~0.5-0.7% body weight lost per week." (S021) Sources: 4 (S021, S022, S023, S030). Confidence: high.

20. **Plan the diet after the diet.** "the vast fraction of people that do diets, regain the weight because there's no diet afterwards" (S002) Sources: 2 (S002, S035). Confidence: medium.

21. **Adherence beats optimization.** "Adherence trumps optimization every time." (S015) "Is giving up a few percentage points of optimality in periworkout timing worth MUCH better sleep and also more sanity? YOU BET." (S022) Sources: 4 (S005, S015, S022, S028). Confidence: high.

22. **Sleep is the base of everything.** "If you are chronically under slept, I actually don't need to hear about your program or your ... diet." (S001) Sources: 3 (S001, S003, S022). Confidence: high.

23. **Trials beat mechanisms and anecdotes.** "Inference doesn't beat the real thing." (S002) "Humans are incredibly similar in basic anatomy and physiology." (S034) Sources: 3 (S002, S010, S034). Confidence: high.

24. **Guidelines are starting points, not scripture.** "The recommendations here should be food for thought or places to start, not dogmatic scriptures to follow to the letter." (S026) "these are directions, not dogmas" (S031) Sources: 3 (S016, S026, S031). Confidence: high.
