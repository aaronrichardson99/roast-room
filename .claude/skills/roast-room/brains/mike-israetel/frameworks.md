# Frameworks: Mike Israetel

Built from 35 sources (21 RP Strength articles, 2015 to 2024, and 14 long podcast transcripts, 2022 to 2025). 18 frameworks, most-used first. Names are his or RP's where they have one; otherwise a short name marked *(unnamed)*. "How to score" lines are scoring guidance for the council built from his stated thresholds, not quotes.

---

## 1. Volume landmarks (MV, MEV, MAV, MRV)
- **What it is:** Weekly hard sets per muscle sit on a ladder. MV keeps what you have. MEV is the least that grows anything. MAV is the range where the best gains happen. MRV is the most you can still recover from. Priority versions (MAV*P, MRV*P) apply when you cut other muscles back to specialize.
- **Parts:** MV about 6 sets a week for most muscles (lower for muscles that get indirect work, sometimes zero for front delts, traps, forearms). Intermediate quads, for example: MV 2-4, MEV 4-6, MAV 6-14, MRV 14-18, MRV*P 18-24+ (S026). Beginners sit lower; advanced lifters see the MEV-MRV window narrow.
- **When to use:** Any question of "how many sets", "is this program too much", "why am I not growing", or "how little can I do and keep my muscle".
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan starts each muscle near its MEV and has a route toward MRV with a deload; 3 if volume is in a sane 10 to 20 sets a week range but fixed; 1 if it sits at maintenance and expects growth, or starts at or above MRV.
- **Quote:** "MV is surprisingly low ... typically around 6 working sets per muscle group per week when training each muscle at least twice weekly" (S031)
- **Sources:** 8 (S007, S016, S017, S019, S020, S026, S031, S032)

## 2. Stimulus to fatigue ratio (SFR)
- **What it is:** Bang for your buck in muscle terms: how much growth stimulus an exercise, set, or technique gives per unit of fatigue.
- **Parts:** Stimulus proxies are tension, burn, pump, perturbation (weakness, cramping), and soreness. Fatigue types are joint and connective tissue, axial (spinal loading), systemic, and lost desire to train. Low-SFR markers: hits too many muscles at once (conventional deadlift), no deep stretch (floor press, rack pulls), top-end partials.
- **When to use:** Exercise selection, choosing between two programs, judging a new technique or a client's favorite lift.
- **How to score:** 5 if the main lifts target the muscle through a deep stretch with low joint pain; 3 if they work but carry heavy axial or joint cost; 1 if the program leans on lifts that tire everything and target nothing.
- **Quote:** "The stimulus fatigue ratio is kind of like bang for your buck." (S001)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S007, S009, S020, S026)

## 3. MEV-to-MRV mesocycle progression
- **What it is:** Start each mesocycle near MEV, add sets week to week when recovery allows, lower RIR from about 3-4 to 0-1, end near MRV, then deload.
- **Parts:** Keep reps stable and add load as RIR falls. Accumulation lasts until systemic MRV: up to 12 weeks for beginners, 3 to 4 weeks for very advanced lifters, 4 to 8 for most hard trainers.
- **When to use:** Building or fixing any hypertrophy program; arguing with a fixed-volume plan.
- **How to score:** 5 if volume and effort rise across the block and a deload is planned; 3 if load progresses but volume never moves; 1 if nothing progresses.
- **Quote:** "Starting each meso at the minimum effective volume (MEV) for hypertrophy has big upsides." (S020)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S020, S022, S026, S031)

## 4. Set progression algorithm (autoregulated set additions)
- **What it is:** Decide next week's sets from last week's recovery and stimulus.
- **Parts:** Three inputs: performance recovery (did reps hold or rise), perceptive recovery (did soreness heal on time), perceptive stimulus proxy (pump and post-set weakness). RP scoring: 1s on soreness and performance add 2-3 sets; 2s add 1; 3s with soreness hold; a 4 on performance means a recovery session or deload (S031).
- **When to use:** Week to week coaching decisions and client check-ins.
- **How to score:** guidance only: add sets when recovering early with weak pumps; hold when barely healing; cut when still sore or reps fall.
- **Quote:** "The process by which such a decision to add sets is made can involve as many as three inputs: performance recovery, perceptive recovery, and perceptive stimulus proxy." (S020)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S020, S031)

## 5. Performance as the grand integrator of recovery
- **What it is:** Recovery is measured best by whether you can perform at or above baseline. In the gym, reps and loads are the measurement.
- **Parts:** Other signals (diffuse joint soreness above normal, low desire to train relative to your norm, drifting body weight, poor sleep) only count when several point the same way. Lazy athletes under-report, conscientious ones over-report.
- **When to use:** "Am I overtraining?", "should I deload?", reading a client's check-in.
- **How to score:** 5 if decisions run off logged performance; 3 if they run off feel plus some logs; 1 if they run off mood or soreness alone.
- **Quote:** "Performance is the grand truth teller." (S003)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S003, S026)

## 6. Local and systemic MRV signs, and the deload
- **What it is:** Two sessions in a row without tying previous reps means local MRV. Systemic MRV is when sleep worsens, appetite falls, you get sick more, and many muscles hit local MRV together.
- **Parts:** Local MRV: a recovery session at half sets, reps and load, then resume at sets halfway between start and MRV, 2 RIR. Systemic MRV: a deload week at MEV sets, half reps, week-one load then half load.
- **When to use:** Any stall, any "should I push through".
- **How to score:** 5 if stalls trigger planned easy work; 1 if stalls trigger more volume by default.
- **Quote:** "This makes the deload VERY EASY, which is the whole point, since hard training doesn't bring down fatigue!" (S026)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S026, S031)

## 7. Three sources of fatigue, and recovery by subtraction
- **What it is:** Fatigue comes from training, from all daily physical activity, and from psychological stress. Recovery is mostly doing less, plus sleep, food, rest, easier training, and time. (Credits Dr. James Hoffmann.)
- **When to use:** A client who "does everything right" and still stalls; busy, high-step, high-stress clients.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan accounts for job, steps, and stress; 1 if it treats training as the only load.
- **Quote:** "You need sleep, food, rest, and easier training than normal. Those are the big rocks." (S003)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S003, S022)

## 8. Acute versus cumulative fatigue *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Hard trainers train more often than full recovery allows, so fatigue builds like debt. One easy day will not clear it. It must be managed at every timescale: easy days, deload weeks, and about two weeks of active rest every six months or so.
- **Quote:** "So if you have a high degree of accumulated fatigue, not only will it affect you much more, it also just doesn't disappear." (S003)
- **Sources:** 2 (S003, S026)

## 9. Effort and RIR: train close to failure, not always to it
- **What it is:** Sets count when taken within about 0 to 4 reps of failure. Failure gives a little more stimulus per set and much more fatigue. Most training should average 1 to 3 RIR, reaching failure late in a meso.
- **When to use:** HIT and "every set to failure" programs; clients who sandbag.
- **How to score:** 5 if RIR is tracked and falls across the block with periodic true failure tests; 3 if sets are hard but untracked; 1 if sets end 5 to 10 reps shy of failure or every set goes past failure at high volume.
- **Quote:** "somewhere between three and one rep and reserve on average for a program is a really good idea" (S001)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S006, S007, S010, S026)

## 10. Rep range 5 to 30 *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Loads from about 30 to 85% of 1RM, giving 5 to 30 reps near failure, grow muscle about equally on average. Split into 5-10, 10-20, 20-30; maybe half of sets in 10-20; compounds lower, isolations higher; heavy before light in the week.
- **Quote:** "anywhere between sets of five and sets of 30 is kind of unlikely to be a ton of really wrong answers" (S001)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S026, S031)

## 11. Sets per muscle per session cap *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Best average is about 5 to 8 hard sets per muscle per session; past about 8 to 12 it turns into junk volume, so add sessions instead.
- **Quote:** "you are likely to exceed the 8-12 set per muscle per session maximum, beyond which systemic fatigue makes more training within that session very inefficient" (S026)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S007, S020, S026)

## 12. Frequency from recovery *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Train a muscle again as soon as performance is back. Most can do 2 to 4 sessions per muscle a week; raise frequency across a block as volume climbs.
- **Quote:** "so long as you’re recovered to train again (can perform at or above normal levels), training is a better idea than waiting to train" (S026)
- **Sources:** 2 (S001, S026)

## 13. Four-factor rest model
- **What it is:** Start the next set when breathing, nervous system drive, and synergists are no longer limiting and the target muscle can do at least 5 reps. Calves can be 10 seconds; heavy squats several minutes.
- **Quote:** "if you're ready to go again, if everything's fucking working and you feel strong, go." (S001)
- **Sources:** 2 (S001, S026)

## 14. Exercise deletion and replacement (four questions)
- **What it is:** Keep an exercise while you still gain rep strength, it causes no worsening joint pain, it fits the rep range, and it still gives a mind-muscle connection. Otherwise swap it. Staleness is falling SFR.
- **Quote:** "If you’re doing an exercise, there should be a reason for it." (S026)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S007, S026)

## 15. Technique checklist and the deep stretch *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Good technique targets the muscle by mechanics, is stable, controls the eccentric, uses mostly full ROM with priority on the lengthened position, and repeats rep to rep. Lengthened partials can be a growth booster; top-end ego partials are not.
- **Quote:** "stretch under load is an independent driver of hypertrophy" (S026)
- **Sources:** 4 (S001, S009, S026, S034)

## 16. Diet phases: mass, maintain, cut, minicut
- **What it is:** Mass at about 0.25 to 0.5% bodyweight a week, maintain after, cut at about 0.5 to 1% a week. Minicuts of 2 to 6 weeks at near MEV training resensitize muscle between masses. After a diet, maintain for two thirds to one times its length.
- **When to use:** Bulk-or-cut questions, rate of loss, how long to diet, rebound prevention.
- **How to score:** 5 if rates sit in those ranges with a maintenance plan after; 1 if the plan crash diets or bulks with no ceiling.
- **Quote:** "minicuts re-sensitize the body to further muscle gains" (S023)
- **Sources:** 6 (S002, S021, S022, S023, S029, S030)

## 17. Diet priorities pyramid *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Calories first, then macros (protein above all), then timing, then food composition, then supplements. Calories, macros and timing carry about 95% of fat loss and 80% of health effects.
- **Quote:** "the vast majority of fat loss effects (about 95%) and the majority of health effects (about 80%) from what you eat occur because you’re controlling your calories, macros, and timing appropriately" (S024)
- **Sources:** 4 (S002, S015, S024, S035)

## 18. Layered diet interventions
- **What it is:** Start a cut with plenty of food, easier training, and modest cardio; add layers in sequence as fat loss slows. Never open with your hardest tools.
- **Quote:** "new layers of intervention were added IN SEQUENCE (not all at once)" (S022)
- **Sources:** 2 (S022, S029)

## 19. The basics test and the variation test
- **What it is:** Judging "it works for him". If an approach violates the basics (compounds, full ROM, high protein, diet plus cardio), the athlete wins in spite of it. If it passes the variation test (injury, leverages, genetics), it may suit only that person.
- **Quote:** "maybe they'd be EVEN better if they didn't do it that way." (S034)
- **Sources:** 1 (S034)
