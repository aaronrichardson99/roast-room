# Frameworks: Eric Helms

Built from the notes on 34 sources (podcast transcripts 2016 to 2025, Stronger By Science and MASS articles 2017 to 2024, his first-author papers and PhD thesis 2014 to 2020). 18 frameworks, roughly most-used first. Names are his where he named them; otherwise a short name marked *(unnamed)*. "How to score" lines are scoring guidance for the council built from his stated thresholds, not quotes.

---

## 1. The Muscle and Strength Pyramids (priority order)
- **What it is:** His master framework. Fix the big rocks first, in order; small rocks only matter once the big ones are in place. Nutrition: energy balance, then macronutrients, then micronutrients, then meal timing and frequency, then supplements. Training: adherence and the basics of volume, intensity and frequency, then progression, then exercise selection, then rest periods and tempo.
- **Parts:** Calories (rate of loss or gain) first; protein and macro minimums; fruit, vegetables and micronutrients; timing; supplements last. In training: hard sets near failure, enough weekly volume, progressive overload, then details.
- **When to use:** Any plan, client complaint, or new trend. Ask where it sits on the pyramid and whether the layers under it are solid.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan nails energy balance, protein, progressive overload and adherence before anything else; 3 if the base is mostly there but effort goes to small rocks; 1 if the plan leads with timing, supplements, or a novel technique while the base is shaky.
- **Quote:** "your calorie intake, your energy balance is probably the big thing you want to know." (S016) "With the big rocks of progressive overload, an appropriate nutrition plan, and a lifestyle and environment conducive to recovery and adherence in place, any small rocks you add on top can only have so much of an impact." (S027)
- **Sources:** 8 (S002, S006, S013, S016, S019, S027, S028, S031)

## 2. Rate of weight change targets *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Diet and bulk by rate, not by calorie dogma. Lose 0.5 to 1 percent of body weight per week (faster early, slower as you get lean); gain about 0.5 to 1.5 percent per month, scaled to training age, about 1 percent for most trained lifters.
- **Parts:** Daily weigh-ins, weekly or 14-day averages, wait two weeks before changing calories, then 100 to 200 kcal changes. Surpluses are small: about 100 to 300 kcal a day by body size.
- **When to use:** Setting up any cut, bulk, or client check-in.
- **How to score:** 5 if within 0.5 to 1 percent a week (cut) or about 1 percent a month (gain) with averages-based adjustments; 3 if a bit fast but short; 1 for crash diets, fast "dirty bulks" for physique athletes, or day-to-day reactions to the scale.
- **Quote:** "Caloric intake should be set at a level that results in bodyweight losses of approximately 0.5 to 1%/wk to maximize muscle retention." (S028) "a good, reasonable suggested rate of weight gain for someone who has been in the gym a while is probably around like one percent of your body weight per month." (S007)
- **Sources:** 10 (S001, S002, S007, S008, S011, S015, S016, S025, S028, S031)

## 3. Volume as hard sets, with diminishing returns
- **What it is:** Count volume as hard sets per muscle per week. More volume grows more muscle on average, but with steep diminishing returns, then a downslope.
- **Parts:** About 10 sets per muscle per week as a starting point for trained people (6 to 8 when returning); 1 to 4 sets gets roughly 60 percent to two thirds of the gains; increase by 20 to 30 percent (1 to 2 sets) when stalled and recovered; never triple volume.
- **When to use:** Program design, plateaus, "should I do more?"
- **How to score:** 5 if volume is moderate, near failure, and raised only with evidence; 3 if very high but recoverable; 1 if volume is maxed by default or jumps sharply.
- **Quote:** "Any adaptation, it’s going to follow a parabolic curve." (S003) "it’s probably better to err on the side of doing a fair amount of volume, but not the most you can handle is that’s not the same as the most you’ll benefit from." (S003)
- **Sources:** 7 (S002, S003, S011, S013, S014, S015, S024)

## 4. Proximity to failure (RIR-based RPE)
- **What it is:** Effort is a primary driver of hypertrophy. Rate sets by reps in reserve: RPE 9 means one rep left, 8 two, 7 three. You need to be reasonably close to failure, not always at it.
- **Parts:** Most work 1 to 3 reps from failure; failure sparingly and on safer lifts; learn to rate by rating for 2 to 3 weeks before using it to prescribe.
- **When to use:** Judging whether training is hard enough, or too hard to recover from.
- **How to score:** 5 if most sets land around 1 to 3 RIR with honest rating; 3 if consistently 4 or more RIR or always to failure; 1 if effort is unknown or sets are far from failure.
- **Quote:** "you don’t need to go to failure to get the benefits, but you need to be reasonably close to failure" (S014) "One of the key components to hypertrophy is having some kind of reasonable proximity to failure." (S009)
- **Sources:** 9 (S009, S013, S014, S015, S020, S021, S029, S030, S034)

## 5. Autoregulation *(systematic, not "by feel")*
- **What it is:** A system in which training adapts to the lifter's readiness and performance. It is individuality made practical, not training by feel.
- **Parts:** Percent of 1RM plus an RPE target; RPE stops for volume; readiness after a warm up picks the session; run sessions in order, not fixed days; exercise choice on accessories with guard rails on skill lifts.
- **When to use:** Intermediate and advanced lifters, dieting phases, busy or variable lives.
- **How to score:** 5 if there is a clear rule for adjusting load or volume from feedback; 3 if adjustments happen but ad hoc; 1 if it is pure feel, or a rigid spreadsheet that ignores bad days.
- **Quote:** "autoregulation is simply a systematic approach to implementing something every trainer worth their salt knows is of supreme importance: the principle of individuality." (S020) "There does need to be some type of system. It’s just that the system is agile, the system adapts to what is happening." (S009)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S009, S020, S029, S030, S032, S034, S011)

## 6. Progressive overload is the primary variable
- **What it is:** Whatever the rep range or technique, you must be doing more over time, and you must compare like with like in your logbook.
- **Parts:** Compounds as the base, moderate reps; track reps and load; judge a block over 6 to 12 weeks by reps and load trending up.
- **When to use:** Evaluating any program, technique, or "it's not working."
- **How to score:** 5 if there is a clear progression model and a log; 1 if no one can say whether performance is going up.
- **Quote:** "progressive overload is still the primary variable we need to ensure that we get any fitness adaptation over time." (S006) "However, progressive overload still matters." (S021)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S002, S006, S015, S021, S011)

## 7. Plateau diagnostic order (volume is the last lever) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** When progress stalls, check in order: lifestyle (sleep, protein, calories, dieting), training quality (technique, real proximity to failure), recovery, and only then add volume.
- **Parts:** For advanced lifters with food in place, the answer is almost always training (volume, effort, specialization), not nutrition.
- **When to use:** Any client who has stopped progressing.
- **How to score:** 5 if the fix targets the first broken layer; 1 if the fix jumps to more volume, more cardio, or a supplement.
- **Quote:** "increasing volume is something to, it's a lever, it's the last lever to pull, and it's when you've turned over every other stone" (S013) "if, in the cases where it could be nutrition or training, it's always training" (S015)
- **Sources:** 4 (S005, S013, S015, S033)

## 8. Start at the evidence mean, then individualize *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Use meta-analyses to set the starting point, then run the scientific process on yourself or your client. You are not an average.
- **Parts:** Start in the ballpark; change one variable; commit for a block; judge by performance trend; repeat. Single studies compare options; meta-analyses tell you where to start.
- **When to use:** Translating research into a plan; responding to "what does the science say?"
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan starts from consensus and has a test built in; 3 if it copies a study protocol; 1 if it rests on one study or one influencer.
- **Quote:** "I’m not the mean of 15 studies on trained individuals. I am me." (S003) "And another thing to consider is that you are not an average." (S013)
- **Sources:** 6 (S003, S005, S013, S015, S020, S023)

## 9. Minimum effective dose and opportunity cost
- **What it is:** Find the least you need to move a goal, then spend the leftover recovery where it pays. Strength and size are not in conflict; the cost is opportunity.
- **Parts:** A low dose of specific work (heavy singles, 1 to 5 hard sets) for strength; back-off and accessory work for size; health goals should aim at the minimum dose.
- **When to use:** Busy clients, concurrent goals, older lifters, powerlifters who also want size.
- **How to score:** 5 if each goal gets its minimum and the rest is allocated on purpose; 1 if everything is maxed at once.
- **Quote:** "you need to understand what the minimum effective dose is for you to improve it both." (S014) "Because the biggest issue with training for strength and hypertrophy is not their conflicting goals. It's the opportunity cost." (S014)
- **Sources:** 4 (S013, S014, S018, S024)

## 10. Optimal within constraints
- **What it is:** "Optimal" depends on what a person will actually do. Natural bodybuilders accept poor returns on extra work; most people should not.
- **Parts:** Ask how much time and stress they will invest; optimize inside that; explain the ROI curve.
- **When to use:** General population clients, time-poor clients, anyone asking for the "best" program.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan fits their real life and they can keep it for years; 1 if it is optimal on paper and abandoned in month two.
- **Quote:** "So, when someone says what's “optimal”, you really need to consider your audience." (S013) "it's all about what can you adhere to long term and what's going to be worth it." (S013)
- **Sources:** 5 (S003, S006, S013, S014, S025)

## 11. Contest prep timeline and structure *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Natural prep needs time. Start about five to seven months out, about 15 percent fat max for men, with backup shows; diet breaks every 4 to 8 weeks; refeeds increasing as you get lean; mandatory deloads; cardio capped.
- **Parts:** Cardio no more hours than lifting (later: cardio energy at most about half of lifting volume); steps up 10 to 20 percent at a time; trim volume late in prep; simple peak week (normal water and sodium, carb up, salt before pump up).
- **When to use:** Any physique competitor, or any client chasing very low body fat.
- **How to score:** 5 if the timeline allows 0.5 to 1 percent a week with slack; 1 for 8 to 12 week preps or crash finishes.
- **Quote:** "If I was to put one thing above them all is simply longer dieting times." (S008) "The eight to 12 week contest prep is a side effect of the length of some of these steroid cycles." (S008)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S008, S011, S012, S028, S031)

## 12. Low energy availability and the recession analogy
- **What it is:** Energy availability (intake minus exercise expenditure, per kg lean mass) drives physiology. Too low and the body cuts costs like a business in recession: hormones, NEAT, sleep, libido, immunity. Below your lower body fat intervention point, body fat itself (via leptin) keeps you there even at maintenance.
- **Parts:** About 30 kcal/kg LBM as a rough threshold; women more sensitive; RED-S symptoms; under-eating can look like overtraining.
- **When to use:** Clients who want to stay very lean, stalled dieters, fatigued athletes.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan keeps energy availability adequate and respects the lower point; 1 if it asks someone to hold stage-lean year round.
- **Quote:** "it’s likely that energy availability is the dominant variable impacting your physiology when you’re between your upper and lower body fat intervention points." (S022) "Technically it seems to be about calories, but if you get too lean, there’s probably an independent negative effect as well." (S010)
- **Sources:** 6 (S008, S010, S012, S022, S031, S033)

## 13. Recovery diet (after a show or hard diet)
- **What it is:** The goal after a peak is to recover, not to stay shredded. Raise calories 1000 to 1500, cut cardio, regain to 5 to 10 percent over stage weight in one to two months, relax food rules gradually.
- **Parts:** Plan the exit before the show; reverse the behaviors as well as the calories; then modified intuitive eating in the off season. Replaced his earlier reverse dieting (2016).
- **When to use:** End of any big diet, any client terrified of regaining.
- **How to score:** 5 if there is a planned surplus and an exit plan; 1 if the plan is "stay lean and add 50 kcal a week" or a list of restaurants.
- **Quote:** "I get someone back up to being five to 10% over stage weight in one to two months." (S008) "So I think reverse dieting is pandering to the negative psychological adaptations from dieting" (S008)
- **Sources:** 6 (S008, S012, S016, S017, S022, S031)

## 14. Informed consent for physique sport
- **What it is:** Competing is a deal with trade-offs. The coach's job is to make them clear and help the athlete get more good than bad.
- **Parts:** Screen for eating disorder history; compete for the right reasons, not to fix something; exit strategy; self-worth separate from placing; compete at most every other year.
- **When to use:** Anyone asking whether to compete, do a photo shoot, or chase a dramatic transformation.
- **How to score:** 5 if the client knows the costs and has a plan for after; 1 if they are competing to fix how they feel about themselves.
- **Quote:** "So informed consent, I think, is the goal." (S008) "that's kind of the whole mission statement I have is to help people get more good than bad and make it worth it." (S011)
- **Sources:** 6 (S008, S011, S012, S017, S028, S031)

## 15. Calorie range plus macro minimums, and tracking stages
- **What it is:** His newest nutrition prescription (third edition of the Pyramids): a calorie range and minimum intakes of protein, fat and carbs instead of tight macro targets. Clients move through stages: self-track to learn, targets, write their own "default diet", then habits without logging.
- **Parts:** Protein about 1.6 g/kg off season, higher when dieting; fat floor for hormones; carb floor by training volume; flexible ranges, not to-the-gram precision.
- **When to use:** Building a nutrition plan, tracking burnout, clients in different food cultures.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan uses ranges and builds skills toward independence; 1 if it demands rigid daily macros forever.
- **Quote:** "we have a calorie range that's appropriate. And then we simply have a minimum intake for fat carbs and protein." (S015) "I think sometimes people get too caught up on the fact that they logged it, rather than the fact that they did it." (S015)
- **Sources:** 5 (S011, S012, S015, S016, S031)

## 16. Autonomy-supportive coaching
- **What it is:** People resist being told what to do. Ask, collaborate, explain what you need from them; use self-determination theory (autonomy, competence, relatedness) and COM-B as a checklist.
- **Parts:** Clear asks for data (session RPE, recovery notes); collaborative changes; coach yourself by writing yourself a weekly check-in.
- **When to use:** Client communication, adherence problems, changing a plan a client likes.
- **How to score:** 5 if the change is made with the client; 1 if it is imposed as "the science says".
- **Quote:** "you can't have a realization for someone else. But you absolutely can assist them" (S014)
- **Sources:** 4 (S009, S012, S014, S017)

## 17. Logic, then lab, then context (judging a technique or trend) *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Ask what the technique logically does, what controlled research shows, and what real-world constraint (time, fatigue, adherence) changes the answer. Lab results do not transfer automatically to the gym.
- **Parts:** Read the methods; small samples are fragile; lots of loud disagreement signals thin evidence; extraordinary claims need extraordinary evidence; converging weak evidence can justify moderate confidence.
- **When to use:** Any new trend, supplement, influencer claim, or study headline.
- **How to score:** 5 if the claim holds on all three; 1 if it rests on a mechanism or one small study.
- **Quote:** "we have to think about rather than how these have been positioned by magazines, but what do they logically do?" (S006) "Training to failure in a lab is not the same as in a gym" (S021)
- **Sources:** 8 (S005, S006, S007, S013, S015, S021, S023, S026)

## 18. The bodybuilding lifestyle
- **What it is:** Long-term success comes from habits that look the same in prep and off season; only the numbers change. Leanness should be an emergent property of good habits, not a number to chase.
- **Parts:** Same meals and routines, daily walks, training as a non-negotiable, low-error food choices when eating out, environment you bring with you.
- **When to use:** Maintenance, long-term clients, anyone yo-yoing.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan builds repeatable habits; 1 if it relies on switching "beast mode" on and off.
- **Quote:** "what you do during prep and what you do in the off season look the same from the outside. And it is really just the quantitative differences" (S012) "I think the leanness should be an emergent property, a healthy, sustainable lifestyle effects." (S008)
- **Sources:** 5 (S008, S010, S011, S012, S013)
