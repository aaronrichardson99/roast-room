# Stories: Eric Helms

Built from the notes on 34 sources. 17 stories and cases he reuses, with the lesson he draws. Source ids in parentheses.

1. **The first show and the restaurant list (2007).** He did his first prep tracking to the gram (half protein bars, a spoon of olive oil), with only a list of restaurants as a post-show plan, and gained about 22 kg in two months. Lesson: be behaviorally skilled, not just scientifically correct, and plan the exit; this gap is why 3DMJ exists (S012, S014, S016, S017).
2. **The 2005 cut that took twice as long.** He thought 200 lb would be near stage ready, got there at about 14 percent fat, and competed at about 178 lb in 2007. Lesson: people need to lose far more than they think (S001).
3. **Nineteen years to a WNBF pro card.** Set the goal in 2006, came close six or seven times, kept himself off stage in 2008 and 2010 to grow, took years off for grad school, and turned pro in 2023 at 40. Lesson: process over outcome; time off is when physiques change (S008, S011, S015).
4. **"If I can't be Mr. Natural Olympia, would I keep lifting?" (2007).** He said it to his wife and saw he had lost the journey to an extrinsic goal. Lesson: protect intrinsic motivation (S011).
5. **2009, the hardest season.** His father died, he tore a hamstring, his job was uncertain, an uncle got cancer; prep gave him structure and friends carried him. Lesson: "it’s not about how hard you can hit but how hard you can get hit and still get up." (S016)
6. **Six or seven pounds of muscle in eight years.** His stage weight rose only four or five pounds from 2011 to 2019 at a leaner condition. Lesson: advanced natural gains are tiny; invest in the lifestyle, not the outcome (S008).
7. **2019 recovery that still did not fix things.** He raised calories by over 1000 and halved cardio for eight weeks at the same weight and looked better, but still slept four hours and had no libido. Lesson: no hack around low body fat; regain is needed (S008, S012).
8. **2023: better and better, lower and lower placings.** Won the NZ overall without a card, then second, then third, then a pro card in Australia. Lesson: judge yourself on objective improvement; competing gives judges some power over your mood (S015).
9. **The bench press decade.** 90 lb added in the first year, 90 more in two years, the last 40 to 50 lb took nearly a decade. Lesson: intermediates misjudge progress against newbie gains (S003).
10. **Missing lats.** Strong and with good rear delts, he had poor lat development and needed years of targeted work. Lesson: fundamentals first, then a specific fix for a specific problem (S006).
11. **Pop-Tarts and flexible dieting.** Alberto Nunez photographed Helms eating Pop-Tarts in 2007 to 2009 to challenge clean-eating dogma. Lesson: it was useful then; moderation matters more now (S002).
12. **The GVT study.** Five sets of ten beat ten sets of ten for gains in recreational lifters. Lesson: you can do too much volume (S003).
13. **6 to 29 reps at 70 percent.** In a Zourdos lab study, lifters did between 6 and 29 reps at 70 percent of 1RM. Lesson: fixed percentages fit no one exactly; use RPE (S009, S020).
14. **His PhD: RPE beat the plan he wrote.** The RPE group increased load faster than the percentage group whose plan he designed. Lesson: autoregulation can speed progress, not only manage fatigue (S009, S030, S034).
15. **Ribeiro's bodybuilders, back-calculated.** The fast-bulk group appeared to gain twice the muscle until Greg Nuckols back-calculated lean mass; the trade was about 4 to 3 lean to fat versus 4 to 1. Lesson: read the methods; faster is not better for naturals (S007).
16. **Travelling for conferences.** On trips he over-corrects, walks far more, and comes back leaner; in 2025 his coaches prescribed a diet break during the trip. Lesson: prep exposes your tendencies, so get an objective coach (S012).
17. **The Amish and the Quakers.** Highly active traditional communities walk around near 10 percent body fat eating plenty. Lesson: environment and activity set where you settle; modern life pulls the other way (S010, S012, S022).
