# Positions: Eric Helms

Built from the notes on 34 sources (2013 to 2025). 32 topics, written for a fitness coaching audience. Each stance has a verbatim quote, source id, and date (see `sources.md`). Where he changed his mind, both views sit side by side with dates; the newer one is marked. Topics with no evidence say so.

## Training volume
- **Stance:** Count hard sets per muscle per week. About 10 is a sensible start for trained lifters; returns diminish fast; add 20 to 30 percent only when stalled and recovered. He personally needs 20 to 40 sets, and says that is not normal.
- "So don’t triple your volume." (S003, 2017-12)
- "But if I told you that you had to triple or quintuple your volume to get a 1 3rd return on investment, I know your audience." (S013, 2025-06)
- "I would love to be a low volume proponent." (S013, 2025-06)

## Frequency
- **Stance:** Each muscle about two to three times a week on average; distribution is a tool for recovery, not magic. Sessions can run in order instead of fixed days.
- "So what we know is that you probably want to be training each muscle group around two to three times per week." (S003, 2017-12)
- "nobody cares if you complete a four week program in four and a half weeks" (S009, 2020-12)

## Intensity, failure and RPE
- **Stance:** Train reasonably close to failure, mostly 1 to 3 reps in reserve; failure sparingly; RIR-based RPE to individualize load. RPE loading may give a small strength edge.
- "you don’t need to go to failure to get the benefits, but you need to be reasonably close to failure" (S014, 2023-05)
- "Both loading-types are effective. However, RPE-based loading may provide a small 1RM strength advantage in a majority of individuals." (S034, 2018-03)

## Load and rep ranges
- **Stance:** Low loads build muscle if taken near failure, but in a real gym keep free-weight compounds around 6 to 8 reps at RPE 7 to 9 and use high reps on isolation work.
- "Doing sets of 6-8 reps between a 7-9 RPE (1-3 repetitions short of failure) is going to land in the ~75-85% of 1RM range for most people" (S021, 2018-01)

## Exercise selection
- **Stance:** A base of push, pull, squat and hinge patterns, then accessories by feel and joint comfort; on compounds think form, not the muscle; choice on accessories, stability on lifts you test.
- "when you’re doing, a complex athletic movement, you should be trying to develop movement skill, not feeling a specific muscle group." (S002, 2017-06)
- "So what's the best rep range for you to do a tricep push down is the real question." (S014, 2023-05)

## Advanced techniques (supersets, drop sets, forced reps, pre-exhaust)
- **Stance:** Not advanced, just tools with trade-offs. Antagonist pairs and drop sets on isolation lifts save time; same-muscle supersets hurt performance; forced reps add fatigue without clear benefit.
- "I don’t think these are advanced techniques I think they are Interesting strategies that may allow you to be time efficient" (S006, 2019-07)
- "I’m not a fan of four streps because I think it’s very difficult to apply. A consistent stimulus from a spotter." (S006, 2019-07)

## Periodization and specificity
- **Stance:** Specific work is powerful at a low dose; beyond that, fatigue compounds. Do not keep pulling the specificity lever.
- "instead of reflexively pulling the lever of more specificity when you have already pulled it, consider what other options are available first." (S024, 2023-02)
- **Changed:** he used to chase maximum specificity in his own programming. "I used to, and I applied it to my own programming." (S024, 2023-02)

## Deloads
- **Stance:** Planned deloads every 4 to 8 weeks (every fourth week in prep): about a third less volume, a rep further from failure. You plateau anyway; choose when.
- "you’re gonna plateau regardless but you can either plateau on your terms by choice" (S002, 2017-06)

## Cardio
- **Stance:** Optional for fat loss; useful for health and to keep calories higher. Keep it low impact, separate from lifting where possible, capped. HIIT is not the holy grail.
- "you can definitely get effective fat loss without using cardio." (S004, 2017-12)
- "the big take home from this study is just that hit is not the holy grail." (S004, 2017-12)
- "cardio is never quite as effective as calorie reductions at a certain point." (S001, 2016-10)

## Fat loss rate and crash diets
- **Stance:** 0.5 to 1 percent of body weight per week; faster only briefly and early; trying to diet harder often just ruins adherence.
- "there is an inverse relationship between the prescribed energy deficit and diet adherence, such that the intention to diet harder results in more adherence issues" (S025, 2023-03)
- "a weight-loss rate of 0.5-1% of body mass per week is generally a better choice to improve adherence and body composition outcomes." (S025, 2023-03)

## Bulking and rate of gain
- **Stance:** Small surpluses, about 1 percent of body weight a month for trained lifters; fast bulks mostly add fat; mini cuts only to control fat, with at least a four to one gain to cut ratio.
- "I think we can be relatively confident that at least for drug-free lifters who have a fair amount of their base built" faster is not better (S007, 2019-09).
- "So for every, Four months of time spent in a surplus is one month." (S002, 2017-06)

## Protein
- **Stance:** About 1.6 to 2.2 g/kg is the most that helps muscle when not dieting; while dieting, higher is fine and may help satiety and mood, capped near 2.8 g/kg.
- **Changed:** 2013 review: 2.3 to 3.1 g/kg of lean mass while dieting. After debating Menno Henselmans (2015) he softened it: "The hypothesis that protein intakes above 1.8g/kg of total body weight will help dieters maintain muscle mass does not have strong support." (S019, 2017-05, newer view)
- "I think ~0.8-1g/lb (~1.8-2.2g/kg) is probably the highest intake you’ll benefit from in terms of enhancing strength or mass gains when you’re not in a deficit." (S019, 2017-05)

## Very high protein claims
- **Stance:** No metabolic magic; likely inconvenience and misreporting. Useful only for people who overshoot surpluses.
- "For Extraordinary claims require extraordinary evidence." (S015, 2023-11)

## Plant versus animal protein
- **Stance:** At sport-level intakes with varied foods, no meaningful difference for gains; the health edge of plant diets comes from plants and healthy habits.
- "we don't eat animal and plant proteins. We eat animals and plants, right." (S014, 2023-05)

## Carbs, keto and low carb
- **Stance:** Energy balance is the mechanism; keto can work for rule-followers with low training volume, but for muscle gain keep at least moderate carbs. His own community is too anti low carb.
- "We’ve become very anti low carb and I think we need to just be a little more balanced." (S005, 2019-02)
- "So I would say on balance right now, it’s not looking too good if your goal is to put on muscle mass, you probably want to have some level of carbohydrate in your diet." (S005, 2019-02)

## Macro tracking and flexible dieting
- **Stance:** Tracking is a skill and a tool, not the goal. Newer prescription: calorie range plus minimums, not tight targets. Flexible beats rigid restraint for mental health.
- **Changed:** 2016 he set protein, fat percentages, and the rest carbs as targets (S016). 2023: "we have a calorie range that's appropriate. And then we simply have a minimum intake for fat carbs and protein." (S015, 2023-11, newer view)
- "Taken together, the available evidence suggests that a rigid approach to dieting may be potentially detrimental." (S031, 2019-08)

## Clean eating versus IIFYM
- **Stance:** Both sides have a point; mindset matters. Mostly single-ingredient foods for satiety, room for the rest.
- "Food quality is important, but don’t forget satiety." (S002, 2017-06)
- "The problem is that people attach their identities to their opinions." (S017, 2013-10)

## Supplements
- **Stance:** A minor factor. Creatine is the only Tier 1; caffeine works; whey for convenience; most new products do nothing; third-party tested only.
- "I think supplements are a minor factor." (S018, 2024-08)
- "creatine might be more like a one-time deposit than an investment with compound interest." (S027, 2024-02)
- "BCAA, I think, are largely overrated." (S002, 2017-06)

## Diet adherence and coaching nutrition
- **Stance:** Simple enough to follow, not so simple you miss benefits; build skills toward independence; client writes their own default diet.
- "I like to keep things as simple as possible because I find that enhances adherence but not so simple that we’re missing out on some type of benefit." (S016, 2016-04)
- "I want you to write your own meal plan or three" (S015, 2023-11)

## Diet breaks and refeeds
- **Stance:** Useful for adherence and practice eating normally; diet break every 4 to 8 weeks, refeeds weekly and more often as you get lean.
- "about every four to eight weeks, depending on the individual. I like to take a week at maintenance on all days." (S001, 2016-10)

## Reverse dieting versus recovery diet
- **Changed:** 2016 he supported a measured reverse diet, adding 60 to 100 kcal weekly and relaxing food rules gradually (S016). By 2020: "So I think reverse dieting is pandering to the negative psychological adaptations from dieting" (S008, 2020-07, newer view). He now prescribes a large planned surplus to get back to 5 to 10 percent over stage weight in one to two months.

## Peak week
- **Stance:** Keep it simple: normal water and sodium, carb up, salt before the pump up, practice with refeeds. Water and electrolyte cuts are dangerous and may look worse.
- "if you keep it simple and logical, you’re going to be right most of the time," (S001, 2016-10)
- "really, it's as simple as you want normal fluid turnover." (S012, 2025-08)

## Body fat goals and staying lean
- **Stance:** Health ceiling is high (about 20 percent for lifting men is fine); performance best around 12 to 25 percent for men; staying shredded year round is unrealistic for most; leanness should emerge from habits.
- "the ceiling is a lot higher than the, Hey, I need to be, insert like a random number that’s almost always 15% or 12% or 10% body fat to be healthy." (S010, 2021-08)
- "it just means that we have to respect wherever our lower intervention points might be." (S022, 2022-07)

## Beginners versus advanced
- **Stance:** Beginners need little: three sessions on fundamentals, low volume, any reasonable program works. Advanced lifters need individual solutions and patience; plateaus are almost always training.
- "It doesn’t take anything fancy and it doesn’t take anything extreme to make really good progress in the beginning" (S003, 2017-12)
- "if, in the cases where it could be nutrition or training, it's always training" (S015, 2023-11)

## Women
- **Stance:** Same principles; women are more sensitive to low energy availability, so diet slower and eat more.
- "Probably should be a little more on the side of airing on eating more versus less and not pushing the boundaries of low energy availability." (S010, 2021-08)

## Aging and lifting after 40
- **Stance:** Most decline is inactivity, not age; naturals peak in their 30s to 50s; be proactive about recovery and injuries.
- "a lot of the things that we associate with age actually have to do with cumulative years of inactivity." (S013, 2025-06)
- "Age is no reason to not compete. Make sure you get a coach, do your homework, and have a strong ‘why.’" (S018, 2024-08)

## TRT and drugs
- **Stance:** No moral judgment of enhanced athletes; natural was his own protective line. TRT is largely marketing driven; fine when properly diagnosed.
- "I'm not against clinically prescribed TRT for hypogonadal men." (S013, 2025-06)
- "So that's why I chose to go the natural route, is to kind of draw a line in the sand that was self-protective." (S011, 2024-03)

## Client communication
- **Stance:** Autonomy-supportive: ask, collaborate, state clearly what data you need; the client is the best window into their own recovery.
- "you can't have a realization for someone else. But you absolutely can assist them" (S014, 2023-05)

## Evidence versus anecdote
- **Stance:** Both have a place: research sets the starting point, coaching experience fills gaps, and converging weak evidence can justify moderate confidence. Loud anecdotes can become false wisdom.
- "individual differences can be amplified by loud or prominent voices and unduly influence what becomes “collective wisdom.”" (S023, 2023-01)
- "the person might be technically wrong for the reason they’re stating, but it’s still good advice." (S005, 2019-02)

## Social media and fitness myths
- **Stance:** Sound bites and "top 10" lists mislead; context matters; influencers profit from leanness and quick fixes; the evidence crowd can be dogmatic too.
- "There aren’t best foods in our rules." (S016, 2016-04)
- "if there's a study on something, you know, therefore, it's actually a commandment of what we must do now" (S014, 2023-05)

## Running a coaching business
- **Stance:** Mission over hype marketing; coaching value is support, context and objectivity, not knowledge; get clients early (a year out for competitors); know your scope.
- "We aren’t trying to blow up or just make a lot of money" (S017, 2013-10)
- "I came to really understand the value of a coach that it's not about knowing science or knowing anything." (S012, 2025-08)
- "a lot of the times we’re inheriting You very bad situations" (S001, 2016-10)
- No recorded position on pricing coaching packages, discounts, or hiring staff.

## Competing in physique sport
- **Stance:** Informed consent, a strong why, an exit plan, compete at most every other year early on; do not compete to fix yourself.
- "we recommend competitors consider competing at most every other year." (S031, 2019-08)
- "you're doing it as, Hey, this would be cool. This would be fun. I wanna see how far I can take this rather than solving something" (S008, 2020-07)
