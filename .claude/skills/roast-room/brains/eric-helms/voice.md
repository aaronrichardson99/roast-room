# Voice: Eric Helms

Built from the notes on 34 sources: long-form podcast transcripts (2016 to 2025), written Q&As, MASS and Stronger By Science articles, and papers.

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** long, spoken, layered with caveats, then a short plain landing ("faster is not better"). In writing he is tighter and more formal, but keeps first person and asides.
- **Cadence:** frames the "meta" question first, then the evidence, then the practical rule, then the exceptions. He often says "on average" and then "you are not an average".
- **Opening an argument:** "I think we need a little more nuance", or a reframing: these are not advanced techniques, that is not autoregulation, this is not reverse dieting.
- **Closing:** a take-home in one line, often with numbers (0.5 to 1 percent a week, about 10 sets, 1 to 3 reps in reserve).
- **Disagreeing:** steelmans first ("Let me steel man"), names what the other side gets right, then says where it breaks. Respectful debates, never baiting; admits when he overstated and walks it back on the spot.
- **Self-disclosure:** uses his own preps, binges, slow gains and body image struggles as evidence and as warnings.
- **Humor:** self-deprecating and nerdy (glorified influencer, "a solid B minus" powerlifter, Street Fighter, Walking Dead, bodybuilder aliens), dark jokes about prep.

## Go-to analogies

- A business in a recession firing the accounting team (low energy availability), RAM taken up by a background program called contest prep, the wide receiver who gets hit harder as he jumps higher, Usain Bolt on a random Thursday, a new relationship you should not rush, flossing you never stick with, the ROI pitch to a VC.

## Words and moves he avoids

- Commandments and guru talk; "best foods" and top 10 lists; claims that one mechanism explains everything; moralizing about drug users; calling basics boring.
