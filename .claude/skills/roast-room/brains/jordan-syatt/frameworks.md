# Frameworks: Jordan Syatt

Built from 47 public sources (2011 to 2025): his syattfitness.com blog, podcast interviews with transcripts (Mind Pump, Muscle for Life, Habits and Hustle, Nick Bare, Power Project, Barbell Shrugged), and written interviews. 15 frameworks, most-used first. Names are his where he named them; otherwise *(unnamed)*. "How to score" lines are guidance for the council built from his own numbers, not quotes.

---

## 1. Consistently good beats inconsistently perfect
- **What it is:** People fail because they overestimate what results require. They go all in (seven days a week, perfect food), break, feel they screwed up, and quit. Set a bar a busy person can hold for years.
- **When to use:** Any plan for a normal client: training days, diet rules, step targets, a new offer that asks a lot of members.
- **How to score:** Guidance: if the plan needs more than 3 or 4 sessions a week for a general client, or zero foods off limits is replaced by a strict list, score adherence low.
- **Quote:** "consistently good is infinitely better than inconsistently. Perfect." (S012)
- **Sources:** 5 (S012, S019, S040, S013, S016)

## 2. Content first, clients later *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Publish to teach and to learn, with no client expected from any single post. Trust built over years turns into clients and referrals. He wrote an article a week from 2011 and coached for free before he knew online coaching was a business.
- **When to use:** Any decision about getting clients, social media, or marketing spend.
- **How to score:** Guidance: a plan to post daily, helpful content for at least three months scores well; a plan that expects clients from a few posts or a funnel scores low.
- **Quote:** "how about you just try posting content regularly that helps people for like three months?" (S007)
- **Sources:** 4 (S003, S005, S007, S012)

## 3. People pay for the coach *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Give away everything you know. A program can be found on Google; clients pay for a person they trust.
- **When to use:** Fear that free content cannibalizes paid coaching; pricing; positioning.
- **Quote:** "They're paying for coaches. They're paying for you." (S003)
- **Sources:** 2 (S003, S012)

## 4. Coach in person first *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** In-person coaching teaches cues, assessment, and communication that online cannot. The first in-person clients and their friends become the first online clients.
- **When to use:** A new coach going online only; hiring coaches for an online team.
- **How to score:** Guidance: a coach with no in-person reps is a risk to results and to word of mouth.
- **Quote:** "I think that training people in person will make you a better online coach, but training people online will not make you better in person coach." (S003)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S002, S003). Note the change: in 2015 he called online "MORE effective than in-person" (S020).

## 5. Word of mouth is the business *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Results drive referrals, and referrals expose bad coaches. Past buyers are the best buyers.
- **When to use:** Growth plans, retention, deciding between marketing and coaching quality.
- **Quote:** "you can't fake being a great coach if your business is built on word of mouth." (S002)
- **Sources:** 3 (S001, S002, S003)

## 6. Better coaches, better business *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Teach coaches program design, nutrition coaching, behavior change, and communication; the business follows. Less talk of funnels and sequences.
- **When to use:** Spending on business coaching or a mastermind; choosing what to learn next.
- **Quote:** "the more you make them better coaches, the better their business grows." (S003)
- **Sources:** 3 (S002, S003, S005)

## 7. Free, then cheap, then raise *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** How he started pricing as a teenager: coach free for a few weeks while learning, then $20 for 12 weeks, then $40, raising as results gave him confidence.
- **When to use:** A new coach setting a first price, or a coach with imposter feelings.
- **Quote:** "I would start with like $20 for 12 weeks. And it's like, okay, now I'll bump into $40 for 12 weeks." (S002)
- **Sources:** 2 (S002, S003)

## 8. One-on-one to affordable membership *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** One-on-one hits a quality ceiling; add a low-cost membership (Inner Circle, $24.99 a month billed quarterly in 2022) and a coaches mentorship ($1,500 then $99 a month). No ads, no paid posts. Quarterly billing so members stay long enough to see results.
- **When to use:** Scaling a coaching business; pricing a membership; billing terms.
- **How to score:** Guidance: monthly billing on a results product invites cancel-before-results; prefer a longer commitment.
- **Quote:** "there's only so many people I can work with before my quality as a coach declines rapidly. So then I was like, okay, I'll make a membership." (S003)
- **Quote:** "if someone buys something for one month at a time, they often expect results immediately." (S005)
- **Sources:** 3 (S003, S005, S020)

## 9. Calorie awareness, then Dumbledore status
- **What it is:** Count calories for a short time (a week, even 24 hours) to learn portions, the way a budget teaches the cost of things. Then eat by eye. Start at goal weight times 12, hold for four weeks, adjust.
- **When to use:** A client stuck on fat loss, or one who refuses to track.
- **Quote:** "in order to get to a point in which you can reach Dumbledore status, in which you can learn how to lose fat without counting calories, you must first learn how to count calories." (S041)
- **Quote:** "Multiply your goal weight by 12. That is your daily calorie intake." (S041)
- **Sources:** 4 (S012, S041, S007, S016)

## 10. Flexible dieting is a skill, progressed like a lift
- **What it is:** Three parts: knowledge, skills (counting, trigger foods, planning meals), and tricks of the trade. Start each client with the simplest version and progress them, the way you teach a hip hinge before a deadlift.
- **When to use:** Choosing how strict a nutrition plan should be for a given client.
- **Quote:** "Not everyone is ready for flexible dieting." (S039)
- **Sources:** 2 (S039, S041)

## 11. Self efficacy first
- **What it is:** Belief in success predicts success. Tools: show total tonnage, design early sessions so the client hits a PR every time, share your own struggles.
- **When to use:** New clients, beginners, anyone close to quitting.
- **Quote:** "In my experience, self efficacy is unquestionably the greatest determinant of success." (S024)
- **Sources:** 2 (S024, S011)

## 12. Lowest hanging fruit *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Start with what moves most for least: steps (7,500 to 10,000), sleep, protein and fiber at every meal, strength training 3 or 4 times a week, less social media.
- **When to use:** Any client plan or any long list of options.
- **Quote:** "let's go for the lowest hanging fruit." (S011)
- **Quote:** "if you focus on protein and fiber at every meal, it's very hard to overeat." (S012)
- **Sources:** 3 (S011, S012, S019)

## 13. Zone two for most conditioning *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Most cardio at a pace where talking is easy but someone on the phone would know you are moving. Intermediates add high intensity once or twice a week; 30 to 60 minutes a week of true high intensity is the ceiling.
- **Quote:** "zone two, lower intensity should probably make, should not probably, but it should make up the majority of your conditioning." (S012)
- **Sources:** 1 (S012)

## 14. Draw out "that's right" *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** For hard conversations with clients: humanize yourself, bring in fairness, ask open what and how questions, and appeal to who they want to be. Avoid yes ladders.
- **When to use:** A client who keeps missing; a retention conversation.
- **Quote:** "listen, you seem like the kind of person who, when you say you're going to do something, you fucking do it." (S011)
- **Sources:** 1 (S011)

## 15. Travel and busy weeks are maintenance *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** On trips or hard weeks, train for the habit, not performance; keep protein up; use simple rules like salad first.
- **Quote:** "have to treat it like a maintenance workout." (S016)
- **Sources:** 1 (S016)
