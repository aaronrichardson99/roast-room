# Frameworks: James Clear

Built from 75 sources (2012 to 2026): his jamesclear.com articles (many are excerpts he posted from Atomic Habits), seven annual reviews, two integrity reports, 3-2-1 newsletters, and nine long interviews with transcripts. 21 frameworks, most-used first. "Sources" counts distinct sources that use the idea (ids listed). Names are his where he named them; otherwise a short name marked *(unnamed)*. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council built from his stated thresholds, not quotes.

---

## 1. The Four Laws of Behavior Change (and their inversion)
- **What it is:** Every habit runs a loop of cue, craving, response, reward. Each stage has a lever: make it obvious, make it attractive, make it easy, make it satisfying. To break a habit, invert them: make it invisible, unattractive, difficult, unsatisfying.
- **Parts:** Cue (environment, visibility), craving (the prediction and the meaning you give the cue; what you crave is the change in state), response (friction and ability), reward (immediate feeling that teaches the brain to repeat). The cardinal rule ties it together: what is immediately rewarded gets repeated.
- **When to use:** Any habit question. Turn the laws into four questions (S016): how can I make it more obvious, attractive, easy, satisfying? He says to start with the first or third law (S074).
- **How to score:** 5 if all four levers point the right way (cue in sight, a version you enjoy, two minutes to start, a reward you feel today); 3 if two are set; 1 if the plan relies on wanting it more.
- **Quote:** "You can think of each law as a lever that influences human behavior." (S016) "behaviors that get immediately rewarded get repeated, behaviors that get immediately punished get avoided." (S074)
- **Sources:** 9 (S003, S004, S005, S016, S021, S029, S074, S075, S076)

## 2. Identity-based habits (every action is a vote)
- **What it is:** Three layers of change: outcomes (what you get), process (what you do), identity (what you believe). Most people start at outcomes. Start at identity and work outward. Habits are evidence; each one is a vote for a type of person.
- **Parts:** Two steps: decide the type of person you want to be, then prove it to yourself with small wins. Phrase it as "the type of person who..." or carry a question: "What would a healthy person do?" (S003). You need a majority of votes, not unanimity. Rewards should vote for the same identity (no ice cream after the gym).
- **When to use:** Resolutions, relapses, any goal stated as a number. Also when someone says they are not the kind of person who does a thing.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan names the identity and a daily vote small enough to cast on a bad day; 3 if it names an identity but no daily proof; 1 if it is pure outcome (a weight, a figure) or asks you to fake a belief with no evidence.
- **Quote:** "Every action you take is a vote for the type of person you wish to become." (S018) "The most practical way to change who you are is to change what you do." (S018)
- **Sources:** 14 (S001, S003, S004, S005, S006, S007, S018, S019, S069, S070, S072, S074, S075, S076)

## 3. Systems over goals
- **What it is:** A goal is the result you want; a system is the collection of daily habits that produces it. Four problems with goals: winners and losers share them, achieving one is a momentary change, they postpone happiness, and they create a finish line after which people stop.
- **Parts:** Use goals for direction, then put them on the shelf. Run the system. Later wording: goals are for winning once, systems for winning repeatedly.
- **When to use:** Goal setting, planning a year, any once-I-get-X-I-will-be-happy thinking.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan is a schedule of actions you can do; 3 if it mixes a deadline with some daily actions; 1 if it is a deadline and a hope.
- **Quote:** "Goals are good for setting a direction, but systems are best for making progress." (S010) "goals are best for people who care about winning once. Systems are best for people who care about winning repeatedly." (S004)
- **Sources:** 11 (S003, S004, S005, S007, S010, S026, S029, S035, S070, S074, S075)

## 4. The Two-Minute Rule (scale it down)
- **What it is:** When you start a new habit, it should take less than two minutes. Read 30 books a year becomes read one page. Master showing up first; scale up later.
- **Parts:** Find the gateway habit on a scale from very easy to very hard. Optional variation: do it for two minutes and then stop. Related: the three rules (start incredibly easy, increase in incredibly small steps, keep every set easy) and setting an upper limit at the start (S027, S008).
- **When to use:** Someone keeps failing to start, plans too big, or is a beginner.
- **How to score:** 5 if day one could be done sick and tired; 3 if it is moderate; 1 if day one is the full ideal routine.
- **Quote:** "When you start a new habit, it should take less than two minutes to do." (S013) "a habit must be established before it can be improved." (S013)
- **Sources:** 12 (S003, S004, S008, S013, S018, S022, S027, S029, S046, S063, S074, S076)

## 5. Environment design (choice architecture)
- **What it is:** Environment is the invisible hand behind behavior. People choose things because of where they are, not what they are. Design the default.
- **Parts:** Automate good decisions; get in the flow (gym on the way home); subtract negative cues; add steps between you and bad habits, remove steps to good ones; put the guitar stand in the living room; phone in another room; a new context (a chair) for a new habit; home court before away court. Question to ask in each room: what is this space designed to encourage?
- **When to use:** Willpower failures, diet, phones, drinking, focus.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan changes what is visible and easy in the rooms where the person spends time; 3 if one change; 1 if it relies on resisting a cue that stays in view.
- **Quote:** "Environment is the invisible hand that shapes human behavior." (S014) "in the long run, your willpower will almost never beat your environment." (S076)
- **Sources:** 15 (S003, S004, S005, S014, S015, S017, S023, S029, S032, S047, S048, S053, S071, S074, S076)

## 6. Never miss twice (plan for failure)
- **What it is:** Slipping once is human; the spiral is the problem. Get back on track at the next chance.
- **Parts:** Plan the comeback in advance (if-then plans for each likely disruption, a pre-mortem a year out); contain the damage to a quarter of the day; treat a lapse as a data point; average speed, not maximum speed.
- **When to use:** After a lapse, a holiday, an injury, a new baby, travel.
- **How to score:** 5 if there is a written plan for the first missed day; 3 if the person shrugs and restarts within a week; 1 if one slip ends the effort.
- **Quote:** "Missing once is an accident. Missing twice is the start of a new habit." (S022) "if the reclaiming of your habit is fast, the breaking of it doesn't matter that much." (S076)
- **Sources:** 12 (S004, S008, S011, S017, S022, S023, S028, S051, S074, S076, S062, S007)

## 7. Reduce the scope, stick to the schedule
- **What it is:** When time or energy is short, do a smaller version at the planned time instead of skipping. Do not throw up a zero.
- **Parts:** 15 minutes instead of 45 (a couple of sets of squats); one mile instead of three; consistency as flexibility (the willow bends, the oak breaks); the bad days matter more than the good days.
- **When to use:** Busy seasons, parenting, travel, illness.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan has a named minimum version; 1 if it is all or nothing.
- **Quote:** "I give myself permission to reduce the scope but stick to the schedule." (S003) "in real life, I think consistency is often adaptability, it's flexibility." (S006)
- **Sources:** 7 (S003, S004, S006, S007, S008, S023, S062)

## 8. Habits as compound interest; the plateau of latent potential
- **What it is:** 1 percent better each day for a year is about 37 times better; 1 percent worse falls to near zero. Results lag; the work is stored, like heating an ice cube from 25 to 31 degrees before it melts at 32.
- **Parts:** Focus on trajectory, not position; separate 1 percent gains that accumulate from those that evaporate; time magnifies whatever you feed it.
- **When to use:** Someone wants to quit because nothing shows yet; comparing options with slow versus fast payoff.
- **How to score:** 5 if the action accumulates into something larger over years and the person is on a rising slope; 1 if it is a one-time burst or a gain that evaporates.
- **Quote:** "Habits are the compound interest of self-improvement." (S011) "the work isn't wasted. It's just being stored." (S005)
- **Sources:** 10 (S003, S004, S005, S011, S029, S055, S062, S069, S071, S075)

## 9. Four Burners and seasons of life
- **What it is:** Life has four burners: family, friends, health, work. To be really good at one or two, others go down. Options: outsource a burner, embrace constraints, or rotate through seasons of imbalance.
- **Parts:** Ask what season you are in and which burner is up. Habits change shape with the season (his writing went from twice-weekly articles, to a book, to a weekly newsletter; his training from 90 minutes before kids to 15 to 45). Life comes in roughly ten-year movements that can be sequenced.
- **When to use:** Work-life balance, new parent, career push, can I have it all.
- **How to score:** 5 if the person names the burner they are turning down and accepts the cost; 3 if they plan a season but hope to keep everything; 1 if they insist on all four on high.
- **Quote:** "You can do it all in a lifetime, but not at the same damn time." (S033) "when your seasons change, your habits often need to change with it." (S004)
- **Sources:** 7 (S004, S006, S033, S059, S068, S075, S063)

## 10. How do I want to spend my days? (draw the box first)
- **What it is:** Decide the daily lifestyle you want, draw a box around it, then chase money, reach, and impact only inside the box. Most people do it in reverse and talk themselves into a life they do not enjoy.
- **Parts:** Companion questions: what am I optimizing for? what season am I in? Optimize for time over money once you have enough. A promotion that worsens daily life is a bad trade.
- **When to use:** Career moves, side businesses, promotions, growth decisions, saying yes to opportunities.
- **How to score:** 5 if the option fits the days the person wants; 1 if it trades a good daily life for status.
- **Quote:** "rather than optimizing for money, let’s optimize for time, so how do I want to spend my days?" (S003) "to want the outcome without the lifestyle is to like torture yourself." (S004)
- **Sources:** 6 (S003, S004, S006, S007, S075, S062)

## 11. Saying no: no is a decision, yes is a responsibility
- **What it is:** Yes commits a future block of time (time debt); no keeps options open (time credit). As success grows, upgrade your no: refuse good opportunities to make room for great ones.
- **Parts:** Tests: Tim Harford's question, would I agree to it if I had to do it today (S037); Derek Sivers' hell yeah or no; Buffett's 25/5 list; Eisenhower box; "You’re not focused enough unless you’re mourning some of the things you’re saying no to." (S070)
- **When to use:** Over-commitment, new opportunities, focus.
- **How to score:** 5 if the yes is exciting enough to drop today's work for; 1 if it is a yes to avoid seeming rude.
- **Quote:** "No is a decision. Yes is a responsibility." (S037) "If you can learn to say no to bad distractions, then eventually you’ll earn the right to say no to good opportunities." (S037)
- **Sources:** 9 (S003, S007, S036, S037, S038, S058, S062, S063, S070)

## 12. Social environment: join groups where your desired behavior is normal
- **What it is:** We belong to many tribes, large and small, each with norms. Habits that fit the group feel attractive; habits that cut against it are hard. The topic he says he undersold in the book.
- **Parts:** Join or create the group (a yoga studio, a book club, author retreats he hosted); people you agree with on 98 percent of topics change your mind most; accountability works best from people whose opinion costs you something (a coach, a neighbor), less from strangers or close family.
- **When to use:** Drinking, fitness, any habit your household or friends do not share.
- **How to score:** 5 if the person will spend regular time with people who already do the habit; 1 if they plan to do it alone against the group.
- **Quote:** "most of the time, the desire to belong will overpower the desire to improve." (S003) "I think I undersold the importance of social environment." (S076)
- **Sources:** 8 (S003, S004, S017, S050, S056, S074, S075, S076)

## 13. What would this look like if it was fun?
- **What it is:** The question he would add to Atomic Habits. Choose the version of a habit you genuinely enjoy, not the one you think you should do.
- **Parts:** Brainstorm many versions (lift, kayak, climb, yoga) and pick the most fun; temptation bundling (the thing you need paired with the thing you want); a party in a bowl salad; grit is fit (David Epstein).
- **When to use:** Exercise resolutions, reading, meditation, anything done out of guilt.
- **How to score:** 5 if the person would look forward to it; 1 if they already dread it.
- **Quote:** "The person who's having fun is actually the person who's dangerous." (S004) "one of my little rules for life is the person who has the most fun wins." (S075)
- **Sources:** 8 (S003, S004, S006, S021, S041, S074, S075, S076)

## 14. Habit stacking and implementation intentions
- **What it is:** Give a habit a time and a place. The formula "I will [BEHAVIOR] at [TIME] in [LOCATION]." (S020) Or stack it: "After/Before [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT]." (S012) Credits BJ Fogg.
- **Parts:** Pick a specific cue with the same frequency as the habit, at a time you control; if-then plans for disruptions; record the habit right after doing it.
- **When to use:** Vague intentions such as eat better or read more.
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan names the exact cue, time, and place; 1 if it says soon or more.
- **Quote:** "Many people think they lack motivation when what they really lack is clarity." (S020) "Soon is not a time and some is not a number." (S023)
- **Sources:** 7 (S004, S012, S020, S021, S022, S023, S046)

## 15. Reflection and review (Annual Review, Integrity Report)
- **What it is:** The meta habit. Annual Review: what went well, what didn't, what am I working toward (later: what did I learn). Integrity Report: what are my core values, am I living them, how can I set a higher standard. A 20-minute Friday business review.
- **Parts:** Check calendar, workout journal, and output against stated values; questions such as "Does this activity fill me with energy or drain me?" (S003) and "Can my current habits carry me to my desired future?" (S003)
- **When to use:** Year-end, feeling stuck, drift.
- **How to score:** 5 if the decision comes out of a written review against values; 1 if no one has looked up from the trenches.
- **Quote:** "reflection review is almost like the meta-habit that is above all others" (S004) "It’s so unlikely that you’ll be stumbling into the optimal way to live." (S075)
- **Sources:** 13 (S003, S004, S006, S038, S057, S058, S059, S060, S061, S062, S063, S064, S065)

## 16. The 3 Stages of Failure
- **What it is:** A way to decide when to quit. Failure of tactics (how): record, measure, adjust. Failure of strategy (what): launch quickly, cheaply, revise rapidly. Failure of vision (why): take stock, set a non-negotiable, navigate criticism.
- **Parts:** Most supposed vision failures are strategy failures you quit on because you felt like it. Be firm on the vision, flexible on the version.
- **When to use:** Should I give up, on a business, a sport, a relationship, a plan.
- **How to score:** Quit tactics freely; quit strategies quickly and cheaply; quit a vision only after a values review.
- **Quote:** "telling someone to never give up is terrible advice. Successful people give up all the time." (S044) "Most of the mistakes that people assume are Failures of Vision are actually Failures of Strategy." (S044)
- **Sources:** 2 (S044, S033)

## 17. Hats, haircuts, and tattoos *(his name)*
- **What it is:** Sort decisions by reversibility. Hats: try and swap. Haircuts: live with it a month. Tattoos: permanent; think hard.
- **When to use:** Any decision someone is stuck on.
- **How to score:** If reversible, decide today; if a tattoo, slow down and review.
- **Quote:** "If the choice is easy to reverse, speed is most important. You should move fast." (S004) "It's very rare that you end up with a tattoo. But we treat them all like tattoos." (S004)
- **Sources:** 3 (S004, S073, S063)

## 18. 10 years and one hour
- **What it is:** The two timeframes that matter. Keep the ten-year aim in mind and ask what to do in the next hour; separate small gains that accumulate from those that evaporate.
- **Quote:** "the two timeframes that matter most in life are 10 years and one hour." (S004) "What can I do in the next hour that moves me toward where I want to be 10 years from now?" (S076)
- **Sources:** 3 (S004, S076, S073)

## 19. Goldilocks Rule and progress feedback
- **What it is:** Peak motivation comes from tasks of just manageable difficulty, plus immediate feedback. Track progress (habit tracker, paper clips, an X on a calendar).
- **Quote:** "The Goldilocks Rule states that humans experience peak motivation when working on tasks that are right on the edge of their current abilities. Not too hard. Not too easy. Just right." (S025) "The most effective form of motivation is progress." (S022)
- **Sources:** 6 (S004, S022, S025, S031, S041, S074)

## 20. Absolute versus relative success; the slope
- **What it is:** Luck decides most extreme outcomes; habits and choices decide outcomes relative to people with similar luck. You control the slope, not the starting point.
- **When to use:** Comparison, envy, privilege debates, someone who feels behind.
- **Quote:** "Absolute success is luck. Relative success is choices and habits." (S055) "You can only control the slope of your success, not your initial position." (S055)
- **Sources:** 3 (S055, S062, S075)

## 21. ABZ and working backwards from magic *(ABZ credited to Sean Puri)*
- **What it is:** Know Z (where you want to end up), be honest about A (where you are), choose a directionally correct B. Brainstorm the magical outcome before reality says no.
- **Quote:** "I actually don't need to know C through Y right now." (S074) "most people become their own bottleneck long before reality prevents them from doing it" (S074)
- **Sources:** 1 (S074)
