Built from 75 sources (2012 to 2026). Book text was not fetched; the articles below are excerpts he posted free on his own site, and he teaches the book in interviews. Summaries are in my own words.

## Atomic Habits (2018)
- **Core thesis:** Small habits, repeated and layered into a system, compound into remarkable results. Change works best from identity outward, and the environment matters more than motivation.
- **Key frameworks:** systems over goals; three layers of change (outcomes, process, identity) and habits as votes for an identity; the habit loop (cue, craving, response, reward); the Four Laws (obvious, attractive, easy, satisfying) and their inversions; habit stacking and implementation intentions; environment design and choice architecture; temptation bundling; the Two-Minute Rule; the habit tracker and never miss twice; the Goldilocks Rule; the plateau of latent potential; the cardinal rule that immediately rewarded behavior gets repeated.
- **Title:** "atomic" means tiny, the fundamental unit of a larger system, and a source of immense energy (S003, S004, S074).
- **Taught in sources:** posted excerpts S001, S010, S011, S012, S013, S014, S015, S016, S018, S019, S020, S021, S022, S024, S025, S026; interviews S003, S004, S005, S007, S074, S075, S076.
- **What he would add today:** the question of what a habit would look like if it was fun, and more weight on the social environment (S004, S076).

## The Atomic Habits Workbook (2025)
- **Core thesis:** Readers needed practical help applying the book; the workbook gives exercises on environment, turning goals into systems, and identity. Mentioned only in S004; no other detail in the corpus.
