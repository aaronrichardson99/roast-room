# Frameworks: Brian Moran and Michael Lennington

Built from 29 public sources (2013 to 2022): 22 posts on the 12weekyear.com blog (20 by Moran, 2 by Lennington), three long Moran interviews, one interview with both authors, and their own site copy. No book text. Each quote names its author. "Sources" counts distinct sources that use the idea. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council built from their stated thresholds, not quotes. Part A is the method in the order a person runs it. Part B is the supporting frameworks. Part C lists what the corpus does not cover, so a field guide writer knows what to check against the books.

---

# Part A: the method, step by step

## 1. The 12 Week Year (12 weeks is the year)
- **What it is:** Throw out the annual plan. Treat each 12 week stretch as a full year with its own goals, plan, deadline, and review. Not a quarter (a quarter is a fraction of a year and keeps the "I can catch up" mindset) and not a year crammed into 12 weeks.
- **Parts:** A hard deadline every 12 weeks; goals set every 12 weeks and tied to a longer vision; a tactical plan; a weekly plan and score; a 13th week to review, plan, and celebrate.
- **When to use:** Any goal that will otherwise drift: a body composition change, a launch, a savings target, a habit.
- **How to score:** 5 if there is a fixed 12 week end date, one to three goals, and a plan with weekly due dates; 3 if there is a goal and a deadline but no weekly plan; 1 if it is an annual goal or a resolution.
- **Quote:** Moran: "12 weeks is a long enough time to make profound progress but near enough where we don’t lose sight of the deadlines" (S023) Moran: "you might catch up on the goal. But you can’t go re-capture the capacity left on the table last week, last month" (S022)
- **Why not a year:** Moran: "the biggest one is that there’s this illusion of lots of time" (S022) Moran: "In January, December looks a long way off" (S014)
- **Sources:** 8 (S011, S013, S014, S018, S019, S022, S023, S047)

## 2. The five disciplines (vision, planning, process control, measurement, time use)
- **What it is:** The fundamentals of execution they bundled with the 12 week cycle. Moran's Inc. walk-through runs them in order: begin with a vision, create a plan, set your process, get some metrics, take control of your time (S014).
- **Parts:** Vision (what you want, life first). Planning (a tactical 12 week plan). Process control (the weekly routines and tools that keep you on the plan). Measurement (lead and lag indicators plus a weekly execution score). Time use (blocking time for the strategic work).
- **When to use:** As a checklist. When a 12 week year is failing, find the missing discipline.
- **How to score:** One point per discipline in place. 5 of 5 is a working system; 2 or fewer is a wish list.
- **Quote:** Moran: "there’s a set of five disciplines. And so when you ask first step, the first discipline is vision." (S023) Moran: "one is process control, which is just our term for those systems and events that you can lean on. So the days you don't feel like doing it, you still do it." (S047)
- **Sources:** 4 (S014, S022, S023, S047)

## 3. Vision first, life before work
- **What it is:** Step one. Write what you want your whole life to look like at three, five, and ten years, then what great (not good) looks like, then what work or business has to do to enable it. The 12 week goal must lean into that vision.
- **Parts:** Long-term life vision; three year vision; then the business or career vision; keep it in front of you daily. For teams, every person (even part-timers) does a personal vision first.
- **When to use:** Before any goal. When motivation fades mid-cycle, go back here, not to a new plan.
- **How to score:** 5 if the 12 week goal visibly serves a written life vision the person can say in a sentence; 3 if there is a goal but the "why" is vague; 1 if the goal is borrowed or "should" based.
- **Quote:** Moran: "what do you want your life to look like three years from now, five years from now, 10 years from now, by the way, what does great look like?" (S022) Moran: "It’s not bluff. It’s the cornerstone of all high performance." (S022) Moran: "not just creating it, it’s staying connected to it, because that’s where it has its power" (S023)
- **Sources:** 7 (S004, S009, S011, S014, S022, S023, S047)

## 4. 12 week goals: one is better than two
- **What it is:** Set the fewest goals that would change things. Limited capacity means more goals make you mediocre at all of them.
- **Parts:** One to three goals for the cycle, often one business and one personal. Each goal is a measurable outcome. Pick the one thing with the greatest impact, nail it, and repeat next cycle.
- **When to use:** Every planning session; whenever someone wants to add a goal mid-cycle.
- **How to score:** 5 for one or two measurable goals; 3 for three; 1 for four or more, or goals that are not measurable.
- **Quote:** Moran: "One’s better than two, two’s better than three. When you start to get over three, you’re stuck the odds against yourself." (S022) Moran: "the more goals you try and pursue simultaneously, the greater the likelihood that you’re mediocre at everything" (S022)
- **Sources:** 5 (S011, S014, S022, S023, S047)

## 5. The tactical 12 week plan
- **What it is:** For each goal, a list of tactics. A tactic is a sentence that describes an action a person takes, with a due week. Concepts ("build referrals") do not execute; actions ("ask for referrals at every meeting") do.
- **Parts:** Goal; tactics written as verbs a person does; owner; due week (week 1, week 5, or weeks 1 to 12 for recurring actions); a mix of one-time and recurring tactics. If you do not know how, the first tactics are to learn or hire (join a gym, hire a trainer).
- **Worked example (Moran, S022):** goal: lose weight to a target body fat. Tactics: download a food app (week 1, one time); log food daily (weeks 1 to 12); order and take supplements; lift four times a week; cardio twice a week; hire a trainer and join a gym if new to training.
- **When to use:** Right after the goals. Revisit when results lag.
- **How to score:** 5 if every line is a discrete action with a week; 3 if half the lines are concepts; 1 if the plan is a list of intentions.
- **Quote:** Moran: "Most plans are conceptual. Concepts don’t execute, you’ve got to get tactical." (S022) Moran: "each tactic in the plan has a due date. That due date is a particular week, week one, week five, weeks one through 12" (S022) Moran: "‘Ask for referrals at every meeting,’ versus ‘Build referrals,'" (S014) Moran: "it’s sort of called squeezes out all the wiggle room, which makes it really uncomfortable By the way" (S023)
- **Sources:** 5 (S014, S022, S023, S047, S011)

## 6. The weekly plan (win the week)
- **What it is:** Each week, copy only the tactics due that week from the 12 week plan. That list is the week. Everything in it is priority A; everything else is B. Then put each item on the calendar.
- **Parts:** Pull tactics due this week; calendarize them by day and time; when a gap opens, go to the weekly plan, not email; at week's end, score it.
- **When to use:** Every week, before the week starts.
- **How to score:** 5 if the weekly plan exists before Monday and every item has a time; 3 if it exists but is not scheduled; 1 if the week is run from the inbox.
- **Quote:** Moran: "the weekly plan is a 1/12th slice of the 12-week plan" (S022) Moran: "By default, everything in the weekly plan is priority A, and everything else is priority B." (S014) Moran: "You get this stuff done, you win the week." (S022)
- **Sources:** 3 (S002, S014, S022)

## 7. The weekly scorecard and the 85 percent rule
- **What it is:** Score execution, not results, every week. The score is how much of what you said mattered most you actually did. 85 percent or better and you will usually hit the goal. Perfection is not the target; consistency is.
- **Scorecard rules (from the sources):**
  - Score weekly, on your own execution of the weekly plan (S002, S022).
  - Score actions, because you control actions, not outcomes (S014, S022).
  - Track results too, as lead and lag indicators, so you can tell a bad plan from bad execution (S014).
  - The target is 85 percent or better, not 100 (S002).
  - A low week gets a candid look and no guilt; then you win the next week (S022, S043).
  - Execution score as tactics completed divided by tactics due that week is our reading of "how well you did what you said was most important" (inferred from S002, S022).
- **When to use:** End of every week, before the peer meeting.
- **How to score:** 5 for a running weekly score averaging 85 percent or more; 3 for 65 to 84 percent, or scoring done irregularly; 1 for no score (guidance, using their 85 percent line).
- **Quote:** Unsigned blog: "Establish a system to measure your individual execution on a weekly basis. The Weekly Plan & Scorecard is designed for that purpose." (S002) Unsigned blog: "You don’t need to be perfect, just consistent and persistent. Typically, if you and your organization score 85% or better you will achieve your objectives." (S002) Moran: "We control our actions, not their outcomes." (S014)
- **Sources:** 4 (S002, S014, S022, S023)

## 8. Lead and lag indicators, and plan versus execution
- **What it is:** Results lag. Measure the leading actions and early signals so you know whether the plan works before the result shows. When results miss, check execution first: most of the time the plan was not run.
- **Parts:** Lag indicator (sales, weight lost, money saved); lead indicators (appointments, store or site traffic, workouts done, meals logged); the execution score; then the diagnosis: flawed plan or flawed execution.
- **When to use:** Any time someone wants to change the plan after a few weeks.
- **How to score:** 5 if they can show execution above 85 percent before blaming the plan; 1 if they are rewriting a plan they have not run.
- **Quote:** Moran: "Measurement is the anchor of reality" (S014) Moran: "65 percent of the time, the problem is the execution" (S014) Moran: "most of the breakdowns aren't in the plan, most of the breakdowns are at the execution level" (S047)
- **Field Guide rules (S053, the user's own copy, Ch. 5 Scorekeeping):** track results daily, weekly, and monthly; build a set of key measures that mix lead and lag; keep it to the top one or two of each. Book quotes: "Lead indicators are measures that show early progress toward the end result, while lag indicators often are the end results." (S053) "don't get carried away with too many metrics. Pick just the top one or two of each for you to track." (S053) "a week lost is a month lost" (S053) For weight loss, the book's example lead is weekly exercise hours and the example lags are waist size or pounds lost (S053).
- **Sources:** 5 (S002, S007, S014, S047, S053)

## 9. The weekly accountability meeting *(the book calls it the WAM; the corpus describes it but never uses the term)*
- **What it is:** A short weekly meeting with a few peers who are also working a 12 week year. Each person reports their execution and progress. It works through productive tension: showing up with a low score does not feel good.
- **Format (from the sources):**
  - Two or three peers striving for something meaningful (S011).
  - Weekly, 15 to 30 minutes (S011).
  - Each person reports their execution (score) and their goal progress (S011).
  - The group challenges, gives feedback and suggestions, and encourages (S011).
  - No one is there to "hold you accountable" or punish. The question is how do we get better this week (S047, S022).
  - Look forward, not in the rear-view mirror: learn from last week, then commit to next week (S043, S047).
- **Why it works:** Moran: "we will let ourselves down easier than we will if someone's looking in someone else's watching" (S047) Moran: "I'm either going to drop out of that meeting, or by gosh, this week, I'm going to do what I said I'm going to do" (S047)
- **Quote:** Moran: "Find two or three other people that are striving to accomplish something meaningful and create a peer group. Meet weekly for 15-30 minutes to discuss how you are doing with your actions (your execution) and with your goal." (S011) Moran: "Peer support can increase your odds of sticking with your actions by nearly 80%!" (S011)
- **How to score:** 5 for a standing weekly meeting with peers who report scores; 3 for an informal check-in; 1 for going alone.
- **Sources:** 4 (S011, S022, S043, S047)

## 10. Time blocking: strategic blocks *(buffer and breakout blocks are named in the book, not in this corpus)*
- **What it is:** Schedule the strategic work first, in protected blocks with email, phone, and door shut, then fit other work around it. Most people do the reverse.
- **Parts (from the sources):** Strategic block: long, uninterrupted, for work that pays off later (S014). Calendarize the weekly plan by day and time (S022). Lennington: block realistic writing time and only write in it (S043). Moran: inside a block, separate starting from finishing (S043).
- **When to use:** Building the model week at the start of a cycle; whenever "no time" is the excuse.
- **How to score:** 5 if strategic blocks are on the calendar every week and kept; 3 if planned but often broken; 1 if strategic work waits for leftover time.
- **Quote:** Moran: "Most people use their time the way they save money" (S014) Moran: "Carve out a time when you turn off your email, shut off your phone, and lock your door" (S014) Moran: "Most of the urgent stuff isn’t as urgent as we make it" (S014)
- **Sources:** 3 (S014, S022, S043)

## 11. The 13th week: review, plan, celebrate
- **What it is:** Twelve weeks of 13 leaves one week each quarter of the calendar. Use it to review the last 12 weeks, plan the next 12, celebrate, rest, or catch up if needed (a flex week). Teams go off site and revisit the shared vision.
- **Parts:** Review the scores and results with candor; learn; celebrate progress; revisit the vision; write the next plan; take time off (Moran plans a vacation every 13 weeks, S019).
- **When to use:** Every week 13.
- **How to score:** 5 if review, plan, and celebration are scheduled; 1 if the next cycle starts with no review.
- **Quote:** Moran: "that 13th week is part of the 12-week year, it’s a flex week" (S022) Moran: "What you celebrate matters. It creates focus, it creates culture, it creates momentum." (S022) Moran: "The 12 Week Year sets up nicely for a vacation every thirteen weeks." (S019)
- **Sources:** 4 (S013, S018, S019, S022)

## 12. What goes wrong across the 12 weeks *(their claims plus our reading; see Part C)*
- **Week 1 (the start):** New results need actions you have not done before, so the plan feels uncomfortable from the first week. Plans that hold too much leave people overwhelmed at the start and scattered later. Moran: "if you’re creating a new result, there’s stuff in there that you haven’t done before. So, there’s discomfort, there’s uncertainty" (S022) Moran: "Most plans have too much in them." (S014) Fix: cut to one or two goals, write tactics as actions, and score the first week anyway.
- **The middle (around week 6, our reading):** The corpus names a "valley of despair" inside any growth effort, where people are tempted to abandon the plan (S033). It does not tie it to a week number. Results lag effort (S007), and a low week tempts people to rewrite the plan instead of executing it (S014). Fix: reconnect with the vision (S004, S023), check the execution score before changing the plan (S014), lean on the peer meeting (S047), and do not try to catch up on missed weeks; win this week (S022). Unsigned site copy: "Within any growth opportunity, there is a “valley of despair,” in which we’re tempted to turn around and abandon our plans." (S033)
- **Week 12 (the end):** The deadline is the point. The near end date creates the healthy urgency that annual plans only produce in November and December. Moran: "not stressed out urgency, like you might be in November, December" (S023) The risk at the end is setting the next target without stopping. Moran: "We want to set the next there and the next there." (S022) Fix: use the 13th week to review and celebrate.
- **Any week:** A bad week ends after a week; a bad cycle ends after 12 weeks. Moran: "the longest it’s going to go is 12 weeks, where you get a fresh start" (S022)
- **Sources:** 9 (S004, S007, S014, S022, S023, S033, S043, S047, S011)

# Part B: supporting frameworks

## 13. Accountability as ownership, not consequences
- **What it is:** Accountability is owning your choices, not being punished. You always have a choice, and you largely choose your consequences. The core of Uncommon Accountability (2022) and one of the 12 Week Year's principles.
- **Parts:** Free will choice; ownership of the choice; look forward, not back; no guilt, no blame; learn and act next week.
- **When to use:** Missed weeks, blame, "I need someone to hold me accountable".
- **How to score:** 5 if the person talks about their own choices and next actions; 1 if they talk about circumstances and other people.
- **Quote:** Moran: "Accountability is ownership. It’s the recognition that we have free will choice." (S022) Moran: "saying I need someone to hold me accountable was probably the least accountable thing you could say" (S022) Moran: "so much of accountability is forward-thinking not rear view mirror. And I think the victim mindset is all rear view mirror." (S043) Lennington: "accountability is, it sort of has two definitions" (S043)
- **Where they differ:** Lennington grants the consequence model some power: "People use accountability as consequences because it creates results. But it gets you so far" (S043). The book site, in Moran's voice, is absolute: "there will be collateral damage 100% of the time… and it's unpredictable" (S046).
- **Sources:** 6 (S022, S043, S046, S047, S052, S004)

## 14. Hold people capable (confront with choice)
- **What it is:** For leaders, parents, coaches. Stop holding people accountable with escalating consequences. Agree on the goal and the actions, then confront the person with their choices and the consequences of each.
- **Parts:** Agreement at the goal level; agreement at the action level (tactical, not vague); when actions are missed, name it, then ask what their choices are and what each leads to; accept that leaving may be the right choice ("a bad fit", not a bad person).
- **When to use:** Coaching a client who skips sessions, leading staff, family agreements.
- **How to score:** 5 if expectations are specific actions both sides agreed to; 1 if expectations are vague and enforcement is by pressure.
- **Quote:** Moran: "the key is to confront people with the freedom of choice, and the consequences of those choices" (S047) Moran: "This approach isn't passive, it's very confrontive." (S047) Moran: "no one is going to sign up and take ownership of something that's vague" (S047) Moran: "stop trying to hold them accountable and start holding them capable" (S046)
- **Sources:** 3 (S046, S047, S052)

## 15. Clarity, transparency, evidence (three structures)
- **What it is:** For any team or group trying to perform: clear expectations (vision, goals, and above all actions), visibility of who did what, and evidence of whether it is producing.
- **When to use:** A family, a training group, a business team running a 12 week year together.
- **How to score:** One point for each structure in place; 3 of 3 is a team that can execute.
- **Quote:** Moran: "You need clarity of expectation at the vision level at the goal level, but most importantly, at the behavioral level" (S047) Moran: "we need transparency with respect to execution" (S047) Site copy: "A high-performance culture requires clarity of expectation, transparency with regard to execution, and evidence of impact." (S052)
- **Sources:** 3 (S022, S047, S052)

## 16. Four keys to commitment (interest versus commitment)
- **What it is:** Moran's test for whether a goal will survive the hard weeks.
- **Parts:** 1. Strong desire (a personal why). 2. Clear actions (one or two keystone actions). 3. Count the costs (time, money, risk, comfort) before you start. 4. Act on commitments, not feelings.
- **When to use:** Before adding a goal to the plan; when someone keeps restarting.
- **How to score:** 5 if all four are explicit; 1 if the person has only the desire.
- **Quote:** Moran: "When you’re interested in doing something, you only do it when circumstances permit. But, when you’re committed to something, you accept no excuses, only results." (S012) Moran: "Learning to do the things you know you need to do regardless of how you feel is a core discipline for success." (S012) Moran: "if I have to decide it in the moment, in my warm bed, with my head on the pillow, the odds are against me getting out and getting after it" (S047)
- **Sources:** 4 (S011, S012, S047, S003)

## 17. Five steps to achieving your goals (the resolution replacement)
- **What it is:** Moran's January answer to New Year's resolutions, which he says fail 92 percent of the time (S011).
- **Parts:** 1. Connect with the why. 2. Identify the keystone action and focus on it. 3. Count the costs. 4. Engage in peer support (weekly, 15 to 30 minutes). 5. Work in 12 week cycles.
- **When to use:** Anyone setting a resolution or a yearly goal.
- **Quote:** Moran: "Successful commitment is the product of caring about something more than your own comfort." (S011) Moran: "It creates an illusion that there is lots of time, which often results in procrastination, discouragement and failure." (S011)
- **Sources:** 2 (S011, S012)

## 18. The keystone action and a little extra
- **What it is:** A few core actions produce most of the result. Find the one or two, protect them, and know that one extra rep on a keystone action is cheap and compounds, while skipping one costs a lot.
- **Quote:** Moran: "skipping a workout adds very little additional free time to my week, but takes away significantly from the total time I’ve dedicated to training" (S015) Moran: "the hardest part of the workout is showing up" (S015)
- **Limit:** Moran: "dedicating more time is not always the best way to create a higher level of performance" (S015)
- **Sources:** 3 (S011, S012, S015)

## 19. Productive tension
- **What it is:** The useful discomfort of seeing your real score, alone or in front of peers. It changes behavior without guilt.
- **Quote:** Moran: "We call that productive tension, which causes me to behave differently next time." (S022)
- **Sources:** 2 (S022, S047)

## 20. Systems over willpower (process control) *(Lennington's systems post)*
- **What it is:** Discipline runs out, so build systems and routines that carry you on bad days. The first system is the execution system; then build one new system per 12 week year.
- **Quote:** Lennington: "A system is a coordinated sequence of actions and events that create consistent outcomes over time." (S005) Lennington: "each 12 Week Year thereafter, determine which system you will work on and develop a 12 Week Plan to create and install it" (S005) Moran: "it's like a fuel tank. And every time you use it, it depletes the tank" (S047)
- **Sources:** 4 (S005, S008, S047, S003)

## 21. Floodlight versus laser beam
- **What it is:** Effort spread across everything versus the same effort on the critical few.
- **Quote:** Moran: "It’s the difference Dan between a floodlight and a laser beam. They’re both light." (S023)
- **Sources:** 2 (S023, S022)

## 22. The knowing-doing gap
- **What it is:** The premise under everything: people rarely lack ideas; they fail to act on what they know.
- **Quote:** Site copy: "There is a gap for most of us between what we know we should do, and what we are actually willing to do." (S030) Moran: "the breakdown is never not knowing, it’s not doing" (S022) Moran: "Knowledge is only power if you act on it." (S014)
- **Sources:** 6 (S001, S014, S022, S023, S030, S049)

# Part C: not in this corpus (check the books before you write it up)

The seat brief and the books use these terms, but no source here contains them, so the brain has no verbatim support. Agents must not quote them.
- **"Greatness is in the moment of choice"** and the principle of greatness in the moment. Only third-party show notes mention it; none were kept.
- **Buffer blocks and breakout blocks** by name, the length of a strategic block, and the **model week** by name. The corpus has strategic blocks and calendarizing only (S014, S022).
- **"WAM"** as a term and any fixed agenda beyond Part A item 9.
- **The emotional cycle of change** stage names and their week timing. The corpus has only the "valley of despair" (S033).
- **The three principles** listed as a set (the corpus has accountability and commitment, not greatness in the moment).
- **Lennington's own versions** of most of the method. Only two of his posts and one joint interview were reachable; his podcast and talks were not.
