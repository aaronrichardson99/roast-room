# Frameworks: Brad Schoenfeld

18 frameworks, roughly most-used first. Built from 45 sources: his blog (2010 to 2024), long interviews with transcripts (2010 to 2024), and his open-access papers (2010 to 2023). "Sources" counts distinct sources that use the idea (ids listed). Names are his where he named them; otherwise a short name marked *(unnamed)*. The How-to-score lines are scoring guidance for the council built from his stated thresholds, not quotes.

---

## 1. Evidence-based practice: research, expertise, and the individual
- **What it is:** Decide from the best available evidence, filtered through practitioner experience and the client's goals and preferences. Research gives guidelines; it never writes the program alone.
- **Parts:** Find the best evidence; check who was studied and how it was measured; downgrade weak designs; add expertise; respect the client's values; experiment on the individual.
- **When to use:** Every training or nutrition decision, and any time someone cites "a study" or "it worked for me".
- **How to score:** 5 if the plan rests on controlled human studies in a similar population and is tailored to the person; 3 if it rests on logic plus a few small or untrained-subject studies; 1 if it rests on a guru, an animal study, or an abstract read in isolation.
- **Quote:** "evidence-based practice is not simply a matter of knowing the research; it involves synthesizing the current body of evidence and then combining this knowledge with personal experience" (S014)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S002, S004, S009, S010, S012, S014, S020, S031)

## 2. Hierarchy of evidence, then logic, then n of 1
- **What it is:** When controlled evidence exists, follow it. When it does not, drop to logical rationale and anecdote, try it, and keep it until evidence refutes it.
- **Parts:** Meta-analyses and RCTs at the top; mechanistic and acute data lower; expert opinion and anecdote at the bottom. "There is always evidence" (co-written, S010). No study means experiment, not paralysis.
- **When to use:** New techniques with no trials (pre-exhaustion, short-rest blocks, loaded stretch) and gaps like elite lifters or older adults.
- **How to score:** Guidance: reward a technique with a sound logic and no evidence of harm as "worth experimenting with"; penalize one that violates basic principles (no progression, spot reduction) even without an RCT.
- **Quote:** "If we don't have any good evidence to refute it and it has a sound logical basis, it is worth trying until we have a basis to refute it." (S004)
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S004, S009, S010, S011, S020, S039)

## 3. Statistical versus practical meaningfulness
- **What it is:** Look past p values to effect sizes, confidence intervals, and the absolute size of the change, then ask whether that size matters for this person's goal.
- **Parts:** Is the effect real? How big is it? For whom? A small effect can decide a bodybuilding show and mean nothing to a stockbroker.
- **When to use:** Supplements, timing tricks, advanced techniques, and any "significant" headline.
- **How to score:** Guidance: small effects (for example creatine's ES of 0.11 on direct muscle imaging, S008) score high only for physique competitors; for general clients, score them low against cost and hassle.
- **Quote:** "This is where you have to take the research and say, what is important to me? How practically meaningful is it?" (S004)
- **Sources:** 8 (S001, S004, S005, S008, S010, S011, S012, S014)

## 4. Many roads lead to gains *(his phrase)*
- **What it is:** Most training choices are not either-or. Different loads, progressions, splits, and cardio types reach similar results; mix them over time.
- **Parts:** Rep progression or load progression; heavy, moderate, or light loads; HIIT or steady state; full or partial ROM; failure on some sets, not all.
- **When to use:** When a client or coach insists on one true method.
- **How to score:** Guidance: a plan that locks into a single method scores lower than one that varies sensibly, unless the goal is narrow (a powerlifting meet).
- **Quote:** "my favorite saying is many roads lead to gains." (S005)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S005, S008, S009, S011, S014, S041, S043, S044)

## 5. Volume dose response, with an inverted U
- **What it is:** More weekly sets produce more growth up to a point, then results flatten and fall with overtraining. Low volume still builds real muscle.
- **Parts:** Under 5, 5 to 9, and 10 plus weekly sets per muscle gave 5.4, 6.6, and 9.8 percent growth (S010, 2016); 1, 3, and 5 sets per exercise in trained men showed a graded response (S038, 2018). Individual response varies.
- **When to use:** Setting weekly sets, deciding whether to add volume, time-pressed clients.
- **How to score:** Guidance: for maximal growth, 10 or more weekly sets per muscle is the floor (S010); for maintenance or busy clients, 3 to 4 sets per muscle per week is enough (S002).
- **Quote:** "The effects of volume on hypertrophy undoubtedly follows an inverted-U curve" (S010)
- **Sources:** 7 (S002, S003, S005, S009, S010, S013, S038)

## 6. The loading paradigm: no ideal hypertrophy zone
- **What it is:** Strength is load-specific (heavy for max strength); hypertrophy is similar at any load from about 30% 1RM up when sets go hard; moderate loads are the most efficient.
- **Parts:** Heavy 3 to 5, moderate 8 to 12, light 15 to 30 plus. Most sets moderate; sprinkle in heavy and light. Heavy plus high volume beats up joints; light loads hurt.
- **When to use:** Rep range questions, older or injured clients, strength versus size goals.
- **How to score:** Guidance: for size, any load from 30% 1RM to about 5 reps is valid if effort is high; for 1RM strength, heavy work is required.
- **Quote:** "Thus, as a matter of principle, there is no ideal “hypertrophy zone.”" (S043)
- **Sources:** 9 (S001, S005, S010, S011, S012, S013, S034, S042, S043)

## 7. Proximity to failure (RIR 1 to 3, failure on the last set)
- **What it is:** Effort is the key variable for growth. Most hypertrophy sets should end one to three reps short of failure; take failure on the last set, mostly on single-joint and machine moves.
- **Parts:** Novices can stop well short; advanced lifters need closer to failure; older lifters and high-frequency routines use less failure; you must have trained to failure to judge RIR.
- **When to use:** Effort prescription, autoregulation, beginners versus advanced.
- **How to score:** Guidance: score low any plan where sets are easy (most gym-goers train too easy, S004) or where every compound set goes to failure.
- **Quote:** "A good rule-of-thumb is to limit its use to the last set of a given exercise; other sets should employ an RIR of 1 to 3." (S009)
- **Sources:** 7 (S001, S002, S004, S005, S009, S011, S007)

## 8. Read the methods, not the abstract *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Before accepting a finding, check the measurement, the subjects, the supervision, the duration, and whether acute data are being stretched into long-term claims.
- **Parts:** DXA, BIA, and BodPod measure fat-free mass, not muscle; prefer MRI, CT, ultrasound. Untrained results do not transfer to trained. Acute protein synthesis does not predict growth. Self-report and poor supervision weaken results.
- **When to use:** Any study, supplement claim, or social media headline.
- **How to score:** Guidance: discount findings built on indirect measures, untrained subjects, or acute markers when the decision is about long-term muscle in trained people.
- **Quote:** "If you just read the abstract, you might think the answer is clear." (S012)
- **Sources:** 8 (S004, S008, S010, S011, S012, S013, S014, S016)

## 9. Hierarchy of nutrition
- **What it is:** Rank diet priorities: energy balance first, then protein, then food quality, then carb and fat split, then timing.
- **Parts:** Deficit for fat loss; about 1.6 to 2.2 g/kg protein; whole foods most of the time (80/20); macro split by preference; meal timing last.
- **When to use:** Any client nutrition question, and to stop clients obsessing over timing or keto.
- **How to score:** Guidance: a plan that gets calories and protein right but ignores timing scores well; one that fixates on timing or "hormones" while calories are off scores 1.
- **Quote:** "I follow what’s called the hierarchy of nutrition" (S003)
- **Sources:** 6 (S003, S011, S012, S024, S032, S036)

## 10. Protein distribution rule
- **What it is:** About 0.4 g/kg per meal over at least four meals reaches at least 1.6 g/kg/day; up to 0.55 g/kg per meal for 2.2 g/kg/day. The 20 to 30 gram per meal "limit" is wrong for mixed meals.
- **When to use:** Meal planning for muscle gain or a cut.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 1.6 g/kg/day is the minimum for lifters, about 2 g/kg is his safety margin (S005); protein timing around the workout is secondary.
- **Quote:** "to maximize anabolism one should consume protein at a target intake of 0.4 g/kg/meal across a minimum of four meals in order to reach a minimum of 1.6 g/kg/day." (S036)
- **Sources:** 6 (S003, S005, S010, S015, S031, S036)

## 11. Responders and non-responders *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Studies report means; individuals vary hugely (in one cluster study, gains ranged from about 58 percent to none on the same program). So every plan is a template to be adjusted.
- **When to use:** Plateaued clients, comparing a client to study results, and "it worked for me" claims.
- **How to score:** Guidance: score higher a plan with a built-in way to check and adjust individual response.
- **Quote:** "Remember that research simply reports the means (i.e. averages)." (S013)
- **Sources:** 7 (S002, S003, S006, S008, S010, S013, S015)

## 12. Minimum effective and maintenance dose *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** Most people reach most of their potential on brief routines, and keeping gains takes far less than building them.
- **Parts:** Three days a week, 30 to 45 minutes, multi-joint first, total body, one or two sets per exercise, paired supersets and drop sets to save time. Maintenance can survive large volume cuts.
- **When to use:** Busy clients, travel, injury layoffs, maintenance phases.
- **How to score:** Guidance: for general clients, a 3-day, 45-minute plan scores as well as a 6-day plan; only bodybuilders need the extra.
- **Quote:** "three days a week of probably 45, half hour, 45 minute workouts can get you good majority of your genetic potential." (S001)
- **Sources:** 5 (S001, S002, S004, S010, S044)

## 13. Train across planes and angles; target, don't isolate
- **What it is:** Muscles have regions and heads with different functions and nerve branches, so full development needs a variety of exercises and some single-joint work.
- **Parts:** Overhead extensions for the triceps long head; leg curls plus hip hinges for hamstrings; lateral raises and rear delt work; leg extensions for the rectus femoris.
- **When to use:** Bodybuilding and physique clients, lagging muscles.
- **How to score:** Guidance: compounds alone are fine for general fitness; for maximal physique, a plan without single-joint work for hamstrings, calves, and delts scores lower.
- **Quote:** "there is really no such thing as "isolation" in resistance training." (S007)
- **Sources:** 7 (S002, S003, S007, S008, S012, S039, S013)

## 14. Progressive overload is more than adding load
- **What it is:** Overload can come from more reps, sets, frequency, density, or load. Rep progression grew muscle as well as load progression over 8 weeks.
- **Quote:** "In this regard, variety may be the spice of gainz." (S008)
- **Sources:** 3 (S005, S008, S007)

## 15. Rest by exercise type, then autoregulate
- **What it is:** At least about 90 seconds to 2 minutes for growth; longer for heavy compounds; shorter for single-joint moves at the end; advanced lifters start the next set when ready.
- **How to score:** Guidance: 60 seconds or less on multi-joint sets modestly blunts growth (S004); fine as a time trade-off for general clients.
- **Quote:** "A viable strategy is to take longer rest intervals on your large-muscle compound exercises such as squats, presses and rows." (S011)
- **Sources:** 5 (S004, S005, S010, S011, S012)

## 16. Mechanisms: mechanical tension first
- **What it is:** Mechanical tension is the main driver of growth; metabolic stress and muscle damage may add. Light loads work because tension rises as you near failure.
- **Over time:** 2010 he gave the three mechanisms more equal weight and leaned on hormones (S007, S030); by 2014 tension was the driver (S006) and by 2016 acute hormones had "little if any effect" (S010).
- **Quote:** "It’s well-established that mechanical tension is the primary mechanism responsible for muscle hypertrophy" (S009)
- **Sources:** 6 (S006, S007, S008, S009, S010, S030)

## 17. Volume budget for lagging muscles *(unnamed)*
- **What it is:** For advanced lifters, keep total sets about the same but move them from developed muscles to lagging ones.
- **Quote:** "you're apportioning it, kind of like a budget towards muscles that might need it more." (S005)
- **Sources:** 2 (S002, S005)

## 18. Three keys to a hypertrophy program
- **What it is:** Train hard enough, progressively overload, recover. Everything else is detail.
- **Quote:** "you need to train hard, sufficiently hard, you need to progressively overload, and you need to recuperate properly" (S005)
- **Sources:** 3 (S005, S013, S007)

His textbook *Science and Development of Muscle Hypertrophy* and his consumer books teach frameworks 5, 6, 13, and 16 plus periodization; *The M.A.X. Muscle Plan* applies them as a six-month periodized template with a metabolic short-rest phase (S003, S004). See `books.md`.
