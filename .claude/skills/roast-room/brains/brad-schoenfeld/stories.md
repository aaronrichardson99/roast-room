# Stories: Brad Schoenfeld

16 stories and cases he reuses, mostly from his own lab, with the lesson he draws. Built from his blog, interviews, and papers (2010 to 2024). Source ids in parentheses.

1. **The 30-rep study that made him a believer.** He expected light loads to fail in trained lifters; 25 to 35 rep sets grew muscle as well as 8 to 12. About half the light-load group puked in the first week. Lesson: light loads work, but they are brutal and can hurt adherence (S011, S034).
2. **Powerlifting versus bodybuilding (his dissertation).** At equal volume load both groups grew the same, but the powerlifting group took about 70 minutes versus 17, complained of sore joints, and two dropped out injured. Lesson: moderate reps are the practical way to accumulate volume (S006, S013, S010).
3. **The leg press rep spread.** At 75% 1RM his trained subjects did anywhere from 7 to 21 reps; at 30% 1RM, from about 20 to 72. Lesson: percent-1RM charts fail individuals; prescribe rep zones (S001, S005).
4. **The deload study that changed his mind.** A one-week layoff after four weeks gave no benefit and slightly less strength; nobody felt they needed a recovery week. Lesson: move from scheduled to autoregulated deloads (S008).
5. **Supervised versus unsupervised training.** Supervised lifters grew more and only 2 dropped out versus 7; most said they had never trained so hard. Lesson: a coach or partner adds effort, technique, and accountability (S008).
6. **Alan Aragon and the anabolic window.** Aragon reviewed his MAX Muscle Plan nutrition chapter and challenged nutrient timing; the review and meta-analysis that followed reversed Brad's view. Lesson: let a colleague's challenge send you back to the data (S014, S031, S032).
7. **The John Ivy debate.** He rebutted Ivy point by point on nutrient timing and offered to publish a reply unedited; Ivy never responded. Lesson: authority is not evidence; debate in the open (S012, S014).
8. **The nursing home leg extensions.** A 1990 study had frail elderly do only leg extensions; strength rose about 150 percent and two of ten walked without canes. Lesson: "functional" depends on the person, not the exercise (S003).
9. **From pro routines to science.** He copied pro bodybuilders, plateaued, then trained scientifically and won natural titles without their genetics or drugs. Lesson: evidence matters most for those without advantages (S004, S007).
10. **The mind-muscle connection study.** Thinking about the biceps nearly doubled biceps growth versus "get the weight up"; quads did not differ. Lesson: sometimes the bros are right, and testing shows where (S009).
11. **The retracted HIIT meta-analysis.** A meta-analysis calling HIIT a fat-loss "magic bullet" was retracted; his course rebuilt it from 56 studies and found no difference. Lesson: headline findings need scrutiny (S009).
12. **"You're done!"** A trainer told a bodybuilder he had maxed out his genetics after a couple of years. Brad answered that nobody can know their ceiling and that a new stimulus can still bring gains. Lesson: do not accept self-limiting claims (S011).
13. **Covid wiped out a study.** Over 500 hours of data on load versus rep progression were lost in 2020; a master's student restarted it and it found both work. Lesson: persistence in research, and many roads lead to gains (S008).
14. **The CrossFitter with no rear delts.** The weakest subject on rear delt flys was a CrossFitter who never trained that plane. Lesson: train in all planes (S013).
15. **The bodybuilding survey and the bro split.** Every one of 127 competitive bodybuilders used a split; his trial found total body grew more at equal volume. Lesson: tradition is not evidence, but volume and novelty are confounders (S011).
16. **The PhD in his 40s.** He earned his doctorate while working full-time; his father had instilled the scientific method in him. Lesson: he treats training as a science and mentoring students as his legacy (S014, S005).
