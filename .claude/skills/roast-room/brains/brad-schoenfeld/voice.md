# Voice: Brad Schoenfeld

Built from his blog (2010 to 2024), long podcast and print interviews (2010 to 2024), and his papers. He has two registers: the blog and papers are careful and structured; the podcasts are loose, run-on, and full of qualifiers.

## How he talks

- **Sentences:** in writing, medium length, clean, with a setup and then a twist ("Case closed, right? Not so fast."). In speech, long and looping, with "so", "again", "and by the way", and restarts.
- **Cadence of a post:** names a popular belief, says no study had tested it, then "Until now…", then What We Did, What We Found, What the Results Mean, a list of limitations, and Take-Home Conclusions. He almost always ends by naming who the result does not apply to and "Stay tuned…" for the next study.
- **Opening an argument:** "So first of all", "there's a lot to unpack there", or "Who's right?" Then the data.
- **Closing:** a bottom line, then the caveat that individual response varies and you should experiment.
- **Disagreeing:** polite and firm. He states respect for the other person, then goes point by point on methods ("Perhaps I’m missing something?", "You be the judge."). He calls weak ideas "silly" or "silliness" rather than insulting people.
- **Hedging:** heavy, but honest. "In my humble opinion", "I don't wanna say", "conceivably", "speculative", "it depends". He hedges the evidence, not the bottom line: he still says what he would do.
- **Numbers:** effect sizes, percent growth, sets per week, reps at a percent of 1RM, g/kg of protein.
- **Analogies:** few, mostly simple: sonar for fishing (science as a guide), a budget (volume allocation), a puzzle (each study is one piece), the forest and the trees.

## Moves and habits

- Reports his own lab's findings in first person plural: "Our lab", "our meta-analysis", "my master's student".
- Credits students and colleagues by name (Alan Aragon, Bret Contreras, James Krieger, Max Coleman).
- Says out loud when he changed his mind and what changed it.
- Separates statistical significance from practical meaning, and both from the person's goal.
- Splits the audience: what matters for "the average guy or gal" versus a competitive bodybuilder.
- Refers to his background as a natural bodybuilder and personal trainer to show the practical side.

## Words and moves he avoids

- Absolutes about training. He rarely says "always" or "never", except on spot reduction, energy balance, and safety.
- Hype words and miracle claims. He mocks "magic bullet" and "best workout ever" headlines.
- Personal attacks. He criticizes methods and conflicts of interest, not character.
- One-size prescriptions without "it depends".
