# Positions: Brad Schoenfeld

32 fitness-coaching topics. Built from 45 sources (blog 2010 to 2024, interviews 2010 to 2024, papers 2010 to 2023). Each stance has a verbatim quote, source id, and date. Where he changed his mind, the versions sit side by side with dates; cite the newer view. Topics with no evidence say so.

## Training volume
- **Stance:** Volume is a primary driver of growth with a dose response, but low volume still builds real muscle and too much tips into overtraining.
- **Over time:**
  - 2010: "Even at the highest levels of fitness, large muscle groups generally require no more than nine to twelve total sets while smaller muscle groups need only six to nine; any more is basically superfluous." (S027)
  - 2015: "First and foremost is volume. So there is a clear dose response relationship" (S003)
  - 2016: "a higher volume approach is clearly necessary if you want to maximize muscular gains." (S010) 10 plus weekly sets per muscle as a minimum for max growth.
  - 2018: "substantially greater training volumes may be beneficial in enhancing muscle growth in those with previous RT experience, at least over an 8-wk training period." (S038)
  - 2018: "volume plays a fairly prominent role in maximizing growth, but nevertheless significant hypertrophy can be obtained at fairly low volumes." (S009)
- **Read:** moved from a volume cap toward higher volumes for trained lifters, always with the inverted U and individual response.

## Minimum dose and maintenance
- **Stance:** About 3 to 4 weekly sets per muscle are enough for most; maintenance takes far less than building. "probably three to four sets per week per muscle group there’s so, you know, some good evidence that’s sufficient." (S002, 2021) "maintaining the gains can be achieved with substantially reduced volumes and time commitments." (S002, 2021)

## Frequency
- **Stance:** Train each muscle at least twice a week for max growth, though once a week works; total weekly volume matters more than how you split it.
- **Over time:**
  - 2010: "I don't necessarily agree that natural lifters need to work specific muscles more than once a week, however, at least on an ongoing basis." (S007)
  - 2015: "it’s best to directly work each muscle at least twice a week" (S011)
  - 2016: "the claims made by some that the typical bro-split only works for juiced-up bodybuilders are patently false." (S010)
  - 2023: three to four days a week covers most people; 48 hours between hits as a rule of thumb (S005).

## Intensity of effort and failure
- **Stance:** Train hard, mostly 1 to 3 reps short of failure; failure on the last set, mainly on single-joint and machine moves; more failure for advanced lifters, less with age.
- **Over time:**
  - 2010: "The evidence seems to suggest that training to failure is a necessary part of optimizing hypertrophy." (S007)
  - 2021: "A good rule-of-thumb is to limit its use to the last set of a given exercise; other sets should employ an RIR of 1 to 3." (S009)
  - 2023: "I come from the old school of, you know, you gotta train hard, go hard or go home. I used to actually, so that opinion has really softened over the years" (S001)
  - 2023: "probably the most important thing from a hypertrophy standpoint, is proximity to failure." (S005)

## Rep ranges and load
- **Stance:** Similar growth across loads from about 30% 1RM up; strength needs heavy loads; most sets moderate for efficiency, with some heavy and light work.
- **Over time:**
  - 2010: "a moderate rep range (65% to 85% of 1RM) seems to be ideal for maximizing the hypertrophic response." (S007)
  - 2015: "No way could you build appreciable muscle using 30 reps per set." (S011) followed by "Or so I thought. I’m now a believer." (S011)
  - 2021: "Thus, as a matter of principle, there is no ideal “hypertrophy zone.”" (S043)
  - 2023: "this is something i've kind of done a 180 on over the years" (S005)

## Fiber-type targeting with light and heavy loads
- **Over time:**
  - 2015: "If your goal is to build as much muscle as possible, it seems appropriate to train across the spectrum of loading zones" (S011)
  - 2018: "there currently is not enough evidence to make a firm conclusion regarding changes that occur at the muscle fiber level with different loading schemes." (S037)
  - 2023: "although I'm becoming less convinced that that might be the case." (S005)
- **Read:** still mixes loads, but no longer leans on fiber-type theory.

## Exercise selection
- **Stance:** Multi-joint lifts first; single-joint work is needed for full development of hamstrings, calves, and delts and for regional growth; no truly bad exercises, only bad application.
- "if the goal is to optimize your muscular potential, it appears necessary to include single-joint exercises as part of a comprehensive training program." (S008, 2022)
- No bad exercises, only "poor application of a given movement based on an individual’s goals, abilities, and medical history." (S020, 2011)
- Deadlift in hypertrophy blocks: leaves it out. "it has a poor stimulus to fatigue ratio" (S001, 2023)
- Squats: "So is there anything magical about a squat? No. Do you have to do squats?" (S001, 2023)

## Machines versus free weights
- **Stance:** Complementary; machines target and are safe for failure; heavy machine work from a dead start can be awkward (S001). In 2010 he said both machines and free weights have a place because they complement each other (paraphrase, S007); in 2023 he still calls machines fine (S001).

## Periodization
- **Stance:** Vary volume, loads, and frequency over time; research on periodization is mixed.
- **Over time:**
  - 2010: "I'm a big proponent of periodization for achieving any exercise-related goal." (S007)
  - 2013: "Despite what you may have heard, research on periodization is highly conflicting." (S015)
  - Beginners need repetition, not variety (S001, 2023).

## Deloads
- **Over time:**
  - 2015: periodize volume in waves with deloads between (S003).
  - 2016: "a week of reduced frequency, volume, and/or intensity is strategically integrated into your program every month or so" (S010)
  - 2024 (newer, cite this): "my opinion has now shifted to recommend autoregulated deloads, where lifters implement a deload when they feel they need one." (S008) "it appears that most would not need a deload for at least 8 weeks if not longer" (S008)
- **Read:** caveats that older, elite, and powerlifting clients may need more recovery (S008).

## Rest between sets
- **Over time:**
  - 2010: "A moderate rest interval of about one to two minutes between sets appears to be ideal for maximizing hypertrophy." (S007)
  - 2014: "It would appear that you can self-select a rest period that allows you to exert the needed effort into your next set without compromising hypertrophic results." (S012)
  - 2016: "it would seem that 2 minutes provides sufficient recovery so as not to undermine growth." (S011)
  - 2024: "my general rule probably would be two minutes would be a safe bet, at least on multi-join." (S004) Advanced lifters autoregulate.

## Warm-ups and stretching
- **Stance:** Specific warm-ups matter for heavy, low-rep, and complex lifts; little value for sets of 8 plus. "You can ditch the warm up" (S002, 2021) Static stretching only if flexibility is a goal (S002, S009).

## Tempo and range of motion
- **Stance:** 2 to 6 second reps all work; avoid superslow; full ROM for the lower body. "My general feeling is that the concentric portion of a rep should be around 1-2 seconds" (S011, 2016) "performing RT through a full ROM confers beneficial effects on hypertrophy of the lower body musculature versus training with a partial ROM." (S041, 2020)

## Advanced techniques
- **Stance:** Drop sets can count as volume; paired supersets save time; pre-exhaustion and short-rest blocks are logical but unproven; loaded inter-set stretch worth trying.
- "The literature seems to suggest that using drop sets appropriately can substitute for volume of additional sets." (S002, 2021)
- "the superSec group finished the workout in 40 percent less time." (S004, 2024)
- "it’s a strategy worth experimenting with if your goal is to maximize gains." (S008, 2022, on loaded stretch)

## Mind-muscle connection
- **Stance:** Helps, at least for arms in moderate rep ranges. "it appears the bros were right; employing a mind-muscle connection enhances muscle growth." (S009, 2018)

## Cardio and fat loss
- **Stance:** Diet is the main lever; HIIT and steady state are equal for fat loss; fasted or fed by preference; lift before cardio in the same session.
- "We can confidently state that HIIT cannot be considered a “magic bullet” for fat loss" (S009, 2021)
- "whether you perform cardio fasted or fed should depend entirely on preference." (S011, 2014)
- "This finding reinforces the fact that diet is the most effective way to lose fat." (S009, 2021)

## Fat loss rate and cutting
- **Stance:** About 1 to 2 pounds a week is realistic (2010); do not cut training volume when dieting; lean mass losses climb when very lean.
- "A realistic goal is to lose about 1-2 pounds per week." (S028, 2010)
- **Over time (volume on a cut):** "Based on this rationale, I’ve always recommended cutting back on the number of sets performed during periods of a caloric deficit." (S008, 2022, describing his old view) then the 2022 RCT: volume neither helped nor hurt over 6 weeks, and "you need to be at least at caloric maintenance (or perhaps in a surplus) to take advantage of higher training volumes from a muscle-building standpoint." (S008)

## Protein
- **Stance:** At least 1.6 g/kg/day; he recommends about 2 g/kg as a safety margin; 0.4 g/kg per meal over at least four meals.
- 2013: "My reasoning is to provide a margin of safety. An insurance policy, if you will." (S015)
- 2018: "to maximize anabolism one should consume protein at a target intake of 0.4 g/kg/meal across a minimum of four meals in order to reach a minimum of 1.6 g/kg/day." (S036)
- 2023: "a minimum of around 1.6 grams per kilogram a day. And probably I generally recommend a little higher as a safety margin, around 2 grams per kilogram" (S005)
- Safety: "Consumption of up to approximately 4 g/kg/day appears to pose no increased health risk when kidney function is normal." (S015, 2013)

## Protein timing and the anabolic window
- **Over time:**
  - Before 2013: "For many years I was a staunch proponent of the concept of an “anabolic window of opportunity,”" (S014)
  - 2013: "the quantity of protein explained virtually all the variance in results!" (S014)
  - 2017: "the trainee is free to choose, based on individual factors (i.e., preference, tolerance, convenience, and availability), whether to consume protein immediately pre- or post-exercise." (S035)
  - 2023: "The timing aspect is another area where I've done kind of a 180 on." (S005) He still tells bodybuilders to take a shake soon after training, since there is no downside (S005).

## Meal frequency
- **Stance:** Personal preference once calories and protein are set; at least three protein meals for muscle. "the decision on how many meals to eat from this standpoint should come down to personal preference" (S011, 2015)

## Diet type (keto, low carb, IIFYM)
- **Stance:** No universal best diet; energy balance and protein decide; choose what you will stick to. "The primary take-home message here is that there is no universal “best” diet." (S012, 2014) On keto versus low fat: "the ketogenic diet versus the low fat diet. Some people do better on one" (S003, 2015) Eat well 80 percent of the time (credits Alan Aragon, S003).

## Supplements
- **Creatine:** works, small muscle effect on direct imaging, worth it mainly for young lifters chasing max gains. "its anabolic effects were more modest than previous meta-analyses that analyzed fat-free mass as a proxy for hypertrophy." (S008, 2023)
- **BCAAs and HMB-ATP:** he co-wrote letters criticizing studies claiming big effects (S010). No recorded position beyond that.
- **Energy shots:** "Bottom line: Be an educated consumer." (S023, 2011)

## Recovery modalities, cold water, NSAIDs
- Cold water: "Our results indicate that you should avoid regular CWI use if your goal is to optimize hypertrophy." (S008, 2024) Occasional use is fine.
- NSAIDs: "For the young hard-training lifter, I’d suggest avoiding consistent use of NSAIDs as it well may hinder gains." (S006, 2014)
- Massage and foam rolling: help mostly by making you feel better (S005, 2023).

## Beginners versus advanced
- **Stance:** Beginners: form, repetition, two minutes rest, effort well short of failure is enough. Advanced: more volume, closer to failure, variety, individualized volume budgets.
- "the training should become more individualized and specialized as you become more advanced" (S005, 2023)
- "If someone tells you that you’re done adding muscle, pay them no heed." (S011, 2015)

## Older adults and children
- **Stance:** Older adults can use lighter loads for muscle and should train some fast concentrics for power; children can lift once mature and supervised. "Zero evidence that it will stunt children's growth." (S005, 2023)

## Women
- **Stance:** Under-studied; women should lift heavy enough to matter. "Bottom line: Don’t worry about bulking up like a female bodybuilder. It simply isn’t possible." (S028, 2010)

## Supervision and client communication
- **Stance:** A trainer or attentive partner improves results and attendance; communicate risk and benefit plainly and respect the client's values.
- "direct supervision during resistance training appears to enhance muscular adaptations for a majority of the recreationally trained lifting public." (S008, 2023)
- "it's not up to me or you or anyone else to tell someone why they should be training." (S005, 2023)

## Evidence versus anecdote
- **Stance:** Anecdote is weak evidence but a good source of hypotheses; test it. "People that dismiss quote unquote, bro science are silly." (S004, 2024) His own early plateau on pro routines made him turn to science (S004).

## Social media, gurus, and fitness myths
- **Stance:** Distrusts certainty and credentials as proof. "people think that people who are. Very definitive and confident in their opinion. No more when it’s, that’s generally the opposite." (S001, 2023) "Just because someone trains a celebrity doesn’t mean they are knowledgeable about exercise science." (S020, 2011) Spot reduction: "you can’t spot reduce fat. It’s a physiologic impossibility." (S024, 2011)

## Functional training and CrossFit
- **Stance:** All exercise can be functional; bodybuilding is functional; CrossFit's concept is fine, its execution often poor.
- "Now, I can’t think of any exercise that would have less functional relevance than a leg extension." (S003, 2015, before the nursing home study punchline)
- "The real “issue” is not with the concept of CrossFit per se, but rather in the way that many facilities carry out its training practices." (S013, 2014)

## Running a coaching business
- **No recorded position** on pricing, marketing, or client acquisition. He ran a personal training facility and trained physique athletes (S007), and praises supervision for results and retention (S008). Treat any business advice from this seat as outside the brain.

## Hormones
- **Over time:** 2010 he thought acute testosterone elevations might play a role (S007); 2016: "Emerging evidence indicates that acute increases in anabolic hormones have little if any effect on muscular adaptations" (S010).
