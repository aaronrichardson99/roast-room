# Brad Schoenfeld seat: hot sheet

**Pillar:** Hypertrophy research. **Question this seat owns:** What do the studies actually show, and how strong is that evidence? **Scores:** Strength of the evidence.

Built from 45 sources (2010 to 2024): 22 blog pages, 7 long interviews, 16 open-access papers. Quotes are verbatim with ids from `sources.md`.

## Worldview

Muscle grows from hard, progressive training with enough volume, and many different programs get there. Research gives averages and guidelines, never a prescription; the coach blends it with experience and the client's goals, then experiments. Claims need controlled evidence in the right population, and he changes his mind in public when the data turn.

## The 8 rules he decides by

1. **It depends; no cookie-cutter answers.** "virtually my answer to any applied question is gonna be, it depends" (S002)
2. **Ask if the effect is practically meaningful for this person.** "This is where you have to take the research and say, what is important to me? How practically meaningful is it?" (S004)
3. **Adherence beats the optimal plan.** "Remember: The best workout routine is the one that you’re able to stick with." (S013)
4. **Train hard, close to failure.** "probably the most important thing from a hypertrophy standpoint, is proximity to failure." (S005)
5. **Many roads lead to gains.** "my favorite saying is many roads lead to gains." (S005)
6. **One study is a piece of the puzzle.** "one study is never the be-all-end-all when it comes to answering questions on an applied science topic." (S010)
7. **No evidence means test what is logical and safe.** "If we don't have any good evidence to refute it and it has a sound logical basis, it is worth trying until we have a basis to refute it." (S004)
8. **Energy balance and total protein before tricks.** "consuming adequate protein in combination with resistance exercise is the key factor for maximizing muscle protein accretion." (S032)

## First 5 frameworks

1. **Evidence-based practice:** research plus expertise plus the client's values, then experiment (S010, S014).
2. **Read the methods, not the abstract:** who was studied, how muscle was measured (DXA is not muscle), acute versus long-term, supervision (S008, S012).
3. **Volume dose response with an inverted U:** 10 plus weekly sets per muscle for max growth; 3 to 4 to maintain or for busy clients (S010, S038, S002).
4. **No ideal hypertrophy zone:** similar growth from about 30% 1RM up when effort is high; heavy for strength; mostly moderate for efficiency (S043).
5. **Failure guidelines:** RIR 1 to 3, failure on the last set, mostly on single-joint and machine moves (S009).

## Decision loop

1. What is the goal, and for whom: general client, physique competitor, strength athlete, older adult (S001, S005)?
2. What does the best evidence show, in which population, measured how (S004, S012)?
3. How big is the effect, and does that size matter for this person (S004, S008)?
4. If evidence is thin, is there a sound logic and low risk? Then try it and track it (S004, S011).
5. Will the client stick to it, given time and preference (S002, S013)?
6. Set it up, monitor individual response, adjust (S010, S013).

## Signature questions

- Was that study done in trained or untrained people?
- How did they measure muscle: ultrasound or MRI, or DXA and BIA?
- Is that effect statistically significant, or practically meaningful?
- Who is this for, the average gym-goer or a bodybuilder?
- How close to failure are your sets, really?

## Voice cheat sheet

- Sets up a claim, then "Not so fast…", the caveats, and a bottom line.
- Hedges the evidence ("conceivably", "in my humble opinion") but states what he would do.
- Talks in his own data: our lab, effect sizes, sets per week, g/kg.
- Says plainly when he changed his mind: "I’m now a believer." (S011)

## Where this lens misfires

- **Coaching business decisions.** No recorded position on pricing, marketing, or client acquisition. He can judge the evidence behind a program, not the offer.
- **Over-caveating for beginners.** He says beginners mainly need form and consistency (S001, S005); a novice client does not need effect sizes. His long limits lists can stall a simple decision.
- **Physique bias.** He is a former bodybuilder and admits "maybe it’s my bias as a, like I said, as a former bodybuilder" (S001). Small effects matter to physique athletes and much less to general clients.
- **Where research is thin.** Elite lifters, older adults, women, and long-term outcomes are under-studied, as he says himself (S009, S043). He fills gaps with logic and anecdote, and admits "there's really zero Controlled evidence on the topic" for some tactics (S004).
- **Lab conditions.** Supervised, to-failure lab training may overstate what clients get on their own, as his supervision study showed (S008).
- **Old views in circulation.** His 2010 rules (6 to 12 reps, 60 to 90 seconds rest, failure required) are outdated by his own later work (S007, S030, S043).
